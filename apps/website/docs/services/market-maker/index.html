Run a market maker

A core node that converts between XRP and dollars slowly runs out of one of them. A market maker brings it back: it trades through the node when the node's rates beat a pool on the XRP Ledger, and swaps the difference back on the pool. This page sets one up, with two nodes of your own.

Early days. Dassie takes test money only: a node refuses the live realm, and the market maker signs only on the XRP Ledger testnet. How far along it is

How it works

Each core node prices what it converts by what it holds: it offers more for the asset it has least of, and publishes its rates in a signed rate feed. The market maker reads those feeds from the core nodes it trades through, its targets, and reads the price of an XRP Ledger pool between XRP and a dollar token. When a target's rate and the pool's price differ by more than a margin, it:

  1. asks for a quote of a payment from one of its nodes to the other, through that target, which converts it;
  2. pays only if the quote, swapped back on the pool, still clears the margin;
  3. swaps funds back from one node's XRP Ledger account to the other's on the pool, when a node holds more than its band allows.

So the target gets back the asset it ran short of, and the market maker keeps the margin. It is a client of nodes, not a node: every amount it moves is moved by one of its nodes or by the XRP Ledger.

Why market makers, not the nodes, rebalance

Two nodes, pinned

You run two nodes, one for each asset of the pool:

  • The XRP node is a member of the testnet's XRP hub, funded from its XRP ledger.
  • The token node is a member of the DSUSD hub, funded from its DSUSD ledger, with an XRP ledger beside it that pays the hub's fees on the XRP Ledger.

A payment from one to the other through a target makes that target convert, which one node alone cannot do. Both are periphery nodes, with no hostname and no relay, and both pin every target as an uplink, so that each payment goes through the target it was meant for. Two nodes serve any number of targets.

Each target must be a core node that publishes a rate feed and is a member of both hubs. Your nodes learn each target's node ID from its discovery document: 

$curl -fsS https://node.example.com/.well-known/dassie/node | jq -r .id

Running the nodes

Two nodes on one machine each need their own data directory, IPC socket and ports. This runs the XRP node as your user; run the token node the same way, with its own directory, socket and ports, such as 4102 and 5102: 

$DASSIE_STATE_DIRECTORY=$HOME/mm/xrp \
  DASSIE_IPC_SOCKET_PATH=$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/mm-xrp.sock \
  DASSIE_RELAY= \
  DASSIE_UPLINKS=<target node ID>,<another target's node ID> \
  dassie daemon --runtime standalone --http-port 4101 --https-port 5101 \
    --feed-listen 127.0.0.1:0
  • DASSIE_RELAY= keeps no relay: nobody pays these nodes at a name.
  • DASSIE_UPLINKS pins the targets, by node ID, comma separated.
  • --feed-listen 127.0.0.1:0 keeps the two nodes off the rate feed's port, which a node at the edge does not use anyway.
  • Leave out --listen: a node's QUIC socket takes the address of its HTTPS listener, and must reach the targets. Nothing needs to reach the nodes' ports from outside, so keep them closed in your firewall.

For a server, the units Dassie's own market maker runs with are a good model: apps/market-maker/vm.

Setting them up

Each node has its own recovery phrase, which you keep in a file. Set each node up from it, joining a hub of its asset, and keeping what it is paid on your own account, which the swaps spend from.

No default hubs are built into Dassie yet, so join hubs that you or someone else founded. Each node adds the owner books its hub funds from, then the hub's scheme with the hub's rules, as hex, in its config. The founder's dassie hub foundings shows the rules. 

$DASSIE_IPC_SOCKET_PATH=$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/mm-xrp.sock \
  dassie setup --passphrase-file ~/mm/xrp.phrase \
    --scheme xrpl-testnet-xrp \
    --scheme 'hub-xrpl-testnet-xrp={"rules":"<hex of the XRP hub>"}' \
    --hub hub-xrpl-testnet-xrp --keep-funds account
$DASSIE_IPC_SOCKET_PATH=$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/mm-dsusd.sock \
  dassie setup --passphrase-file ~/mm/dsusd.phrase \
    --scheme xrpl-testnet-xrp --scheme xrpl-testnet-dsusd \
    --scheme 'hub-xrpl-testnet-dsusd={"rules":"<hex of the DSUSD hub>"}' \
    --hub hub-xrpl-testnet-dsusd --keep-funds account

A DSUSD hub's escrow pays the XRP Ledger's fees in XRP, so the token node adds the XRP book too, before its token's. Running setup again changes nothing, so you can run it at every start. Then fund the XRP node's account on the XRP Ledger testnet, such as from the testnet's faucet. When the XRP node holds more than its band, the market maker's first swap buys DSUSD for the token node from the surplus, so you need not buy DSUSD yourself.

The testnet's default hubs, which --hub joins on its own with no --scheme, are not built in yet: the testnet is founding them again under the current rules. What's next

Config and secrets

The market maker reads one JSON file, and checks all of it before it connects to anything:

targets

The core nodes to trade through, each once, by URL. A target whose discovery document names no feed port is written {"url": "…", "feedPort": 7768}.

xrpl

The XRP Ledger server, wss://, on the testnet, and the token the pool pairs with XRP: its currency code and issuer. The market maker refuses to sign for a server that is not on the testnet.

xrp, token

Each of your two nodes:

  • socket: its IPC socket.
  • ledger: the ledger of its hub, which its trades are paid on and pay from.
  • ownerBook: its ledger of the same asset on its XRP Ledger account, which funds the hub and which the swaps spend.
  • passphraseFile: the file with its phrase.
  • tradeSize: how much one trade moves.
  • band: its band, below.

Amounts are in the nodes' ledger units, a billion to one XRP or one dollar: 10000000000 is 10.

marginPpm

How much better than the pool a trade must be, in parts per million: 5000 is half a percent.

slippagePpm

How far the pool may move against a swap before it fails rather than pays a worse price, in parts per million.

caFile and allowPrivate are for dev networks and tests only. The full rules are in config.rs.

The phrases

The market maker signs its swaps on the XRP Ledger with your nodes' account keys, so it needs their phrases, not only their sockets. It checks that each phrase is its node's, keeps only the signing key in memory, and never logs a phrase.

Careful. A phrase file is that node's money. Make it readable by the market maker's user alone (chmod 600), and keep it off shared disks. Dassie's own market maker reads its phrases into memory-backed files at start, and removes them when it stops.

Run it

$dassie market-maker --config ~/mm/config.json

It decides every ten seconds and logs one line per decision: the trade through a target, the swap, or why it holds. It stops at start if something it needs is missing, such as a node with no account in its hub yet; once it runs, it rides out outages and keeps going. A target that does not answer is asked again a minute later, while the others trade.

On each target, dassie status counts the feed's subscribers, so the target's operator can see yours.

Bands

Each node has a band: the least and the most of its asset it should hold, counting its hub and its own account together.

  • Below the band: no trade takes a node below the bottom of its band. A trade that would is made smaller.
  • Above the band: before any trade, the market maker swaps the node back to the middle of its band on the pool, from its own account to the other node's, never delivering more than the other node's band has room for. So the two never swap the same funds back and forth.
  • Both above: it holds, and trades nothing.

Set each band's middle above what the node keeps in its hub, so that what a swap spends is on its account. Bands are shared by every target, so adding a target needs no more funds.
