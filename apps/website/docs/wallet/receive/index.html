Receive money

Other wallets pay you at your node's addresses, through Open Payments. Share an address, or ask for a set amount with a payment request.

Early days. Dassie takes test money only: a node refuses the live realm. How far along it is

Your addresses

Choose Receive on the Dashboard. The Open Payments tab lists your node's wallet addresses: one for each asset you have a ledger for. Each shows two forms of the same address, a short payment pointer and a URL, each with a copy button, and the ledger what arrives there is booked on.

Payment pointer URL Pays in
$pay.example.com https://pay.example.com/.well-known/pay XRP, the default
$pay.example.com/pay/dsusd https://pay.example.com/pay/dsusd DSUSD

The short one, your node's name alone, pays in the asset marked default: the first of your ledgers, until you choose another with its Make … pay in button. The Dashboard shows it as your payment pointer. The default asset is also reachable at its own /pay/<asset> form, such as $pay.example.com/pay/xrp. An address names an asset, never a ledger or a hub, so where you keep the money can change and the address stays the same.

Payments from other nodes reach yours through its peers, and your node has peers only in a settlement hub.

Your name without a domain

A node without a domain name of its own, such as the desktop app, is named at Dassie's front door: $<node ID>.dassiepay.net, where the node ID is your node's 39 characters. The front door passes payments on to your node over the connection your node keeps to it. A name you register reaches your node the same way, at $<name>.dassiepay.net.

Above your addresses, and under your payment pointer on the Dashboard, the wallet says whether your node is connected to the front door now. When it says "This address may reach no one", payments to that address fail until it is fixed:

  • It is not connected now. Check that your node can reach relay.dassiepay.net on port 443, over UDP or, where UDP is blocked, over TCP. Right after a start it says "It is connecting now" for a moment, which is fine.
  • Your node keeps another relay, or none, because DASSIE_RELAY names another or is empty. Then the front door cannot reach your node: don't hand this address out.

Either way, a domain name of your own avoids the front door: Core and periphery explains the difference.

Ask for a payment

A payment request asks for a set amount, or any amount, with a note on what it is for. Whoever pays it sees that note.

  1. On the Open Payments tab, under Request a Payment, pick the asset if you have more than one.
  2. Type the amount, or leave it empty to take any amount. Add what it is for, if you like.
  3. Choose Create Request, and share the link it shows with whoever pays you. Any Open Payments wallet can pay it, Dassie's Send among them.

Under STREAM credentials, the request also has a destination address and a shared secret, for a sender that speaks STREAM directly rather than Open Payments. Share those only with the one sender. The request shows in your activity, with what it received.

Deposits on the XRP Ledger

Beside Open Payments, Receive has a tab for each asset, with where to send it on the XRP Ledger testnet:

  • Your own account, with its address and the faucet. Add and fund a ledger goes through it.
  • Your account in a settlement hub, when you keep what you receive in that asset in the hub, the default, and your account there has its number. The tab then shows the hub's escrow address and a destination tag.

Careful. A deposit to a hub's escrow must carry your destination tag, always. The escrow refuses a payment without one, and a payment with another tag is credited to someone else's account.

Where the money stays

What other nodes pay you arrives in your settlement hub of its asset, and stays there by default, ready to spend. For each asset a hub holds, Received Funds in Settings lets you keep it on your own account instead: then your node keeps a float in the hub and moves the rest to your account. Your hubs explains the float.

Why it works this way: one address per asset, and deposits that follow where you keep your money.
