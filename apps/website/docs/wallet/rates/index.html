Exchange-rate settings

When a payment passes through your node from one currency to another, your node converts it. You decide what it keeps for that, and where it learns the rates. Both live in Settings.

Fee, spread and rebalancing

Open Settings, and find the Peers card. Its three numbers apply only to what passes through your node from one peer to another. Your own payments, and payments to you, are never charged.

Setting What it does New node
Fee A share of everything that passes through, converted or not. It is rounded down, so a packet too small to carry a fee passes free. 0%
Spread A share of what your node converts from one currency to another, kept as the difference between the market rate and the rate it gives. 0%
Rebalancing How strongly your holdings move the rate. Bringing in what your node is short of gets up to this much better than the market rate. Taking it out gets worse, with no cap: at the default 5%, holdings split 25/75 already cost about 6% for taking out the scarce one, and more the further out of balance they are. 0 converts at the market rate. 5%
  1. Type each as a percentage, such as 0.3 for 0.3%. Each is at most 10%, with up to four decimals.
  2. Select Save. The new numbers apply to the next packets your node handles.

Rebalancing is how your node keeps its holdings in balance without trading. When it holds less XRP than it would like, it gives a slightly better rate to payments that bring XRP in, and a worse one to payments that take XRP out. Over time, what flows through brings your holdings back toward an even share of each asset.

Dassie is early, and few nodes route payments yet, so there is no going rate for a fee or a spread. A new node charges neither. If you set them, start low and watch what passes through.

Why it works this way: pricing by inventory instead of trading.

Where rates come from

Your node needs a rate for each currency it converts between, and for the currency you see your total in. It asks its sources about once a minute, and takes each currency's rate as the median of the sources that answered, once more than half of them did. One wrong source cannot move a rate past what the others say.

A new node starts with four sources. All are free and need no key:

  • Coinbase, which covers every currency;
  • Kraken, the last trade on its markets;
  • Bitstamp, which covers XRP;
  • European Central Bank, its daily reference rates for the government currencies.

So three sources cover every currency. DSUSD needs no rate: your node books it as a dollar, as long as the peg guard lets it.

Change the sources

  1. In Settings, find the Exchange Rates card. It lists your sources.
  2. To add one, choose it under Add a source and select Add. A Coinbase source can take a URL: leave it empty for Coinbase's own, or enter an https:// server that answers in Coinbase's format.
  3. To drop one, select Remove next to it.
  4. Select Save. Nothing changes until you do.

Your node keeps at most eight sources.

Careful. Without a source, rates go stale, and payments across currencies stop. A currency only two of your sources cover gets a rate only while both answer and agree. One that only a single source covers takes whatever that source says.

Check the rates

Open Ledgers. The Exchange Rates card at the bottom lists what one dollar buys in each of your currencies, with its age, such as: 

1 USD = 1.8656 XRP (2 minutes ago)

A rate older than an hour is stale, and payments that need it are refused until a source answers again. Your node keeps its last rates across restarts, so it converts again right after one.

A core node also publishes the rates it converts at for peers in its rate feed. See Network and peers.

The exchanges license their data for personal or internal use only. Rate sources whose terms allow a node's use are not there yet. What's next

The currency of your total

In the same card, Show totals in picks the currency the Dashboard adds your ledgers up in: USD, XRP, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, CAD or AUD. Choose No total to hide it. It applies at once. See Activity and assets for how the total reads.
