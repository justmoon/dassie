Activity and assets

Three places in the wallet tell you where your money is: the Dashboard, Activity and Assets. This page shows you how to read each, and what is still on its way.

Your balances

The Dashboard is the first page after you log in. Its balance card lists what you have of each asset, such as XRP or DSUSD, in its own currency: everything, in your settlement hub and on your own account together, since your node moves money between the two by itself, the reserves and what is held for your peers included. Each asset's row opens its own page. The numbers update as payments move. Beside it, Recent shows your last few payments, with a link to all your activity.

  • Spendable. Where a payment cannot spend all of a balance, a quiet line under it says how much it can, such as 15.55 spendable. The rest is reserves, and what is held for your peers.
  • In flight. A small figure in brackets, such as (XRP 0.25 in flight), is the part of that balance that payments under way hold right now. It is still yours until those payments finish, usually within seconds.
  • In all. At the top of the card, in large figures, the wallet adds your assets up in one currency. You choose that currency in Exchange-rate settings. The total shows only while every rate it needs is fresh: with one missing or stale, there is no total rather than a wrong one.

Amounts are exact. The wallet cuts digits it does not show and never rounds up, so a balance never looks larger than it is. Each amount shows its currency first, then two decimals, then up to two more muted ones.

An asset's own page, from its row or from Assets, breaks its balance down: what is in your own account, what is in the hub, what is on its way between them, and what each part sets aside. An asset's page explains it.

A balance can go below zero if the underlying ledger lost money, for example when a token's issuer takes some back. Your node stops spending on that ledger until it is above zero again.

Payments in and out

Open Activity in the top menu. It has two lists, newest first, each with the last 100 payments.

Incoming payments

Each incoming payment is a request to be paid: one you made under Receive, or one an app or another wallet made through Open Payments to pay you. Each row shows:

  • what it is for, if whoever made it said so, else Incoming payment;
  • what has arrived, and of how much, if it asked for a set amount, such as $ 4.20 of $ 10.00. Once it is paid in full it shows the amount alone, $ 10.00, even if what arrived is a hair over, less than half a ten-thousandth of a unit: no payer can hit an amount to the last digit, so the last packet rounds up (see Follow the payment);
  • who requested it: You, an app by its address, or A client known by its key; and when.

Its badge says where it stands:

Badge Means
open It still takes money.
completed It takes no more: it was paid in full, or someone completed it.
expired Its time ran out. What arrived before stays yours.

An open payment has a Complete button. Use it when you have what you need, say a payer sent less than you asked and you are fine with that. It takes no more money after that, whatever it asked for.

Outgoing payments

Outgoing payments are the ones you sent with Send, and the ones apps you approved sent for you (see Connect apps). Each row shows who it went to, who sent it, and the amount the payment fixed. For a payment of what they receive, that is what has arrived of what they receive, in their currency, such as XRP 12.00 of XRP 25.00, and once it is sent, what they receive. If it failed after money left, the row adds what it cost you of the most it may take, such as XRP 12.00 of XRP 25.00, paid $ 15.84 of $ 33.32. For a payment of what you send, it is what it has sent of the most it may take from you, such as $ 9.80 of $ 10.00, and once it is sent, what it sent.

Badge Means
sending It is still under way.
completed It delivered what it was meant to.
failed It stopped. The row says why, in plain words.

Select the recipient to open the payment's own page. It follows the payment live until it completes or fails, and shows what the receiver gets, what it cost you, when it started and when it finished.

If a list says Reconnecting or Not available, the wallet has lost its live view of your node, and shows that rather than old numbers as current. It reconnects by itself.

What your node holds outside its books

Your balances are your node's books. The money behind them sits in accounts outside it: your account on the XRP Ledger testnet, and your account in each settlement hub you are in. Each asset's page on Assets shows them.

Under What the XRP Ledger holds, the page shows your account as the ledger last reported it, never added up from the books:

  • its XRP, and the reserve the ledger holds back on it;
  • each token on a trust line, with its issuer;
  • a red notice if a token's issuer froze your line or the whole token, and what that stops.

Its Deposit section has the account's address, as text and as a QR code, where you send money to fund it, and on the testnet a Get test XRP button, which asks the testnet faucet for free test XRP, once a minute at most. If the page says the account does not exist yet, send it XRP or wait for the faucet. Add and fund an asset says more.

For your hub accounts in detail (balance, holds toward peers, deposits and withdrawals) use the Hubs page.

Why it works this way: holdings as the ledger reports them, never as the books add them up.
