# Testing

How Dassie is tested: what each level of tests is for, what the deterministic simulations judge and why, how to reproduce a failure, and the campaign that runs the simulations continuously.

**Paths:** `crates/*/tests/`, `crates/*/src/sim/`, `crates/dassie-sim/`, `scripts/sim-campaign.ts`, `scripts/sim-campaign/`, `crates/dassie-kit/src/sim.rs`, `tests/e2e/`, `tests/interop/`, `testdata/`, `.config/nextest.toml`, `**/*_test.ts`
**Read with:** [Engineering](../engineering.md), [Release](release.md), [Website](website.md), [Dev environment](dev.md)

## Levels

Deterministic simulation is the main source of confidence ([tigerstyle](../decisions/tigerstyle.md)), and the other levels cover what a simulation cannot judge:

- golden vectors pin the wire formats and derivations that must never change;
- interop tests pin the standards against their reference implementations;
- tests over real sockets, and end-to-end tests, check the real IO layer that a simulation replaces ([below](#over-real-sockets)).

Rules for every test:

- **Every decoder has a "never panics on arbitrary input" property test.**
- **Every simulation or property-test failure prints the seed that reproduces it.**
- **CI runs fixed seeds only, no simulation sweeps and no fuzzing:** a simulation in CI is a scenario on one fixed seed, or the few that between them reach what it checks ([Engineering](../engineering.md#no-fuzzing-or-random-seeds-in-ci)).
- **Each Rust test runs in its own process, at once with every other.** CI runs them with cargo-nextest (`.config/nextest.toml`), crate by crate, so that it takes as long as its slowest test rather than all of them in turn, and runs again only the tests of the crates a change reached ([crate-by-crate-builds](../decisions/crate-by-crate-builds.md)). A test relies on no other test's process, state or order, and takes its ports from the OS, or fixed ones no other test uses. Nextest retries no test: one that fails only sometimes fails CI.
- **No Rust test's outcome depends on how fast the machine is.** A Rust test waits for progress, on its own clock or for an event, never for a span of real time ([below](#over-real-sockets)). The end-to-end tests cannot: they run the real binaries on a real clock with dev timings ([below](#simulation-time-follows-production)), so they wait for the protocol in real time. A wait for progress there polls against a generous limit, a hang's guard, and a check that something does not happen watches for longer than the dev timing that would make it happen.
- **The end-to-end tests skip what they lack outside CI, and fail on it in CI** (`CI=true`), so a missing tool never passes CI silently.

**Which stub a test settles on.** By the [hubs-only](../decisions/hubs-only.md) decision, a test whose subject is not settlement does not depend on the plain stub settling, except those that pay across currencies, until those move. Such a test or simulation run (of the peer protocol, routing, payments, crashes and restarts, the wallet, the RPC) runs its nodes on the stub with unlimited trust, so that no credit runs out, and no request to settle or settlement shapes what it checks ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-stub-scheme)): the node's core tests, its daemon tests, its targeted tests on simulated networks, the scripted `node-stub` runs and a share of the `node` simulation's runs do. A test or run whose subject is settlement runs on the stub with commitments, whose settlements fund holds as a hub member's do, or on a simulated hub ([below](#over-a-settlement-hub)). Settlement methods are tested on a second stub with commitments that pretends to be a second hub: a pair of engine cores over two pretend hubs peers on both, keeps each method's accounts, credit, requests and backoff apart, carries packets on both, each on a slot of its own in the peer's endpoint, keeps both across a restart, and when one is dropped takes no new packet on its slot, while those in flight on it resolve there and its hub's scheme hears of each, fulfilled or rejected ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods), [Connector](connector.md#settlement-method-slots)). Only the targeted tests of direct settlement, on the plain stub or the fake XRP Ledger, still settle directly, and leave the code with it ([Roadmap](../roadmap.md#hubs-only)). The scripted runs of owner books keep each node's books on the fake XRP Ledger and peer on none of them ([below](#the-simulations)).

The install tests, the NixOS VM test and the static-binary check run only in the release workflow, before anything is signed ([Release](release.md#tests)).

## Golden vectors

The frozen surfaces of the [overview](../overview.md#frozen-surfaces) are pinned by vectors generated from the original implementation's code (`testdata/original/`, see its README), into `crates/<crate>/tests/data/`. Each file records its source commit, and regenerating must not change a committed vector. Where Dassie deliberately differs from the original (a bug fixed), the Rust test carries its own hand-written vector.

Other vectors come from their own reference: the identity chain and every seed path from a generator apart from Dassie's code, with libraries independent of it and the phrase's Reed–Solomon code written out on its own (`testdata/identity/`, [Identity](identity.md#derivation-chain)), XRPL Payments, with and without `SendMax`, signed as xrpl.js signs them, and the live XRP Ledger's records and the proof servers' answers, captured read-only by the scripts beside them (`testdata/xrpl/`), Open Payments from the standards' own examples, releases from minisign with a test key (`testdata/release`). The rate feed has no other implementation, so its vectors come from its own generator with a fixed key (`testdata/feed`, [Rate feed](rate-feed.md#test-vectors)): they pin its bytes against change, not against a reference. So do a settlement hub's (`testdata/hub`, [Settlement hubs](hubs.md#golden-vectors)). The WASM builds run the same vectors, and must produce the bytes the native tests check, so the wallet derives exactly what the node derives.

## Property tests

Properties pin what no example can: every decoder never panics, only canonical encodings parse, no transfer breaks a ledger limit, balances equal the journal. Their regression files (`*.proptest-regressions`) are committed beside the tests. They run from one fixed seed, set in `.cargo/config.toml`, so a local `cargo test` runs what CI runs; a seed in `PROPTEST_RNG_SEED` explores others, outside CI ([random-seed-simulations](../decisions/random-seed-simulations.md)).

## Interop

Dassie runs against the reference implementations, which are the oracle for the standards: STREAM against `ilp-protocol-stream` in both directions, natively and in WASM; the BTP server against `ilp-plugin-btp`; Open Payments against `@interledger/open-payments`, which validates every response against the specs, with the node both as server and as a client of another node. The WASM build is also run in a headless browser, as the wallet and the website load it.

## Over real sockets

The Rust tests that run nodes over real sockets (`crates/*/tests/`) are the only check of what a simulation replaces with its in-memory transport: the transports and their security, their framing, and how a node handles the connections it makes and takes. They check that IO layer, and leave the protocol to the simulations:

- **A real-socket test checks only what a simulation cannot.** It runs as much protocol as it needs to reach the IO it checks. A protocol scenario judged for its own sake (a validator catching up, a peering ending, settlements after a partition) belongs in a simulation.
- **The protocol it runs, it runs on a clock the test moves by hand** (`ManualClock`). The node's protocol timers, the engine's and those of the services beside it, run on the node's clock and wake when it moves, so that a test reaches a wait of minutes or days in steps rather than in real time. Only the IO layer's own timers (a connection's or a handshake's timeout, the backoff before a connection is made again, the pace at which a ledger server is read) and the supervisor's backoff between a failed service's starts run on real time by design.
- **A wait is bounded by progress, never by wall time.** A test waits for what it looks for, while it moves its clock on, and gives up after a number of steps of its clock, never after a span of real time: no sleep that stands in for a wait, no polling against a real-time limit a slow machine can reach. Where only real time will do, as for QUIC's own transport timers, the wait is generous, a hang's guard rather than what decides the test, and its comment says why. Nor does a test race a real-time timer it does not check: one that holds a place open to count it has the node bound it longer than the test waits, so that only what the test does ends it.

A simulation already judges the protocol, deterministically and on every seed it runs, so a real-socket test that also races the wall clock adds nothing to that confidence, only a way to fail. CI runs every test at once on a shared machine, where a loaded one runs a real-time scenario several times slower than a developer's, and nextest retries nothing: such a test fails pull requests that never touched what it tests.

## Deterministic simulation

N nodes, or a crate's state machines, are wired in-process through the sans-IO crates, with a virtual clock and a seeded RNG (`dassie_kit::sim::SeededRng`, reproducible, not cryptographic). Every simulation is library code in its crate's `src/sim/`, so the scenario tests (on fixed seeds) and the runner (on any seed) run the same code. What they share (the seeded RNG, a run's outcome, running seeds) is the `sim` feature of `dassie-kit`, a leaf crate that depends on no other of the workspace's: a protocol crate's simulation takes on nothing of the node's.

- Between them, the simulations inject latency, loss, reordering, duplication, partitions, clock skew, crashes and restarts, failed commits, lying peers, and lying ledger servers. What the `node` simulation does not inject yet is in the [roadmap](../roadmap.md#continuous-simulation).
- Every state machine's invariants are checked after each step.
- A run is reproducible from its seed: two runs with the same seed produce identical event traces, and tests check it, and that the run passes, on seeds whose runs are short.

### Simulation time follows production

The simulations run the production timings, not the dev ones: on the virtual clock a simulated day costs seconds, and the ledgers they settle on, like the fake XRP Ledger, run at their real speed. Dev timings, scaled down, are only for what runs on a real clock: the dev environment and the end-to-end tests.

Once a run heals, payments must complete, settlements arrive, routes converge and no node owe an honest peer past that peer's trigger, within a time derived from the production rules: for the `node` simulation, the longest wait a fault leaves behind (the backoff of a registration, a refresh, a peering attempt or a settlement, or a peer's hold-down), then as long as a network takes to be ready, then as long as a run's payments, and the settlements they call for, may take. A deadline is derived from the rules the node runs by, never chosen so that a run passes ([random-seed-simulations](../decisions/random-seed-simulations.md)). The one exception to payments completing is also a production rule: a node's payer gives up after a bounded number of reconnects, so an outgoing payment that failed in the chaos, or whose payer was already reconnecting when the run healed (since its node last started: a start gives the payer all its reconnects again), may end failed, since what is left of its reconnects can run out before the healed network routes it, for instance while a pair an owner's end split must peer anew. A payment started after the heal must complete. After a partition that outlasted the chaos it starts once the healed network is ready again, as the run's first payments do, since the network then forms anew for longer than a payer keeps trying; otherwise it starts at once, while the network re-forms after the chaos. Nor does the heal ask for routes or settlements across a pair that an owner's end or a ban keeps apart.

### Failed commits

Faults stay within what the node relies on. The store commits with `synchronous = FULL`, so a commit that returned is on disk, and a torn write is SQLite's to handle; the only fault injected at the store is a commit that fails, as on a full disk ([random-seed-simulations](../decisions/random-seed-simulations.md)).

- **It fails in SQLite,** through its commit hook, behind the `testing` feature, so SQLite rolls the transaction back as it would, and the engine meets the error its real commit returns.
- **The node stops as the daemon's engine does** ([Node](node.md#the-engine)): the core panics without handing over anything of the step, a peer request it was answering gets the daemon's 503, and the simulation treats the node as crashed until it restarts it from its last good commit.
- **It is aimed at the commits that matter:** one that books a packet's Fulfill, at a node with a payment under way; one that holds a settlement, ours prepared or executed or a peer's booked; or any. A later such commit may be the one that fails, so that a settlement can fail once it was executed.
- **What it checks** is that nothing the node sent rested on the lost commit: each node's books still hold against what its clients, receivers and peers saw, and a request answered 503 that is sent again is taken once.

### Long partitions

In some seeds the `node` simulation splits the network in two, cutting one node off from the rest or the network in halves. A split's length is drawn from the production timings that act on silence, never from what lets a run pass. Most splits are shorter than the silence that ends a peering, and spread over the timings below it: a peer's confirmation and hold-down, a stale link state, a registration's renewal. The rest outlast it, so that peerings end for silence, registrations and lists lapse and incoming payments expire before the network heals.

A split that outlasts the chaos keeps the network as the chaos left it, its cuts too, until the split ends; only a node that is down starts again, as its supervisor would start it, since held down for days it would leave the split no peering to part. A cut one way then lasts as long, as behind an asymmetric firewall: a node hears a peer it cannot reach.

- **When the split ends,** nothing has happened that must not happen even with money in flight, and every outgoing payment past its deadline has ended, completed or failed: a payment does not wait for the network to heal.
- **A client paying across the split** stops once its receiver's incoming payment has expired, with what arrived; its books are judged as any, but it need not have received all it asked for.
- **After the heal,** the production rules decide what must happen: peerings ended for silence form again through the bootstrap nodes and auto-peering, routes converge and settlements resume, within the [heal's deadline](#simulation-time-follows-production).

### Each node judged by its own books

Beyond the invariants, a simulation judges each node on its own, by its own books, and never compares two nodes' books or adds them up: each node keeps its own, and they may drift ([Engineering](../engineering.md#each-node-keeps-its-own-books), [own-books](../decisions/own-books.md)).

- **Against what really happened:** a node's books hold what its scheme holds, on a token exactly its line in whole units; its owner's funds moved by what its receivers took and its payers sent, and each token's by what its client sent. What an issuer did to a node's line counts as its owner's: what it minted a deposit, what it clawed back a withdrawal. The underlying ledgers neither make nor lose money, each asset apart in runs across currencies, a token's holders holding what its issuer minted less what came back to it.
- **Its decisions, against the liquidity its books show:** it refuses a Prepare for liquidity only by the limits its books set; it takes every settlement an honest peer sends; once the network is calm, it owes no peer past its own threshold for long; once it knows a peering is ending, it starts no settlement to that peer until they peer again; when its owner bans a peer, it tells the peer at once, asks it for nothing and never peers with it again; and when it ends one, it tells the peer only once what the peer last said it had on its way is booked, earlier bookings included, or once the wait for it ran out ([ending-a-peering](../decisions/ending-a-peering.md)).
- **Against the ledger's truth,** whatever its servers lie: nothing booked that the fake XRP Ledger did not apply, no settlement cancelled that it applied, and nothing written off, since it never resets in a simulation. Each would leave a node's books apart from its account, which its scheme could only reconcile without a reason it can name, so no node may reconcile anything but a change of the reserve. And every node on the XRP Ledger anchored, on a ledger its UNL validated ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#11-the-fake-ledger-the-lying-server-and-the-simulation)).
- **Against adversaries:** whatever a lying peer does (never settle, spend the credit it was given and vanish), an honest node loses no more than the credit it extends ([own-fulfills](../decisions/own-fulfills.md)); one that vanishes loses its peering.
- **Against forgery:** a copy of a request, sent again by someone who saw it arrive, can only be sent under the forger's own key, since the transport proves the sender's; the simulations' transports deliver it so. Nothing is taken from the forger but what anyone may send: a read, a link state or a commitment to a name ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#authentication)). The `peer` and `node` simulations both send such copies.
- **Liveness:** payments complete, and settlements arrive. A client pays an amount whatever its path delivers, as STREAM leaves to its user: it measures the rate again a while after a measurement failed, or after packets that kept failing stopped its money, as when a withdrawn route sends them through a hop that charges more or none is left ([STREAM](stream.md#responses)), and connects anew once its connection closed. What arrived must still be what was sent at the rates less what the hops charged. Not across a link that crashes drifted past what it survives, which the design does not save. The simulation tells how far each pair's books drifted from what crossed the network, never from the books: a Fulfill the peer booked and its sender never took, lost to a crash or a failed commit, or else refused, late or cut off. A network that did not settle down while crashes alone drifted a link that far is ruled out; drift from anything else excuses nothing ([crash-drift](../decisions/crash-drift.md)).

The peer protocol's own simulation checks what the protocol must make agree, which is not money: link states and routes converge, peerings are two-sided, and each settlement is applied exactly once.

### The simulations

Each simulation lives in its crate's `src/sim/`, and `dassie-sim list` names them all. The `peer` simulation runs the peer protocol alone; `connector` drives the connector with misbehaving clients; `stream` runs a payment over a lossy path, a share of its packets taking a dearer route; `hub` runs a settlement hub's consensus among faulty and colluding validators ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#consensus)), then its ledger, in most seeds a token hub's through its issuer's freezes, fees and clawbacks ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#the-ledger)); `names` has nodes register names with four bootstrap nodes, one of them perhaps faulty ([below](#names)); `node` runs real engine cores with their own databases, on the stub with unlimited trust or settling on the stub with commitments, through a seeded stretch of chaos and a heal, where owners also end peerings and ban peers, but never so that the pairs they keep apart split the network; the scripted node runs set up one situation each (crashes and restarts mid-payment on the stub with unlimited trust, and packets over the stub with commitments); `node-owner-books` runs the same script with each node's owner books of XRP and DSUSD on the fake XRP Ledger's testnet beside the stub with unlimited trust, peering on neither ([XRPL](xrpl.md#owner-books)), while its owner pays out of the XRP book and the token's issuer claws back and freezes lines, then its servers tell every kind of lie in turn while a validator rotates its key, a quorum is withheld and a list lapses; `node-owner-books-live` keeps the owner books of XRP and RLUSD in the live realm on the fake ledger's mainnet, funded by its owner's deposits, through the issuer's acts and a lying server, its nodes peering with no one, since the stub runs only in the test realm; and `node-hub` and `node-hub-dsusd` run members peered over a settlement hub of XRP or of DSUSD whose validators run in the simulation, `node-hub-lies` the XRP hub's members and validators under servers that tell every kind of lie, `node-hub-currencies` payments across dollars in the DSUSD hub and XRP in the XRP hub, and `node-hubs` nodes in both hubs, each pair peered over both ([below](#over-a-settlement-hub)). Seeds that draw their configuration print it before the run starts.

Half the `node` simulation's runs settle over the stub with commitments ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-stub-scheme)), so that their packets take the path over a settlement hub ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)); the other half run on the stub with unlimited trust, where every node's stub must end holding what it started with. After every step, no node's stub holds more of its packets to a peer, or of the peer's to it, than its connector holds pending with that peer: a promise kept for a packet the connector let go would never be resolved, and would take one of the peer's places for good. And the credit each node extends to each peer over the stub is its hold toward the peer and its settlements to it on their way ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)).

An issuer's freeze stops what the nodes it touches pay or are paid in its token, and lasts up to the grace a peer short of room has, drawn from the production timings. Where pairs settle in the token, the check that routine settlement withdraws no honest peer accepts that the pairs a freeze touches are withdrawn while it holds and for a grace after it is lifted: that is the production rule at work ([Settlement](settlement.md#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)). It accepts the same for a stretch in which the ledger's servers or validators fail a node that settles on that ledger, for the pairs it is in, since a node that cannot see its ledger settles nothing: a lying server fails only the node it lies to, a withheld quorum or a lapsed list every node. In `node-owner-books` the freezes, the lying servers and the failing validators touch only owner books, which peer with no one, so the check excuses nothing there.

**Lying servers.** A node's connection to the fake XRP Ledger drops and comes back to a server that tells one kind of lie ([XRPL](xrpl.md#proofs-in-the-fake-ledger)), for twice the longest of the production timings that end a connection; the next connection is honest, and so is one the node makes itself on catching the lie. `node-owner-books` tells every node every kind in turn. The `node` simulation draws no lying servers until it draws runs over a hub, whose members' owner books they would lie to, for a time drawn up to that longest and in an order each seed shuffles ([Roadmap](../roadmap.md#continuous-simulation)). Validators sign with a keyed hash under a secret only the fake and the nodes' check hold, behind the interface the design names, so that a simulated day stays cheap and no server can forge a validation; everything else is checked for real ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#11-the-fake-ledger-the-lying-server-and-the-simulation)).

The node simulations count what they reached (crashes mid-payment, cuts, packets over a hub, settlements, withdrawals, …), and `dassie-sim run` adds the counts up over a range of seeds, so that whoever changes the generator sees whether it still reaches every kind of fault and payment. On the XRP Ledger they also count the lying servers, what the nodes' accounts proved (anchors, transactions, absences), the lies told and caught, the lists fetched and the keys rotated, and the nodes that anchored. The fixed seeds of `node-hub-lies` must between them tell every kind of lie, since a lying server's connection may be too short to tell some, and catch them ([below](#over-a-settlement-hub)), and the fixed seed of `node-owner-books` must catch lies, prove what its nodes read and see its owners' payments out land: so a change that quietly bypasses the verifier fails. An outcome a drawn run reaches only rarely, such as a settlement booked after its peering ended, has a targeted test.

### Over a settlement hub

The `node-hub` simulation runs a hub of 4 to 7 validators inside the node simulation, each the node's own validator role without its IO: the driver on a database of its own, and the watcher of the escrow on its own connection to the fake XRP Ledger, proving what it reads ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#the-validator-in-the-node)). The sponsor sets the escrow up on the fake ledger as the rules say; the validators' messages, and the members' requests to them and their answers, cross the simulated network in the region's delays, with its losses; the validators' clocks are off within the precision. The hub's terms are the design's starting values, with the timings derived from consensus's (the margin with f faulty leaders, and the allowance the rest of the connector's message window) and the stall the shortest the rules allow. Each seed draws:

- **the hub:** 4 to 7 validators, up to f of them faulty, as the `hub` simulation's roles are (silent, withholding their proposals, proposing what is invalid or to too few, equivocating), and in half the runs an honest validator that crashes and restarts from its database while payments are under way;
- **its members:** 3 or 4 nodes that join the hub by deposit from their XRP Ledger testnet accounts, then the network, so that they peer over the hub; two of them never peer, so payments cross a hop, both ways;
- **a lying member,** in three runs of four: a payer that sends no claims, a payee that keeps its Fulfills and claims on the hub only just before the commitments expire, or a payee that claims on the hub what its payer's claims cover, and each claim twice.

Once the network is calm (each account read the hub up to a recent block, each move of funds into or out of the hub is done, and each peer short of room on any method settled once asked, all within the waits the production rules give), it checks each node by its own books ([Engineering](../engineering.md#each-node-keeps-its-own-books)): its books hold what its schemes hold, on the XRP Ledger its account's balance less the reserve, in the hub its account's balance and its peers' holds toward it as the honest validators have them, once it read the operations of its own they applied; its owner's funds on both ledgers moved by what its receivers took; the credit it extends to a peer on each hub method never fell below its hold toward the peer there and its settlements on their way; and what it booked as lost on a peer over a hub is never more than the holds between them on that hub ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#packets-promises-and-claims)). The escrow holds what the hub owes and what the sponsor left, and the fake ledger made or lost no XRP. After every step of a validator, its invariants hold, no two honest validators finalized different states at one height, and no evidence names an honest one. Payments must complete, but those through a member that keeps its Fulfills or claims twice, of which only what must never happen is checked; and every honest member's account must read the hub again, and its moves be done.

Then the hub ends. In nine runs of ten it loses f + 1 validators at once, its faulty ones among them, and stalls: each validator still up declares it within the stall and a view, and signs the payout set of its last final block; the honest ones lost come back one by one, a stall apart at most, and sign in their turn. The simulation gathers the signatures over time from what each validator kept, as a member would, and submits the latest set a quorum signed, in sequence order. The escrow must then have paid each account with a disbursal address and enough to pay out its balance, its reserves and the holds it paid into, less the fee, worked out apart from the set; every transaction of the set applied; and the escrow kept what it held less what the set paid. In the tenth run, f + 1 validators collude and fork the chain: should honest validators finalize different states, evidence an honest one found must name the colluders, and only them.

Three more runs share its hub, its waits and its checks of the hub and of each member's books, with every validator honest and no member lying. A run may found two hubs on the one fake ledger, the XRP hub and the DSUSD hub, each with its own nonce, escrow, validators and endpoints, and the checks then hold hub by hub: each member's books in each hub it is in, each escrow in its asset (an XRP hub's with what its sponsor left, a token hub's XRP being gas), each hub's validators. Each method of a peering is held to its own hub: what a member booked as lost on a peer over a hub is bounded by the holds between them on that hub, which bounds what the peer can cost summed over the methods of their peering ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)), and the check that a member settled once asked looks at each method on its own books.

**Lying servers over a hub.** `node-hub-lies` does to the members of a hub what `node-owner-books` does to nodes keeping their owner books on the XRP Ledger ([above](#the-simulations)). Once 3 or 4 members joined a hub of 4 to 7 validators and pay each other, their connections to the fake ledger come back to servers that lie, in rounds that give every member the next kind of lie, in an order the seed shuffles, until every kind was told: a lie of a stale anchor needs a ledger older than the first rounds may have, so some seeds tell it and others do not. As each connection drops the member's owner asks the faucet for more, and the members pay each other while their servers lie. One member's owner moves funds into the hub by hand, as its connection drops and again once its node takes the move while its server lies, so that the server has the member's own deposit to the escrow to leave out or misreport; since an owner's move pauses its node's own moves for longer than the run (`OWNER_PAUSE_MS`), the other members' nodes move their funds by themselves, on what their lying servers tell them. Then one of the ledger's validators rotates its key; the publisher publishes a list that is to lapse, and a quorum of the validators is withheld, so that every member, having lost quorum, fetches the lists at once and holds the one that lapses when it does ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#5-the-unl-from-its-publishers)); the list lapses a stall after the quorum is back, and another comes once the members waited for it. In each round, the servers of one to all of the hub's validators lie as well, for as long as the round lasts and on every connection their watchers make: in one round in two each tells another of the round's lies, in the other all tell the same one. Each member's books must hold to the fake ledger's truth throughout (nothing booked, cancelled or written off that the ledger did not do) and to the hub as the run above checks them; the validators must attest nothing false, so that after every ledger of a round the escrow holds what the hub owes and the sponsor left, but for withdrawals it paid that no quorum attested yet, and no more but for deposits on their way, and the hub writes off no reset; every member anchored; the payments complete; no honest peer is withdrawn for room but where its ledger failed it, as above; and the hub finalizes blocks throughout. The test's seeds must between them tell every kind of lie, which the members catch, and lie to validators, and an owner's move and a node's own move must each be made while a server lies.

**Across currencies over hubs.** `node-hub-currencies` runs 3 or 4 nodes across dollars and XRP over two hubs of 4 to 7 validators each: node 0 in dollars in the DSUSD hub; node 2 in XRP in the XRP hub; nodes 1 and 3 in both, node 1 charging for what passes. Each member runs the owner book of XRP, and of DSUSD if it is in the DSUSD hub, peering on neither, and funds each hub from the book of its asset. Once the members joined their hubs, each one's owner moves a stake into each (`HUB_STAKE_XRP`, `HUB_STAKE_MICROS`) and turns its automatic moves there off, so that a node that converts into a hub, or pays from it, holds there as much as on a ledger it settles on: the automatic moves keep only what settling needs, far less than a payment converted into the hub takes ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)). Clients pay receivers in the other currency both ways and into a node's own receiver, and an outgoing payment crosses, at the rates, through nodes that price by their inventory ([passive-rebalancing](../decisions/passive-rebalancing.md)). What a run across currencies checks must hold: each payment arrived with at least what was sent at the rates, moved by the converting node's curve, less the hops' fees; no node priced beyond its curve; no ledger made or lost an asset; no honest peer was withdrawn for room; and in each hub, what the run above checks of the validators, the escrow and each member's books.

**Two hubs per node.** `node-hubs` puts 3 or 4 nodes in both hubs, each of 4 to 7 honest validators ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)). Each node runs the owner books of XRP and DSUSD, peering on neither; the issuer mints it DSUSD, and it joins each hub from the book of its asset and moves a stake into each, as above. Once they joined both, they join the network, two of them never peering, and every peering must come to have a method on each hub, so that each node's link state lists both hubs and each pair settles over both. Payments go in each currency across the hop and from one into the other, each packet on the method of the ledger it came in on ([Connector](connector.md#settlement-method-slots)), so each method must carry packets. Once the network is calm, what a run across currencies checks must hold, and in each hub what the runs above check. Then the hub of the method the peerings were not made on loses f + 1 validators, stalls, and its escrow pays out exactly the final balances, as in `node-hub`, while payments go on over the first: they must arrive less only the hops' fees, the first hub must finalize blocks throughout, every peering must be kept with its method on that hub, and that hub and each member's books in it must hold as before.

The `node-hub-dsusd` simulation runs as `node-hub` does over a hub of DSUSD, of 4 to 7 honest validators, its escrow holding DSUSD on its trust line to the fake ledger's issuer ([token-hubs](../decisions/token-hubs.md)). Each of its 3 or 4 members runs the owner books of XRP and DSUSD on its XRP Ledger testnet account, peering on neither; the issuer mints it DSUSD, and it opens its account in the hub by a deposit from its DSUSD book. Once they pay each other across a hop over the hub, the token's pool moves off its peg: every member's peg guard must halt the conversions of the hub's ledger within its trip hold and the rates' next reading, while the members still pay each other over the hub. Then the escrow's XRP is brought below the least gas, in no transaction, as the fees of a long life would bring it, and a member's owner withdraws: the node may take the withdrawal only once the members' top-ups from their XRP books since the fall have made up the escrow's shortfall, and the escrow must pay it after. Then someone sends the escrow XRP tagged with a member's account, which must credit no one. Once the network is calm, each member's books hold its XRP book's account less the reserve, its DSUSD book's line, and in the hub what the honest validators have; its owner's DSUSD in the book and the hub together moved by what its receivers took; and the escrow holds in DSUSD what the hub owes, but for what is on its way, and the fake ledger made or lost neither XRP nor DSUSD.

Between the pool's move and the fall of the escrow's XRP, the issuer holds the escrow, a moment into a round of payments, so that packets are on their way when it comes: by a seed's draw it freezes the escrow's line, deep-freezes it, freezes its token for every holder, or charges a transfer fee ([A frozen hub](hubs.md#a-frozen-hub)). Every member must read the freeze from what the validators state of the issuer's hold within the ledger that applies it, two of the validators' proofs of the issuer's state and a minute. While it lasts, longer than any commitment taken before it runs, no member may send a Prepare over the hub, a payment made meanwhile must not cross it, and no member's account may be in step for moves or packets; between the freeze's block and the thaw only claims within the freeze's window may take from the holds, which the run counts as the claims the frozen hub paid, and no hold may grow or open. Once the issuer lets go and every member read the thaw, every payment must complete, those the freeze held up and new ones; no member may have booked a loss on a peer over the hub but the fee of a claim it made there itself, and the checks of the calm network that follow must find each member's books whole. Node 1 then sends node 0 a Prepare whose commitment holds in every respect but that it expires a second further ahead than a connector holds a packet and the hub's precision, which node 0 must refuse (T04), and node 1 must then pay node 0 as before. Once the network is calm after the gift, the hub ends. In three runs of four the issuer claws back a quarter to a half of what the escrow holds: once the hub ended, counting what the issuer took, the escrow must have held what the hub owes less that, and paid the withdrawals the hub had sequenced in full and each account with a disbursal address its share of what it held beyond them, in proportion to what the hub owed it, worked out apart from the set and rounded down, to the unit, and never more than it held. Otherwise the hub stalls, and the escrow must pay each account what the hub owed it. Either way it pays in DSUSD, after which each member's DSUSD book holds its line. The test's seed ends by a clawback. Members that lose nothing they had not redeemed when a hold's reduction was pending at a freeze are not yet part of the run ([Roadmap](../roadmap.md#token-hubs)).

**Joining by being paid.** Two scripted runs share the `node-hub` simulation's hub, of 4 honest validators, and its checks. They are no runs of their own that `dassie-sim` names or the campaign draws: the node's tests run each on a fixed seed (`crates/dassie-node/tests/hub_sim.rs`). In each, nodes with no account in the hub, their own moves off so that they never deposit, peer over it with one member alone ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#the-members-side)). In the first, the newcomer, paid less than the hub's minimum for a new account, is not paid in; paid past it, its payer pays it in, though the payer crashes with its `pay` on its way and the newcomer once its account is open; it sets its disbursal address, which the hub takes before any other operation of a new account, never deposits, is paid and pays over the hub from then on, and once the network is calm every check above holds, its owner's books without the account reserve its owner never paid. In the second, fresh keys paid less than the minimum try to spend twice what they are owed, and go away: none is paid in, none spends more than it is owed, and the member extends none of them credit.

**Validators under lying servers.** In every run over a hub, each validator's watcher of the escrow proves what it reads under the fake ledger's UNL ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#the-validator-in-the-node)). The node's `hub_proofs` tests run a simulated hub whose validators' servers lie, each a different lie at once, all the same one, or one validator through every lie in turn, and check that the members' books hold, that no reset is written off that the ledger did not make, and that once the servers are honest the members join and their books match exactly; `node-hub-lies` does the same while its members pay and their own servers lie ([above](#over-a-settlement-hub)).

### Names

The `names` simulation registers names with four bootstrap nodes, the registrars ([Network](network.md#names)), at the production timings. Each seed draws:

- **the registrars:** all honest, or one of them faulty in one of the ways [the design](../designs/names.md#6-why-it-holds) allows for. One shows nodes different name lists, each one it could have served: each other registrar its own, so that every side of a split sees itself confirmed, and every other node, by the parity of its index, for each name the holder an honest registrar lists that sorts first, or last. One logs every commitment and never answers it. One passes every name unsealed at it to an adversary, a core node that at once registers the name itself at every registrar, and lists the adversary for it. The adversary registers through its own node, whose limits on registrations under way and on names it is shown as holding refuse most of what it is told, so it commits to the first few names of each window only. Its own state follows the protocol: what it does wrong is what it tells others (`dassie_peer::sim::Role`);
- **the registrants:** 2 to 4 core nodes beside the registrars, and 1 or 2 periphery nodes, which, with the honest registrars, register names through a stretch of chaos (a rough network, crashes, restarts and cuts), now and then two of them the same name at once;
- **the registrar cut off:** an honest registrar that, in the aftermath, restarts cut off from the other registrars.

The guarantees hold for an unsealing that reaches every honest registrar within its window ([the design](../designs/names.md#3-registering)), so the chaos ends early enough in a window that an unsealing it held up is still retried, and arrives, in time, and the network stays calm until every unsealing has arrived. Then comes the aftermath: the chaos again, with no registration started, while the last windows close, the names are granted and read, and a split waits to be released. The registrar cut off restarts at its start, losing the lists it read, and stays up, cut off from the other registrars, for longer than a split's wait, so that their lists are missing or stale at it throughout and must count for nothing ([Network](network.md#releasing-a-split)). A registrar restarting starts its waits again, so with registrars crashing as often as the chaos has them, splits are mostly released in the calm that follows, which lasts a split's wait and the lists read after it.

- **Throughout:** no two nodes but the faulty registrar ever take different holders for a name, frozen views and views restored at a restart too; the adversary never holds a name; no honest registrar releases a name that a registration must hold, such as one it lists while the others' lists are stale; every bound holds; and with a registrar that withholds receipts, no name is unsealed, granted or held: the censorship the design accepts, and no more.
- **Once calm:** every registration that unsealed before any other registration of its name started is held by its registrant in every node's view, and every view is fresh; with a registrar that withholds receipts, every registration was abandoned instead; and with every registrar honest, no split is left: every name a registrar lists, a quorum of them list, for one holder.

Each run counts the kind of registrar it drew and what it reached: names held, names contested and released, with every registrar honest too, names the adversary tried to take, names the registrar cut off kept, views frozen while they held names, and restarts of nodes that held names; `dassie-sim run names` adds them up over a range of seeds.

**What it does not cover,** and where that is tested instead:

- **A tip-off at commitment time.** The adversary learns names only from unsealings, never guesses one from who commits ([the design](../designs/names.md#6-why-it-holds)). A guesser who wins at one honest registrar only splits the vote, as two registrants committing at once do, which the simulation draws; `crates/dassie-peer/tests/names_holders.rs` releases a split of two to two, and has a faulty registrar fail to give a name two holders after one.
- **More than one adversary.** There is one faulty registrar, the most four tolerate, and one front-runner; several front-runners are tested nowhere, since the design's argument holds for each alone: its commitment is later than the honest one at every honest registrar.
- **Other than four registrars.** The quorum for each n is a unit test in `crates/dassie-peer/src/names/holders.rs`; `names_holders.rs` takes holders from five registrars with one faulty, and `names_registrant.rs` registers and renews with two.
- **Expiry and renewal.** At the production timings a name lives a year, so none expires or is renewed in a run. The registrar's unit tests and `names_registrar.rs` let names expire, renew them by their holder alone, in the grace period too, and free them; `names_registrant.rs` has a node renew its name by itself, at a registrar cut off past the name's expiry in its grace period too.

### Outcomes

A run ends as `pass`, `safety` (something that must never happen did: an assertion, an invariant or a check of what the run did), `liveness` (something that must happen did not, once the network was calm), or `unsupported` (the run drew a situation no code could recover from, such as a majority of bootstrap nodes cut off, or a link that crashes drifted past what it survives; it neither passes nor fails) (`dassie_kit::sim::Outcome`). Each of the node simulation's checks says which of the two failures it found. A run that broke both says safety: one that did not settle down is first checked for what must not happen even with money in flight, and one that did runs every check and reports a safety failure before any liveness failure.

Every outcome but `pass` also says where the run ended (`Finding`): the name of the check that failed or ruled the run out, or the file and line of a panic. It holds nothing a seed draws, so every seed that ends at the same place shares it; a check's name is words in lower case, so it cannot carry a number. With the simulation and the outcome, it is the run's **signature**, `<sim>/<outcome>/<where>`, which tells two failures apart whatever their seeds.

A check's name changes only when the check is renamed. A panic's file and line are what a panic offers, and cost two things: an edit above it in the same file moves its line, so one bug seen before and after such a commit gets two signatures; and a panic inside a dependency that does not pass on its caller's location puts every caller under one. The first splits an issue, the second may merge two, and the message, the trace and the replay tell them apart.

### The runner: `dassie-sim`

`dassie-sim` runs any simulation on any seed, to replay a failure, search a range for one, run a commit's own seed, or run random seeds as a campaign. It is a developer tool, never released.

**The campaign command.** `dassie-sim campaign` runs random seeds, drawn from the OS, on every core for a given time, and prints a line of JSON per finished run, then a summary. The lines are the runner's contract with the campaign's supervisor, set out in the runner's docs (`crates/dassie-sim/src/main.rs`).

- Each run's simulation is drawn by its weight. A weight counts runs, not time, so the simulations whose runs take milliseconds cost almost nothing, and the `node` simulation, with the most weight, gets most of the time.
- A record names its seed and signature; a failure's also holds what its seed drew and the end of its trace, enough to file it without running it again.
- Each run is a process of its own, so that runs share nothing and each takes as long as it does alone, on a core of its own. No run outlives its campaign: one that ends early stops and reaps its runs, and a run dies with a campaign that is killed, since nothing else would stop a stuck one.
- A panic is caught as a safety failure, and a run whose process ends without its record, such as one that overflows its stack, is one at `abort`, so that a run that panics or aborts cannot end the campaign. The supervisor's sandbox still stops the whole campaign when the kernel kills any of its processes for want of memory. Every simulation bounds its steps, so every run ends; a run that still takes far longer in wall time than any run should, stuck within a step, is stopped and reported as a liveness failure at `wall-time`.
- The campaign exits 0 when it ran to its end, whatever its runs found, since its records say that.

**The tripwire.** The `tripwire` simulation fails on purpose, for safety at one fixed place, on a small share of seeds, and passes the others at once. A campaign runs it among the others, so that a campaign whose tripwire stops failing shows that it has stopped. No test expects it to pass on such a seed.

**Reproducing a failure.** A test or run that fails names its simulation and seed.

- `dassie-sim run <sim> <seed>` replays it, and `dassie-sim explain <sim> <seed>` prints its trace, optionally only the lines that match.
- Build the runner with `--profile sim`, which is the test profile with assertions and overflow checks on, so a seed replays exactly as the test of the same seed.
- A seed reproduces a failure only at the commit it ran on, so a fix comes with a targeted test that sets up the situation directly.
- A panic is a safety failure that loses its trace; replay the seed to see what led to it.
- A git commit's full SHA is also a seed, so that a commit names its own run.

## The campaign

The campaign runs the simulations from random seeds on spot VMs, outside CI ([random-seed-simulations](../decisions/random-seed-simulations.md)). Its supervisor, `scripts/sim-campaign.ts`, runs one loop at a time on each VM: it builds a commit's `dassie-sim` in the `sim` profile, runs the runner's [campaign command](#the-runner-dassie-sim) on every core for a while, reports, and exits. systemd starts the next loop from a fresh checkout of `main`, so a change to the supervisor or the runner on `main` reaches every VM within a loop. The VMs' setup is in `scripts/sim-campaign/`.

- **What runs.** Most loops run `main`. Open pull requests labelled `sim` share some of the time; the label is the gate, since only people with triage access to the repository can set it.
- **It is not a check.** The campaign posts no commit status, on `main` or on a pull request: it reports in issues and comments only ([random-seed-simulations](../decisions/random-seed-simulations.md)).
- **A failure's [signature](#outcomes)** is its simulation, its outcome, and the assertion or check that failed: what tells two failures apart, whatever their seeds.
- **The [tripwire](#the-runner-dassie-sim),** a simulation that fails on purpose on a small share of seeds, shows that the campaign runs: while its issue keeps getting seeds, the campaign has not stopped. Its issue is never commented on.
- **A commit of `main`** gets one issue per failure signature, labelled `sim-failure`, or `sim-tripwire` for the tripwire, and found again by a marker in its body. The issue holds the commit, the configuration, the end of the trace and the commands that reproduce the seed that failed fastest. Later seeds update its counts and its fastest seed in place, and a comment says when it fails on a newer commit, a bounded number of times, so that no issue grows without end. A closed issue is never reopened: a signature seen again after its fix gets a new one.
- **A pull request** gets comments on itself, and nothing else: a summary of its latest loop, and one comment per failure signature, each edited in place. One that does not build gets the end of its build's log there instead.
- **A loop on `main` counts only if all of it was read.** A line the supervisor could not parse, a failure past what one loop keeps, or a count that disagrees with the runner's summary could each hide a failure. Such a loop files a failure under a signature of its own, `campaign/unreadable`, rather than pass over one nobody saw. A loop the runner did not finish is not failed for its last line, cut short: a runner stopped at its sandbox's limits leaves one, through no fault of the commit. Anything else it could not read still files one.
- **The campaign's own trouble** has an issue of its own, labelled `sim-campaign`. CI keeps `main` building, so a commit of `main` that the campaign cannot check out, fetch, build, or start the runner of almost always means the campaign itself is broken: its sandbox, its toolchain, a VM's disk, or its network. Such a loop files or updates that one issue, with the commit, the end of the step's log and the time, and exits with an error, so that its systemd unit shows as failed; systemd starts the next loop a minute later all the same, so that a fix on `main` or a network that comes back takes effect at once. Later occurrences update the issue in place, with a count and the latest time, and never comment, since the supervisor retries every minute. It is not a `sim-failure`, since nothing in it is the commit's fault. Without it, a campaign that cannot build would only go quiet, which nobody watches for.

### Isolating pull requests

A pull request's code is unreviewed, and the VMs run in the project that holds the bootstrap nodes' phrases. It must not be able to read the GitHub credentials, touch the issues, or change what a later loop on a trusted commit builds or reports.

- **The credentials live in the supervisor alone.** The VMs' service account has no role in the project and can read one secret, the GitHub App's key. The supervisor reads it into memory through the metadata server and mints short-lived tokens from it; it never writes it to disk or hands it to a child.
- **Every build and run is sandboxed** (`sandboxArgs` in the supervisor): as a user of its own, one for trusted commits and one for pull requests, with the file system read-only but for the step's own directories, no view of `/root`, `/home` or other users' processes, private `/tmp`, `/dev` and IPC, no privilege to gain, only a service's system calls, a share of memory and tasks that leaves the supervisor room to report, and no network, so no way to the metadata server, whose token reads the key. Build scripts and proc macros are the pull request's code too, and run inside.
- **Fetching dependencies needs the network,** and runs none of the commit's code: `cargo fetch --locked`, in a sandbox that reaches only public addresses through public resolvers, outside the commit's tree, so that no Cargo configuration of the pull request applies.
- **The Nix daemon is out of reach.** Its builds run outside the sandbox, with the network, so only root may use it and no sandbox can see its socket. The toolchain is `main`'s, from the flake's `campaign` shell: a pull request's flake is never evaluated, since its dev shell would run as root.
- **Nothing of a pull request outlives its loop.** It builds from nothing, in a directory removed before every loop and after its own, however it ends, so that no build of one can leave the disk full for `main`; its sandboxes stop with the loop; and trusted builds live in directories only the trusted user can write.
- **What it prints is untrusted data.** The supervisor parses it within bounds, uses it only for that pull request's comments, and puts its texts in fenced blocks they cannot close.

A pull request can still waste its own loop, fill the disk for the rest of it, and say what it likes in its own comments.

## End to end

The dev environment's scenarios run headless in CI on real binaries with the wallet built in: start several nodes, set them up through the HTTP API, add ledgers, peer, pay, settle, restart, and check that the balances are unchanged. None settles directly ([Dev](dev.md#scenarios)): a test whose subject is not settlement runs on the stub with unlimited trust, which never settles, and the scenarios' tests (`scenarios_test.ts`) check that the stubs never move and that each node's books owe its peers what its owner spent; a test of settlement settles over a hub. They also cover what only real IO shows: a fresh install, socket-activated, getting its certificate from an ACME test server; reference clients paying through BTP and Open Payments; the wallet in a headless browser, which makes a phrase, refuses a BIP-39 phrase and one of the other realm, and corrects a damaged phrase only once the owner confirms it; DSUSD in an owner book on the fake XRP Ledger (`dsusd_test.ts`), which books the issuer's mint, pauses its conversions while the pool prices it off its peg and still books what arrives, and sees the issuer freeze its line and books its clawback; and the XRP Ledger under a settlement hub, below. Every test on the XRP Ledger runs on the fake ledger alone, its nodes' XRPL schemes following it on proofs as they follow the public testnet: a hub's tests found a hub of their own on every run, whose escrow and validators stay off the public testnet.

**Mainnet's schemes in the live realm** (`mainnet_test.ts`, on `live-owner-books`): a node, set up in the live realm through the dev switch, holds XRP and RLUSD on the fake ledger's mainnet in owner books on one account, offering only mainnet's schemes, its incoming payments under `g.`. Its XRP book books a deposit once, and still once after a restart. Then RLUSD's pool is drained below its floor of XRP, which is no price, and the guard pauses the book until it reads one again; then the pool is moved 5% off its peg, and the node pauses its RLUSD book once the guard's hold has passed, on dev timers, says so in its ledger, and still books what the issuer sends it. Running again after the recovery hold, a minute even on dev timers, is tested on the guard and the engine, on their own clocks ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-peg-guard)).

**Across currencies** (`cross_currency_test.ts`, on `cross-currency`): the node in both currencies and the node with XRP join the scenario's hub by a deposit from their owner books, and the node with dollars pays an incoming payment of the node with XRP on the hub's ledger. The quote measures the path's rate, which is spot less the converting node's fee and spread, with its rebalancing curve off; the payer pays from its dollars, the converting node converts them and keeps its fee in dollars, and the payee receives XRP over the hub. The converting node settles with the payee over the hub, its hold toward the payee growing, and the payee's owner gets what arrived, less the fees of the payee's own moves; the stub settles nothing, and all three nodes' books are unchanged across their restart.

**A settlement hub** (`hub_test.ts`, on the `hub` scenario): four validators run the hub their sponsor founds on the fake ledger, and two members fund their accounts in it from their owner books, peer over it alone and settle a payment over it; what arrived is what was sent, each member's books hold what its accounts hold, in the hub its peers' holds toward it too, and both members' books are unchanged across their restart. Under the hub, the XRP Ledger (`xrpl_test.ts`): the faucet funds the members' owner books of XRP, which follow the fake ledger at addresses that start with `r`; a payment of more than the payer's account in the hub can spend above its floor makes the payer move XRP into the hub: its owner book pays exactly what moved and the XRP Ledger's fees for it, and its hub book gets what moved, less the payment and the hub's fees; and across both members' restart their owner books, their hub books and their accounts' counters in the hub are unchanged, each book holding what its account holds, so nothing is booked twice. The wallet's Receive page of a member's owner book (`xrpl_deposit_test.ts`, on a node added to the scenario as a member, so that its account is new) shows the deposit address, and its faucet button reaches the node, which refuses, since it asks the faucet at most once a minute.

**Passive rebalancing, the whole loop** (`market_maker_test.ts`, on the `market-maker` scenario), over hubs: the core node and the market maker's two nodes join their hubs by deposits from their owner books, and each of the market maker's nodes peers with the core node over the hub they share. A core node short of DSUSD publishes its feed, and `dassie market-maker`, run from the same build for a bounded time, trades through it over both hubs with its two pinned nodes and swaps back through the fake ledger's pool from its XRP node's owner book ([Rate feed](rate-feed.md#market-maker)). It reads what each party holds from its own books and the ledger, each asset in a hub and the owner book that funds it taken together: the core node's share of its value in DSUSD rose, toward its target; the market maker's value, at the pool's price after the run, grew; and every member's books are unchanged across their restart. With two targets (`market_maker_targets_test.ts`, on `market-maker-two-targets`), it trades through each: both core nodes take DSUSD in, and each one's share of its value in DSUSD rises. It waits on what the books and the pool show, each wait bounded, never on the market maker's log; the decisions themselves are the strategy's unit tests. Until a core node's inventory pricing counts each asset whole ([Rate feed](rate-feed.md#market-maker)), its feed sends the market maker's trades the other way: meanwhile the test with one target checks only that a trade crosses the core node over both hubs, which converts it, whichever way, and the books across the restart, and the test with two targets is skipped and says why.

**The wallet's Hubs page** (`wallet_test.ts`, on the `hub` scenario): once the members peered over the hub and one holds something for the other, that member's own moves are turned off and the test waits until none is on its way; a headless browser signed in to it shows its hub with the balance and the hold the node reports, in the hub ledger's units; a deposit made on the page reaches the hub, which its `deposited` counter shows grown by exactly that much, a refusal failing the test at once, and the page turns the node's own moves on again ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)).

**Across two hubs** (`hubs_cross_test.ts`, on the `two-hubs` scenario): the validators run an XRP hub and a DSUSD hub, both founded through the sponsor's owner RPC, and the members join each of their hubs by a deposit from their owner book of its asset. The two members in both peer over both hubs, with a peer account on each hub's ledger; the others over the one hub they share with each, and the two members in one hub alone, which share none, do not peer. Each funds a hold toward each peer in each hub they share before the payment, which can take a member that peered before its deposit opened its account a minute longer: its peers learn of the account when they next ask the hub. A client of the member in the XRP hub alone pays a receiver in DSUSD of the member in the DSUSD hub alone, through a member of both, which converts; what arrived is what was sent at the dev rate, moved by the member's inventory curve and less the connectors' fees and spread ([passive-rebalancing](../decisions/passive-rebalancing.md)), and each node's books hold what its accounts hold, each hub's with its peers' holds toward it as the node reads them from the hub. The payer's owner paid in XRP, the payee's got dollars, and the owners of the members of both took XRP in one hub and paid dollars in the other, each hub taken with its owner book; what their accounts hold may not move, since the payment may take no more from the holds toward the payee than they held. No payout in DSUSD is tested, since the payout set's change of signer list needs more XRP than the least gas leaves the escrow ([Token hubs](../roadmap.md#token-hubs)).

## Installer and updater

The install tests run the installer and the updater in containers with systemd as PID 1, against a mirror of signed test releases; [Release](release.md#tests) states what they check. Two rules for changing them:

- **Between them, the images must take every path the installer has:** both verifiers and both downloaders. Each Dockerfile (`tests/e2e/install/`) says which path it takes; a change to an image must keep every path covered.
- **The installer runs under `umask 077`,** as on a hardened host, so what it installs must still be readable by the node's own user.
