# Settlement hubs

What is built of [settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md): the hub's rules and its ledger, the deterministic function that takes a hub from one block to the next, Simplex consensus that orders its log, its escrow on the XRP Ledger, a member's side: its account in the hub and the `hub-xrp-testnet` scheme on it, and the validator role in the node ([below](#the-validator-in-the-node)). The node runs both: a member's side for a peering over a hub ([below](#in-the-node)), and a validator for each hub its owner has it validate.

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-hub/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/hub/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/hubs.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/hub_funding.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/hub_moves.rs`, `proto/dassie/ui/v1/hub_member.proto`
**Read with:** [Settlement](settlement.md), [Encoding](encoding.md), [Identity](identity.md), [XRPL](xrpl.md), and for `member/` [Connector](connector.md#responses-and-expiry) and [Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#message-set)

## The rules

A hub is named by the digest of its rules: the sponsor's terms and one signed acceptance per validator (`rules.rs`). An acceptance names the terms by their digest and no other validator, so a validator that drops out before genesis is replaced without touching the others' ([settlement-hubs](../decisions/settlement-hubs.md)).

`Terms::check` refuses terms whose values contradict what the hub's safety rests on: an account reserve too small to pay its account out at shutdown, fewer places for missing claims than a payee lets its payer leave missing, a hold notice no longer than a packet may be held, no places kept for deadlines, a vote window shorter than a validator's turn, blocks or claims larger than a follower decodes. The values themselves are starting points for the simulation.

## Digests and signatures

Everything is named by SHA-256 under a domain tag of its own (`digest.rs`), and every signature signs such a digest, so a signature made for one purpose never verifies for another. A member's operation is signed over the hub ID, its account number, the account's next sequence and the most fee it pays; a validator's over the hub ID, its index and a sequence that only grows; a payer's commitment and claim over the hub ID and the hold. Account and hold numbers are never reused, so nothing signed can be replayed in another hub, or twice in one. Weak Ed25519 keys are refused wherever a key is decoded, since under ZIP-215 they verify forgeries ([Identity](identity.md#node-key)).

All wire types are canonical OER with fixed-width integers ([Encoding](encoding.md)).

## The ledger

The ledger (`ledger/`) is a pure function of the blocks it executes: time is the block's timestamp, and nothing reads a clock or an RNG, so every validator that executes the same blocks reaches the same state, which the state's Merkle root lets anyone check.

- **Every drop is in exactly one place.** After every block the ledger asserts that what the escrow holds for the hub equals everything the hub owes: balances and reserves, holds and their reserves, pending withdrawals with their ledger fees, fees waiting for their voters, and the reserve account. That sum is the supply each validator publishes.
- **Bounded tables.** Accounts, holds, holds per account, withdrawals, pending and remembered deposits, claimed sequences per hold and operations per block each have a bound in the terms. An operation that would pass one fails; it never grows a table.
- **Refused or failed.** An operation that is not its signer's to make (unknown account, bad signature, wrong sequence) or cannot pay its fee is refused: it pays nothing and uses no sequence. One that is valid but cannot take effect fails: it pays its fee and uses its sequence, as a `tec` result does on the XRP Ledger. A failure changes nothing else.
- **Deadlines first.** A block keeps places for `redeem` and `claim`, which go first; a block that breaks the order or the bounds is not executed, and validators do not vote for it.
- **Each account counts the owner's moves:** what deposits credited it, opening it included, what its withdrawals and its payout took, ledger fees included, and, apart, what withdrawals the escrow could not pay brought back, so a deposit is told by its own counter. All three are in the account's state, so in the state root, and only grow. Deposits are credited by validators' attestations, which no member's operation shows, so the counters are how a member tells them from anything else that changed its balance ([below](#the-members-side)).
- **New accounts set a disbursal address first,** so every account the hub pays out at shutdown has somewhere to go. Validators' accounts, which receive their fees, are exempt and lock no reserve. A closing account does nothing more: it waits for its payout.
- **Nothing a block holds can stop the ledger.** A panic would happen in every validator at the same block, and the hub could never execute it, so whatever members and validators can put in a block is refused or fails before it reaches an assertion: terms that would let the ledger reach a state it cannot leave are refused by `Terms::check`, and new rules never shrink a table.

## Holds

A payee takes from a hold only what its payer signed (`ledger/holds.rs`):

- `redeem` pays what the payer's claim adds to the last redeemed total, less what `claim` already paid for sequences up to the claim's W. Totals and W only grow.
- `claim` pays commitments above the last redeemed W whose fulfillments hash to their conditions, in a block stamped before their expiry. A hold remembers each sequence claimed until a redeem passes it, so no packet is paid twice. A claim is all or nothing.
- A payer's reduction takes effect at the first block stamped after the hold's notice; a hold reduced to nothing closes and frees its reserve.
- A payee may release a hold toward it, which goes back to its payer at once. Only the holds an account pays into count toward its limit per account: anyone can open a hold toward an account, and it can release them, so no one can use up another's room.

## What validators attest

Deposits, withdrawal outcomes, the escrow's setup and resets of the test network each take effect once a quorum of validators attested the same thing, so up to f faulty validators can neither make one up nor hold one back (`ledger/escrow.rs`, `ledger/validator.rs`).

- **A reset counts validators.** Honest validators see a new chain at different ledgers, so a reset takes effect once a quorum of validators attested one, whatever ledger each names; each votes once. So that no vote outlives its reset, the hub takes no attestation of a reset for a quiet period after a write-off (`RESET_QUIET_MS`, well above the time an online validator takes to see a new chain and get an operation into a block, and far below the time between resets): otherwise up to f honest votes arriving late, with f faulty ones and one validator a lying server fooled, would make a quorum for a second write-off. Votes that reach no quorum within the same period lapse, so stray votes months apart, each a faulty validator's or one fooled by a lying server, never add up.

- **A deposit is credited once.** The hub remembers credited transactions back to a floor that rises as its table fills, and a deposit below the floor that is not pending already is not taken: it may have been credited and forgotten. One that is pending was never credited, and is attested whatever its ledger.
- **Each validator has its own share of the pending deposits,** so a faulty one that attests made-up deposits fills only its own share, and honest attestations still fit.
- **Deposits wait until a quorum attested the escrow,** at genesis and again after a reset.
- **A change of validators moves attestations to their validators' new indices** and drops those of validators that left, so they never count toward the new set's quorum; a withdrawal or deposit whose attestations make a quorum of the new set is settled at the end of the block. Votes start again. A pending deposit below the floor that no one attests any more is kept, so it can still be credited; such entries count toward the table, and each removed faulty validator can leave up to its share of them behind. A validator may change its vote for new rules. When the consensus or escrow keys change, the new set keeps the escrow's next sequence for the old set's change of the signer list, and no new rules are taken until it is made.
- **Validators' accounts are outside the bound on accounts,** which counts only accounts opened as members', so a change of validators never finds the table full; an account a validator joins with stays open, a payout that would have closed it a plain withdrawal.

A reset writes off every balance, reserve, hold and pending payment, and keeps the accounts, which lock their reserve again from what they next receive. Only the testnet's network ID allows it ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)).

## Consensus

`consensus/` is Simplex (Chan and Pass), without IO and generic over a block's payload, which the ledger fills and checks through the `Application` hooks. Commonware's implementation is the reference and the source of its scenarios; nothing depends on it.

- **What an honest validator signs.** It notarizes at most one block per view, and never one it nullified; it nullifies only its current view; it votes to finalize a block only if it neither nullified that view nor notarized another block in it, and only one block per height. So two conflicting votes, two certificates of one view, or two finalized blocks at one height name only validators that broke these rules: the evidence never blames an honest one.
- **Never two conflicting votes, even across a crash.** Before it sends a step's messages, the host persists the step's batch: the view, every vote it signed above its last finalized block, the heights it voted to finalize, the blocks it holds above it and the certificates it holds, and whether it declared the hub stalled. Certificates are kept so that a hub whose validators all restart at once can still skip the views they had nullified. A validator that declared a stall never votes again.
- **Timing follows from the region.** Every duration derives from Δ, the region's one-way delay (`consensus/config.rs`): a timestamp is timely within twice Δ of a validator's clock, a block is proposed every interval and final three delays later, a silent leader's view is skipped after the interval and two delays. The hub's margin M is the precision, f skipped views, an interval and three delays. The values are never tuned for the simulation's sake.
- **Leaders** take turns by view, view mod n of the set running the epoch.
- **Epochs.** Each validator set runs an epoch of its own, numbered, with views from 1, and every block, vote and certificate names its epoch, which a vote's signature covers. Once the last block of an epoch (the one before the height a change takes over at) is final, the next epoch runs from view 1 and its first block builds on that block at view 0: every validator hands over at the same place, whichever certificate told it the block was final ([epoch-handover](../decisions/epoch-handover.md)).
- **The end of an epoch is a property of the chain.** The block that schedules a change may be notarized and not final while blocks after it are proposed, so a validator reads where the running epoch ends from the chain a block builds on: the change the application's state after the block's parent names (`Application::scheduled_change`), with any change final blocks scheduled. So no block of the old set at or above the boundary is notarized on any chain, whatever each validator has finalized.
- **The application's states.** The application keeps the state after a block it found valid, final or not, for as long as it likes, and says whether it still holds it (`Application::holds`); the state after the last final block it always holds. It lets go of a state only while it verifies or finalizes a block, and never of the one it just made, so a state it holds stays held at least until the next call to verify or finalize: the engine verifies a block, asks `scheduled_change` about the state after it, and then has the application verify or propose a block on it. Before the engine judges or builds a block, it has the application verify, in height order and from the payloads it holds, every block between the nearest ancestor whose state the application still holds (or the last final block) and the parent: a block whose state was let go of counts as not verified. `scheduled_change` is asked only about blocks whose state is held, so its "no change" never stands for "unknown". A block whose payload the engine no longer holds cannot be built on until it is fetched again, as any missing block. Verifying again costs at most the chain that waits to be final per view.
- **A repeat at the end of an epoch.** While an epoch's last block is notarized and not final, the epoch's next leader proposes a repeat of it: the same height, the block as its parent, built on through the view that notarized the block or a repeat of it, no payload. Only the old set makes one, only of its epoch's last block on the chain it builds on (or of the highest block that may wait to be final, below), and the application never sees it; finalizing the repeat finalizes the block. Without it, votes split by a partition (some to finalize the block, some to nullify its view, neither a quorum) would halt the hub for good, since nothing of the old set may build on its last block. The rule that two certificates of one height are evidence counts a repeat as the block it repeats. Repeats never build on each other.
- **Finality before the boundary is judged.** A finalization proves its block and every ancestor final, whatever the block is, so a chain is finalized content first, in order: a block in it may schedule the change that ends the epoch, and the repeats that follow it are only the proof. A chain stops where its epoch ended, instead of asserting it cannot.
- **Finality returns after any outage, in bounded memory** ([finality-after-any-outage](../decisions/finality-after-any-outage.md)). An engine keeps the views from the highest one that notarized a block that may extend its last final block, their blocks, the chain from the last final block to that block, and the views below that certified a block of the chain, persisted with the rest; everything below goes, and nothing it keeps is dropped while it is needed. A validator behind on the chain asks for what it lacks by the view its child was built on through, a peer hands it the chain from there down, as much as a delivery holds, and it takes that below its floor, as it takes a finalization of a block of its chain there; a proposal above the heights it lets wait makes it ask for what others finalized. No block is notarized more than `MAX_UNFINALIZED_HEIGHTS` above the last final block: past it, leaders repeat the highest block, and finalizing the repeat finalizes the chain. Views that fail in a row, past `Config::backoff_after_views` of those held, each wait twice as long as the one before, up to the longest view (`MAX_VIEW_TIMEOUT_MS`, half a minute): in a long outage, a view led by a live validator comes only twice a minute (those of up to f faulty leaders end at once), and once the network heals, blocks are final again within about one longest view, not after as long as the outage was. So the stall in the rules lies between twice the recovery from the longest outage, f + 1 of the longest views for the largest set (`MIN_STALL_MS`), and the outage whose views an engine can still hold, at n / (n − f) views per longest view (`MAX_STALL_MS`, with `MAX_ROUNDS`); `Terms::check` refuses any other.
- **Catching up, epoch by epoch.** Peers keep the recent finalized blocks with their certificates (`RETAINED_BLOCKS`), and the last block of each of the last few epochs (`MAX_BOUNDARIES`) with what proves it: its own finalization, or a repeat's. A delivery never crosses the end of an epoch, so a validator finalizes the block that scheduled a change, and learns the next set, before it sees anything that set signed; a delivery may end in a repeat, which the asker takes once it has applied the blocks before it, and ends there. A validator further behind than peers keep is handed the last block of its own epoch, or a later one its set signed; one behind a run finalized at once that does not fit in a delivery is handed the run's last block. A finalization is always checked with a set the engine already knows, and a set reaches it only from data such a set signed: the ledger's report after a block it finalized, or the state after a checked block.
- **The host's contract for a validator far behind.** `Event::Behind(F)` carries a block F already checked with a known set. The host loads the state after F from a validator or member and checks it against the state root F carries, then calls `Engine::jump(F, change)`, where `change` is the change of validators that state has scheduled and not yet made, if any. `jump` checks F again before anything changes. `Engine::reconfigure` is only for a change read from the state after a block the engine finalized.
- **Room for blocks, per view.** A view holds its leader's first proposal and only blocks a verified certificate names beside it, and only blocks whose view is held and whose height one block per view since the last final block allows; below the views held, only a block the chain to the highest notarized one lacks, with the certificate that names it. So a faulty leader fills only the room of the views it leads, and can never crowd out an honest block.
- **Changes of validators are in the chain.** New rules that fell due in a block take effect two blocks later: the ledger schedules them for that height, and after each final block the engine asks the ledger for a scheduled change and takes it in before it finalizes the next. So the ledger and consensus hand over at the same height, every validator alike however far behind, and no block records votes from before the set in force.
- **A certificate it cannot check** never steers the engine: a message of a later epoch makes it ask a validator of its own set for what it lacks, never the message's sender.

The `hub` simulation runs 4 to 7 validators over a network with loss, duplication, slow links, partitions, crashes and clock skew, with up to f faulty ones (silent, withholding, proposing invalid payloads, equivocating, flooding proposals, proposing to too few to notarize) or, one run in ten, f + 1 colluding, and applications that keep only a few states. In a third of the runs, one more validator follows the hub and replaces another at a height the chain schedules, while validators that fall behind catch up across the change, loading states that a peer hands them and the block's root checks. Half of those runs split the network just before the boundary, some with the least notice the ledger gives, and some lose every vote to finalize the epoch's last block itself, so that only a repeat finalizes it; one run in six loses every vote to finalize for up to a minute, longer than the chain an engine lets wait to be final: knobs for coverage of the handover and of finality after an outage, not production values. It checks that honest validators never finalize different blocks at one height, that a fork always leaves evidence and that evidence never names an honest validator, that every honest validator past a change runs the new set, and that blocks keep becoming final within production timings once the network heals.

## The escrow

`escrow/` holds what the hub does on the XRP Ledger, as functions of the rules and the ledger's state, so every validator builds the same transaction from the same block and signs it alone.

- **Keys** come from the node key under `node/hub/<nonce>/…`: a validator's consensus key, escrow key and BIP 32 payout key, and the sponsor's escrow master key. The master key is the sponsor's to keep: the same key makes the same escrow account again after a reset of the test network ([Identity](identity.md#seed-paths)).
- **Setup and check.** The sponsor funds the escrow, then sets its signer list, `RequireDest`, and disables its master key last. The validators take an escrow only with the master key disabled, no regular key, `RequireDest`, the signer list exactly the rules' (each escrow key weight 1, quorum q) and no other objects, all as of a validated ledger, the objects as of that ledger or later: before it, the master key could still have made one.
- **Nothing one party does can stop the escrow's sequence.** A transaction the XRP Ledger refuses as malformed never uses its sequence, and every escrow transaction after it would wait for ever. So `Rules::check` takes only acceptances whose escrow key is a key of the XRP Ledger and not the escrow's own (a signer list cannot name its account), and whose payout key is a secp256k1 point; and the ledger refuses the escrow itself and the zero account as a withdrawal's, a close's or a disbursal's destination.
- **What validators attest** from the escrow's transactions: a deposit is a successful Payment of XRP to the escrow with a destination tag, for what it delivered; for tag 0 the memo names the new account's key, and anything else attests no key, so the hub pays it back. An escrow transaction's outcome is its sequence and whether it succeeded or failed with a `tec`. The old validators' change of the signer list, once a quorum saw it apply, frees its kept sequence, and the reserve account pays its fee. A withdrawal's outcome counts only if the transaction at its sequence is its own Payment, delivering its amount.
- **The payout set** of a final block: in escrow sequence order with no gap, the withdrawals not yet settled and a pending change of signer list, as already signed; the selector, a signer list of every validator's payout key's child at the block's height (two 31-bit levels); and one payout per account with a disbursal address whose balance, reserves and the holds it paid into, less the fee, reach the least payout. Withdrawals and the selector are signed with escrow keys, payouts with the set's own keys. The first selector the XRP Ledger applies decides which set pays: every other set's payouts are refused for keys not on the list (`tefBAD_SIGNATURE`), without using a sequence.
- **The fee** of every escrow transaction is the rules' ledger fee, which must cover a quorum's signatures.

## The validator in the node

The node runs a validator for each hub its owner has it validate (`crates/dassie-node/src/hub/`), as a supervised service of its own, once it has its key.

- **Opt-in, per sponsor.** Nothing is validated unless the owner names a file in `DASSIE_VALIDATOR_CONFIG` ([Node](node.md#configuration)): the sponsors it validates for, and each hub's genesis rules and genesis timestamp, which every validator starts from alike, since the genesis block's digest covers it. A hub whose sponsor is not listed, or whose rules do not check, stops the daemon at start; one whose rules name no acceptance of this node is left out and said so in the log. A node validates at most a few hubs, its own limit ([settlement-hubs](../decisions/settlement-hubs.md)).
- **Persisted before it acts.** Every input is a step: a message, the time, or what the node saw of the escrow. The step's consensus batches are folded into the stored consensus state and written, with the ledger's snapshot after a block that became final, in one transaction, on a blocking task off the async runtime, before any of the step's messages go out. So a validator never sends a vote it could forget in a crash, and the two it starts from are always of the same block: the snapshot is restored against the root the stored tip names, and `Validator::resume` refuses them otherwise. A validator that comes back knows no history from before: it answers `Since` from further back with `Trimmed`, and members read their state anew ([below](#when-the-history-is-gone)). A write that fails ends the step with nothing sent, and the service starts again from the disk.
- **Request and reply only.** Validators and members exchange the hub's messages on the hub's stream ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#the-hubs-stream)). The reply to a message is what the validator sent back while it handled it: the statements a member asked for, or a consensus answer to another validator's fetch. Nothing is pushed: a validator's notice to a member it is not answering goes nowhere, so a member behind a network that takes no connections is served as any other, and asks. A validator sends to the others as exchanges of their own, over QUIC alone, and takes their replies as messages from them; one it could not reach is left alone for a while that grows with each failure, and consensus sends again what matters.
- **Catching up from a peer's state.** It also keeps the states after a few recent final blocks and after the last blocks of the recent epochs, as many as the engine keeps those blocks, written in the steps that made them. A validator further behind than its peers keep blocks learns a final block from them (`Behind`): their tip, or the last block of its epoch. It asks the validators of the sets it knows, one after another, for the state after that block on the hub's state stream ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#the-hubs-stream)), which serves the hub's validators only, and hands it to `Validator::catch_up`, which takes it only if it is the state the block names. That block is unverified until its state is loaded, so one fetch runs at a time, and fetches are spaced.
- **Its own operations** are attested again later when its pool has no room for them, a bounded number.
- **Following the escrow** on its own XRPL server, over a WebSocket of its own (`hub/escrow.rs` decides, without IO): it checks that the server is on the hub's network and where its ledger stands against the newest seen ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)), subscribes to the escrow, and reads the escrow's history from the last ledger it attested, a window back, since an attestation lost in a crash must go again and the hub refuses one sent twice. Each validated transaction becomes an attestation as [What validators attest](#what-validators-attest) reads it, remembered, and passed by the cursor, only once the validator took it: one its queue has no room for waits, in order, and is not attested twice; an outcome at an escrow sequence the hub has not given out yet, as this validator executed, waits until it has. While no quorum checked the escrow, at genesis and after a reset, it checks it again and again, and attests its next sequence once it passes. A reset of the test network is attested once, naming the ledger of the new chain it was seen at, and not at all if the hub wrote one off since that ledger closed.
- **What it submits and signs.** It submits each escrow transaction a quorum signed to its server, queueing them while it is not connected. A payout set it signs is kept, its signatures as the hub's `Share` messages, the newest few per hub, and logged; members cannot fetch them yet.
- **The owner sees** each hub's validator in `GetStatus` and `dassie status`: which validator of how many, the last final block and view, the hub's phase, whether it declared a stall, whether a quorum checked the escrow and whether it follows it now, with a hint only where something is wrong.

**Not built yet:** the genesis certificate, and accepting a sponsor's terms through the node; members fetching payout bundles; and gathering signatures under the old escrow keys after a change of escrow keys: the withdrawals given a sequence before the change, and the old set's change of the signer list, need the old set's escrow keys, and nothing gathers them ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#settlement-hubs)).

## Fees

A share of every fee goes to the reserve account; the rest waits in the pool of its block until the vote window has passed, and is then split equally among the validators whose votes for that block later blocks recorded (`ledger/fees.rs`). A block records each vote once, and only for blocks within the window. The time a validator's vote was last recorded also decides absence: a validator unrecorded for longer than the terms allow shuts the hub down.

## The validator's service

`service/` is one validator, without IO: consensus and the ledger together (`Validator`). A block's payload is the operations its mempool holds, deadlines first, and the votes for earlier blocks it recorded; a block is verified by executing it on a copy of the state and comparing the root. Members talk to it in self-authenticating messages (`client.rs`): requests, and statements the validator signs, which a member trusts once f + 1 validators signed them alike.

- **Whatever its pool keeps pays when a block takes it, or proves nothing, and is gone after the next final block** (`service/mempool.rs`). A member's operation is taken only if the account can pay its fee and those of everything it already has waiting, and per account only the run of consecutive sequences from its current one is ever selected; an operation past a gap waits in a small set of its own and goes first when the pool is full. Each validator has a bounded share of the pool, and its own operations take at most an equal part of half the places a block does not keep for deadlines, so no validator can fill the blocks. Deadlines have a part of the pool ordinary operations never take, as they have places of their own in every block. After each final block the pool drops what the block refused and what the new state makes impossible, and after a change of validators what no longer verifies.
- **A leader's block always fits.** It fills a block by bytes as well as by count, below the payload's bound by a margin (`payload::OPERATION_BYTES`), and the pool takes no operation one block could not hold, nor a claim with more commitments than the rules allow. Otherwise every leader would build the same block too large to propose, and the hub would stall.
- **Catch-up is for validators.** A delivery can be as large as a consensus message, so the service serves catch-up only to a sender the transport authenticated as the node of a validator in the set in force or the one scheduled; a member reads the state through requests. A message's content takes the largest consensus message (`wire::MAX_CONTENT`), and nothing on the way out is encoded past its bound: statements are split across messages by count and bytes.
- **Shares of an escrow signature come only from escrow keys.** It signs each withdrawal the log gave a sequence and passes its signature to the other validators; it counts a signature only if its key is the escrow key of a validator of the set in force, once per validator, and submits once it has a quorum. Anyone can sign a payment with keys of their own, and a quorum of such signatures would make it submit a transaction the XRP Ledger refuses.
- **A member's tally of statements is bounded per validator** (`client::Agreement`), so no validator can push out another's signatures: each has a bounded number of signatures, and of bytes of statements (`MAX_SIGNER_BYTES`), still waiting for f + 1, and a signature past either is refused rather than any dropped: the lowest are the ones to agree first, after which it has room again. A statement further above the height f + 1 validators have reached than any honest one could be is refused. What is dropped or refused of a statement still waiting is reported, so the member reads that part again rather than count an answer it no longer holds whole.
- **What it answers, to anyone.** It takes operations to submit, and answers for an account or a hold by key or number, for what touched an account since a height (`Since`), and for an account and every hold it pays into or takes from (`Holds`), each as of a final block. `Holds` lists every hold, over as many pages as they take, each a statement of its own, so f + 1 alike vouch for the whole list; the ledger keeps its holds indexed by account, so the answer looks at the account's holds only. Requests carry no authentication, since a member need not be known to the validators and the answers prove themselves, so anyone can read any account and every operation that touched it, claims with their fulfillments included.
- **Subscriptions are bounded.** A `Since` also subscribes its sender to pushes for that account, in place of what it subscribed to before; a validator keeps a few subscriptions per account and a bounded number in all, and a new one pushes out the oldest, so neither reconnects nor anyone subscribing to accounts not their own lock members out.
- **The contract it keeps for `Since`:** every member operation that touches the account from the height asked (its own, a transfer, hold or close toward it, and any operation on a hold it pays into or takes from), in the log's order, each named by its height and its place in its block, then the newest final block it covered. Deposits credited and withdrawals returned show in the account's state, not in `Since`. An answer that grows long, by count or by bytes (`MAX_ANSWER_BYTES`), stops before a block, but always carries at least one block whole, over as many messages as it takes, so a block that touches an account more often than one answer holds never stalls the member. A `Final` is its word that it sent everything up to it. What a block touched is worked out from the state before it, since an operation can change whose a hold is.
- **Its history is bounded,** by blocks and by bytes: it keeps the recent blocks that touched any account (`KEPT_BLOCKS`, `KEPT_BYTES`), and answers a `Since` from further back with `Trimmed`, since it can no longer vouch for what happened there. A member then reads its state anew. A final block that leaves before it was kept, as when more become final in one step than the chain holds, raises that height too: nothing is dropped silently. The states kept for blocks not yet final are copies of the ledger, at most `MAX_EXECUTED` of them; while finality stalls the lowest go first, and the engine verifies such a block again from the nearest state still held before it relies on it.
- **A final block it cannot follow stops it.** A block finalized while it was behind is executed from the final state; one that does not decode or execute as certified means more than f validators are faulty, or a bug. The validator then follows no further block, never votes again and signs the payout set of the last block it executed, as at a stall, rather than crash.
- **What it pushes vouches for nothing:** statements it sends unasked carry no `Final`, so they never count toward what a member read up to a height: a member that missed a push would otherwise count a later one over what it missed.
- **The states it keeps.** The service keeps the state after each block it found valid, at most `MAX_EXECUTED` of them, letting go of the lowest first and never of the one just verified, and says which it holds (`holds`): the engine verifies again, from the nearest state held, a block whose state it let go of. It tells consensus the change of validators the state after a held block names (`scheduled_change`), which is what the final state reports once that block is final.
- **A snapshot of the ledger** (`ledger/snapshot.rs`) is its whole state as bytes, with one encoding per state: every table in strictly ascending order, and what is derived from the tables, such as the index of keys, rebuilt rather than read. `Ledger::restore` takes the root the state must have, the one a certified block carries, and refuses a snapshot whose root differs, so no caller can rely on a state before its root is checked.
- **A validator further behind than peers keep blocks** is told so (`Action::Behind`, with a final block its set certified): its host fetches the state after that block from a validator, and `Validator::catch_up` takes it only if it is the state the block's root names, at its height. The engine jumps to the block, taking in the change of validators that state has scheduled, and the chain goes on from it. It vouches for no history below the block, so a member asking from further back is told to read its state anew.

## The member's side

`member/` is a member's side of a hub, without IO: its account as an [external asset](settlement.md#external-assets) and the `hub-xrp-testnet` settlement scheme on it, which the node books as every scheme ([Settlement](settlement.md#settlement-hubs)).

**What the account trusts, and when it reads.** The account's ledger is the hub's validators, each at its endpoint. It trusts a statement once f + 1 validators signed it alike: one is honest. It reads the operations that touch it in the order of the log, each once, and only up to a height a quorum covered:

- **The contract a validator keeps** ([the validator's service](#the-validators-service)): its answer to `Since` lists every executed operation that touches the account from the height asked, in the log's order, each named by its height and place in its block, and ends with the newest final block it covered; an answer that grows long, by count or by bytes, stops before a block, but always carries one block whole, over as many messages as it takes. A `Final` is a validator's word that it sent everything up to it; what it pushes unasked carries none.
- **Coverage counts from the validator's own endpoint only.** A `Final` names no account, so one passed on from another member's answer would vouch for nothing; the node's driver authenticates each endpoint.
- **Why a quorum.** Once 2f + 1 validators covered a height, f + 1 honest ones sent every operation up to it, so each is agreed, and the account reads them, in order, up to that height. A faulty validator can neither make one up nor hide one. A payer decides a packet without a fulfillment only from a final block up to which it read everything ([below](#packets-promises-and-claims)).
- **Coverage counts only what the account still holds.** A validator's `Final` covers only if the account dropped none of what that validator sent since its last `Final`; when the tally refuses one of its signatures, or the account clears what it agreed above its cursor because it holds too much, the validator's coverage falls back below what was dropped, and the `Final` it is sending covers nothing, so the account asks again. The tally gives each validator room for a buffer's worth of statements and a message more, and bytes for two of the longest answers to `Since` and one to `Holds` (`MAX_SIGNER_BYTES`), each bounded however many messages it spans, so an honest answer always fits, and what one validator signs never takes another's room. So a tally holds at most n times `MAX_SIGNER_BYTES` of statements still waiting; those that agreed, at most a bounded number, f + 1 validators signed, among them an honest one. Within a block, operations are read by their place in it, so one a faulty leader put in a block twice is read twice, as the log has it.
- **Its own state** is told to the scheme only as of a block through which it read everything, and nothing touching it since, so the scheme checks its books against it without counting anything twice.
- **What it mirrors.** Its own holds, and those toward it from its peers, which the scheme names to it, at most two from each (the one it follows, and the next once that closes) and a bounded number in all. Anyone may open a hold toward an account for a hold reserve, so holds from others wait in a small pool of their own whose oldest go first: they never take the place of its own or its peers', and a peering made just after its peer opened its hold still finds it.

**What it submits.** One operation at a time, to every validator, again until one executes: claims and redeems before what waits, never before what is on its way. A settlement is sent only once the node executed it. An operation is signed with the account's sequence when it is sent; refused for its sequence, it is signed again with the one the account had as of that block or later. A sequence only ever goes up, so the refused one can never apply later. A new account sets its disbursal address before anything else, as the ledger requires.

**One scheme per account.** The node writes the two in one batch, and the scheme refuses a state that is not in step with its account's. When the scheme stops, the account reads nothing more until a restart, so neither misses what the other read.

**The owner's deposits and withdrawals.** The scheme books, between two states of the account it matches its books to, how far the account's counters grew ([above](#the-ledger)): what deposits credited and what withdrawals that came back brought as the owner's deposits, what withdrawals took as the owner's withdrawal, and only then reconciles the rest through suspense, as the reserve's when the account reserve changed. The ledger fee of a withdrawal of ours is a fee: booked when the hub executed it, and kept back from the growth of the withdrawals counter that shows it, with the scheme's state, so a restart in between books it once. One whose execution the account never read in order (it read its state anew) is booked whole as the owner's withdrawal. The first sync books the whole balance as the owner's deposit, as for any scheme, and takes the counters as they are then; the scheme keeps the last counters it matched with its state, so a restart books nothing twice. The owner's withdrawal is an operation of ours like any other (`pay_owner`): one at a time, at least the hub's least payout, never to the escrow or the zero account, within what the account can spend after what it has queued; its fee and the ledger's are booked once it executed, and what it paid out from the counters. The scheme answers for it by an ID of its own, with what became of it, kept with its state.

### When the history is gone

A validator keeps a bounded history, and answers a `Since` from further back than it keeps with `Trimmed`: it can no longer vouch for what touched the account there. Once f + 1 agree, the account reads its state anew (`Holds`): its own account and every hold it pays into or takes from, each as of one block, which f + 1 sign alike page by page; it takes the list only once every page of it is in, so the list is complete. It takes them as of that block, moves its cursor there, and reads in order from it. What happened in between is known only by its difference (`member/resync.rs`):

- **A hold toward us from a peer** grows only by its payer's funds, and a take moves what it holds to what was taken, so what the two grew by is the payer's settlements, booked as theirs. A hold a peer opened in between is followed from then on, once an account the validators agree on confirms the key the peer gave at peering.
- **Our hold toward a peer:** what the payee took in between beyond what we owed it and it had not taken is what the hub paid with claims the account never read: a loss on the peer (`PaidLate`), and part of T from then on, since the hub overrules our books.
- **Our operation that used its sequence in between:** a settlement is taken as applied when the hold shows its funds, and cancelled otherwise; a claim of ours is made again.
- **Packets waiting for the hub** are decided by the block read anew as by any final block: without a fulfillment once it is stamped at or after their commitment's expiry and no claim for them was read, since a claim the account never read gives no fulfillment to pass upstream. What the hub paid for them is in the loss above.

The account's state as of the block reconciles the rest, the fees in between among it, through suspense. What `claim` took above a hold's redeemed W does not show in its view, so these differences err toward booking less as a settlement or a loss, and more through suspense.

### Packets, promises and claims

- **Amounts.** A commitment promises its packet in drops rounded up, so it never covers less than the packet. T is what the packets up to W add up to in internal units, rounded down to drops: what is less than a drop stays owed until there is more.
- **T covers W and nothing above.** The hub subtracts what `claim` paid from a redeem only up to its W, and remembers the rest, so a T that counted a packet above W would pay it twice. T counts every packet the hub paid with a claim, whatever the payer's books said: the hub overrules them.
- **Each hold has records of its own.** A payee may release the hold toward it at any time, and the next settlement opens another. The hub starts every hold at a total of nothing, so once a hold closed, released or reduced to nothing, the payer's T, W and sequences and the payee's record of what it took start anew with the next one: a T carried over would have a redeem pay it again. The next hold from the same peer is followed as the first was.
- **The payee takes a commitment** only once an operation of the payer's on the hold that the hub executed verified under the key the payer gave at peering: the hub checks claims with the account's own key. It takes the packet if the hold covers it beyond what the payee has not taken, what is in flight, and a reduction due before the commitment expires.
- **Peering again as another member waits.** A peer that comes back with another account, or another key for its account, is refused while anything of its old membership is unsettled: a hold with funds either way, a packet of ours in flight or waiting for the hub, or one of its in flight or fulfilled that no claim covers yet. Otherwise it would leave behind what the payee fulfilled and never claimed, or have the payee take promises under a key the hub refuses. Once settled, the new membership starts with records of its own and a key the hub has yet to confirm.
- **The payee takes a claim** only if its T is what the payee fulfilled up to its W by its own record, and no packet up to W is still in flight: a redeem through such a packet would forbid claiming it on the hub. A claim that leaves out packets whose commitments expired before the payee could claim them is still taken: only the payer can pay those now, and the claim is worth more than none.
- **Missing claims.** A payer's claim for a packet is late a reply leg (the rules' allowance) after every packet below it was resolved, since only then can the payer cover it; and a margin before the commitment expires in any case, so the claim lands in a block stamped before. A claim the hub refused is tried again with each packet alone, a bounded number of times, each try costing a fee. Past 1024 packets its claims left uncovered, the payee takes no more of the payer's Prepares.
- **Redeeming** waits until the claims it took add two largest packets to what it took (`policy::redeem_threshold`): each redeem costs a fee, and what is not taken is lost if the hub fails.
- **The payer's waits.** A packet whose Reject upstream waits for the hub is decided at once by a claim the hub paid for it, and otherwise without a fulfillment once a final block, up to which the account read everything, is stamped at or after its commitment's expiry.

**What a peering costs beyond its hold** is booked as a loss on the peer (`Effect::PeerLoss`), as the design lists it ([Settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#13-how-it-fits-the-node)): the fee of every claim the payee had to make; a packet the hub paid that the payer had rejected upstream or forgotten; and a packet the payee had rejected and then claimed, which no honest payee does, so it also counts against the peering. Each loss is named by the operation that caused it, its cause, the hold and the packet's sequence on it, so the journal, which books a name once, books every packet's loss, however many one claim paid. The node journals each as a `loss` keyed by the scheme and its ID on the hub, Dr `expenses/losses`, Cr `assets/settlement` for a claim's fee and the peer's `assets/interledger` for a packet paid, recorded whatever the owner's funds since the money is gone. A peer that claimed what it rejected loses the peering, collected first as any ending is; banning it stays the owner's word.

### In the node

The node runs a peering over a hub as any other, with these differences (`engine/core/hubs.rs`):

- **Joining.** The `hub-xrp-testnet` scheme's config holds the hub's rules; the node's account in it uses a key derived for that hub, and pays out at the hub's end to the node's own XRPL testnet account. A hub whose margin and allowance together exceed the connector's message window is refused: it is too far apart for its legs to fit the window, and a peering over it needs no window of its own ([Settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#4-peering-over-a-hub)).
- **Advertising and choosing.** Once the account has its number and the scheme runs, the node's link state names the hub (`HubMembership`), as the scheme reports it (`Commitments::membership`). A node asks a peer to peer over a hub only when both are members of the same hub and take peers there, and prefers it then; otherwise it picks the first scheme in common that is not over a hub, as before hubs. A hub scheme without a shared hub would leave each side without the other's account.
- **Credit is the hold.** A peering over a hub extends no unsecured credit, junior or senior, and earns no growth. The credit a node extends to a peer over a hub is its own hold toward the peer, its money set aside, and its settlements to the peer still on their way, which fund that hold once they execute; it starts at nothing. The node keeps it there with trust transfers before every commit, whatever changed the hold: a funding, a claim or redeem taking from it, a reduction taking effect, a release or close, as the scheme reports the holds (`Commitments::holds`). It lowers the credit only as far as the peer's account leaves room, never past what the peer may still owe it. The peer's hold toward us is not counted: its funding is booked already, as the peer's settlement to us, so the payee's room for the payer is the payer's hold less what the payee has not yet taken from it, as the design has it ([Settlement](settlement.md#settlement-hubs)).
- **Answering a request.** A payer over a hub asked to settle funds its hold toward the payee with what it would otherwise have paid: as a junior that extends the default credit would, counting its hold as what it paid in advance, whatever it owes. It raises its credit by that much before it books the settlement, so the books take it, and funds no more than its hub account can spend. One that can spend nothing answers that nothing is on its way, as before, and its books stay as they were.
- **The packet path.** A Prepare to a peer over a hub goes with the scheme's commitment; one the scheme will not commit to is not sent. A Prepare from such a peer goes on only if its commitment covers it, and is rejected with T04 otherwise, as one beyond a peer's credit is. Our Fulfill keeps its fulfillment in the scheme until a claim covers it; our reply to a peer's Fulfill carries our claim after the verdict. A packet that timed out waits for the hub ([Connector](connector.md)), and is settled by what the scheme decided.
- **Reaching the validators.** The account's requests are the hub's own wire messages; the engine's driver sends each to its validator on the hub's stream ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#the-hubs-stream)), over QUIC pinned to the node key the validator's acceptance names, or `POST /hub` when QUIC cannot reach it, and hands every message of the reply to the account as from that validator: coverage counts from each validator's own endpoint only. An exchange takes at most the account's interval for sending again, since its next request supersedes a later answer, and an account has at most a few exchanges in flight with each validator, so a slow validator holds up neither its other validators nor other accounts; what does not arrive the account asks again.
- **In tests and simulations,** peerings over a hub run over the stub with commitments, which keeps a member's books of its packets and holds without a hub ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-stub-scheme)): whatever becomes of a packet, sent, refused, failed, answered or decided, and while its peering is being ended too, the scheme lets go of it when the connector does, and the credit is what the holds call for after every step ([Testing](testing.md#the-simulations)).

It also moves the owner's funds between its XRPL testnet account and the hub, as an owner moves them between any two of its ledgers (`engine/core/hub_funding.rs`, `engine/core/hub_moves.rs`, [Settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#11-choosing-a-hub)):

- **Between a floor and a ceiling.** The node keeps what its hub account can spend above a floor, and what it keeps in the hub (the balance, its reserves, its holds toward its peers, which are the credit it extends them, and what the holds toward it hold, not taken yet) below a ceiling, both counted in largest packets ([Settlement](settlement.md#positions-and-thresholds)). The floor is, per peering over the hub, the most one request to settle has it fund (as answering a request does, above), so a hold is funded at once when asked. Below the floor, it deposits from its XRPL testnet account up to the midpoint, never past the ceiling; above the ceiling, it withdraws to that account down to the midpoint, never below the floor. Only peers' settlements grow the holds toward us, so only they take the hub past the ceiling, until the node takes them and withdraws. A deposit is the owner's withdrawal on `xrpl-testnet+xrp` once the XRP Ledger applied it, and the owner's deposit on the hub's ledger once the hub credited it; a withdrawal the other way round: each side books its half when it sees it ([XRPL](xrpl.md#deposits-and-withdrawals)).
- **One move at a time, never paid twice.** A move names the hub account's counter that shows it ([The ledger](#the-ledger)) and the value it takes it to: what deposits credited as agreed when it was made plus what a deposit pays, or what withdrawals took plus what a withdrawal takes with its ledger fee. It is done once an agreed state of the account reaches that value; failed only once the scheme of the side it leaves says its payment moved nothing (the XRP Ledger failed or never applied it, the hub refused or failed the withdrawal); and on its way otherwise, however long. The database keeps it with its payment's ID, which the scheme answers for across restarts, so a restart neither forgets a move nor pays it again. A move that failed holds the next back for a backoff that doubles. Nothing is moved while the XRPL account is not up to date with its ledger.
- **Late, never paid again.** A move still on its way a while (`CREDIT_WAIT_MS`) after the XRP Ledger applied it, or after it was made, is late: `GetHubStatus` says so, and the owner looks. A deposit the XRP Ledger applied and the hub never credits stays on its way: the node never pays it again by itself, nor moves anything else meanwhile, since what the hub does with it is not the node's to guess.
- **Giving up a late move** is the owner's (`HubGiveUp`, `dassie hub give-up`), and only once the move is late. The node forgets the move and books nothing: each side books its half when it sees it, so the XRP scheme booked a deposit as the owner's withdrawal when the XRP Ledger applied it, and if the hub credits it later anyway, its `deposited` counter grows and the scheme books it as the owner's deposit then, as any deposit; a withdrawal the hub executed is booked from its counter, and its arrival on the XRP Ledger when the XRPL account sees it. Nothing is booked twice, either way. The node's own moves then wait until the owner turns them on again or moves funds himself, so the node never pays again by itself what may yet arrive; meanwhile `GetHubStatus` names the given-up move and says the other side may still show it.
- **Joining by deposit.** While the hub says it has no account for the node's key, the first deposit opens it: a Payment to the escrow with tag 0 and the account key as its memo ([The escrow](#the-escrow)), of the account reserve, the floor and the starting balance the rules set for the first operations' fees. Later deposits name the account by its tag.
- **The owner's moves.** The owner sees the account, its holds toward and from its peers, the floor and the ceiling, the move on its way and how many validators answer, and moves funds either way, or opens the account, through `HubMemberService` (`proto/dassie/ui/v1/hub_member.proto`) or `dassie hub` ([Node](node.md#the-dassie-command)). An owner's move goes the same way as the node's, one at a time, and pauses the node's own moves for a while (`OWNER_PAUSE_MS`), so they do not undo it at once. The owner may turn the node's own moves off, which the database keeps.

**Not built yet:** validators pushing statements, which the account asks for in the meantime; closing by the owner; a floor and a ceiling the owner sets; joining by being paid; following a change of validators, since the account takes its validators from the rules it was built with; and a booking of their own for a hold's reduction or release, which the scheme reconciles through suspense meanwhile, so the credit follows such a hold down only as far as the books leave room.
