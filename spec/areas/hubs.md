# Settlement hubs

What is built of [settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md): the hub's rules and its ledger, the deterministic function that takes a hub from one block to the next, Simplex consensus that orders its log, and its escrow on the XRP Ledger. The member's scheme and the node's validator role are not built yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#settlement-hubs)); the design describes them.

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-hub/`
**Read with:** [Settlement](settlement.md), [Encoding](encoding.md), [Identity](identity.md), [XRPL](xrpl.md)

## The rules

A hub is named by the digest of its rules: the sponsor's terms and one signed acceptance per validator (`rules.rs`). An acceptance names the terms by their digest and no other validator, so a validator that drops out before genesis is replaced without touching the others' ([settlement-hubs](../decisions/settlement-hubs.md)).

`Terms::check` refuses terms whose values contradict what the hub's safety rests on: an account reserve too small to pay its account out at shutdown, fewer places for missing claims than a payee lets its payer leave missing, a hold notice no longer than a packet may be held, no places kept for deadlines, a vote window shorter than a validator's turn, blocks or claims larger than a follower decodes. The values themselves are starting points for the simulation.

## Digests and signatures

Everything is named by SHA-256 under a domain tag of its own (`digest.rs`), and every signature signs such a digest, so a signature made for one purpose never verifies for another. A member's operation is signed over the hub ID, its account number, the account's next sequence and the most fee it pays; a validator's over the hub ID, its index and a sequence that only grows; a payer's commitment and claim over the hub ID and the hold. Account and hold numbers are never reused, so nothing signed can be replayed in another hub, or twice in one. Weak Ed25519 keys are refused wherever a key is decoded, since under ZIP-215 they verify forgeries ([Identity](identity.md#node-key)).

All wire types are canonical OER with fixed-width integers ([Encoding](encoding.md)).

## The ledger

The ledger (`ledger/`) is a pure function of the blocks it executes: time is the block's timestamp, and nothing reads a clock or an RNG, so every validator that executes the same blocks reaches the same state, which the state's Merkle root lets anyone check.

- **Every drop is in exactly one place.** After every block the ledger asserts that what the escrow holds for the hub equals everything the hub owes: balances and reserves, holds and their reserves, pending withdrawals with their ledger fees, fees waiting for their voters, and the reserve account. That sum is the supply each validator publishes.
- **Bounded tables.** Accounts, holds, holds per account, withdrawals, pending and remembered deposits, claimed sequences per hold and operations per block each have a bound in the terms. An operation that would pass one fails; it never grows a table.
- **Refused or failed.** An operation that is not its signer's to make (unknown account, bad signature, wrong sequence) or cannot pay its fee is refused: it pays nothing and uses no sequence. One that is valid but cannot take effect fails: it pays its fee and uses its sequence, as a `tec` result does on the XRP Ledger. A failure changes nothing else.
- **Deadlines first.** A block keeps places for `redeem` and `claim`, which go first; a block that breaks the order or the bounds is not executed, and validators do not vote for it.
- **New accounts set a disbursal address first,** so every account the hub pays out at shutdown has somewhere to go. Validators' accounts, which receive their fees, are exempt and lock no reserve. A closing account does nothing more: it waits for its payout.
- **Nothing a block holds can stop the ledger.** A panic would happen in every validator at the same block, and the hub could never execute it, so whatever members and validators can put in a block is refused or fails before it reaches an assertion: terms that would let the ledger reach a state it cannot leave are refused by `Terms::check`, and new rules never shrink a table.

## Holds

A payee takes from a hold only what its payer signed (`ledger/holds.rs`):

- `redeem` pays what the payer's claim adds to the last redeemed total, less what `claim` already paid for sequences up to the claim's W. Totals and W only grow.
- `claim` pays commitments above the last redeemed W whose fulfillments hash to their conditions, in a block stamped before their expiry. A hold remembers each sequence claimed until a redeem passes it, so no packet is paid twice. A claim is all or nothing.
- A payer's reduction takes effect at the first block stamped after the hold's notice; a hold reduced to nothing closes and frees its reserve.
- A payee may release a hold toward it, which goes back to its payer at once. Only the holds an account pays into count toward its limit per account: anyone can open a hold toward an account, and it can release them, so no one can use up another's room.

## What validators attest

Deposits, withdrawal outcomes, the escrow's setup and resets of the test network each take effect once a quorum of validators attested the same thing, so up to f faulty validators can neither make one up nor hold one back (`ledger/escrow.rs`, `ledger/validator.rs`). Resets count only when a quorum saw the same new chain.

- **A deposit is credited once.** The hub remembers credited transactions back to a floor that rises as its table fills, and a deposit below the floor that is not pending already is not taken: it may have been credited and forgotten. One that is pending was never credited, and is attested whatever its ledger.
- **Each validator has its own share of the pending deposits,** so a faulty one that attests made-up deposits fills only its own share, and honest attestations still fit.
- **Deposits wait until a quorum attested the escrow,** at genesis and again after a reset.
- **A change of validators moves attestations to their validators' new indices** and drops those of validators that left, so they never count toward the new set's quorum; a withdrawal or deposit whose attestations make a quorum of the new set is settled at the end of the block. Votes start again. A pending deposit below the floor that no one attests any more is kept, so it can still be credited; such entries count toward the table, and each removed faulty validator can leave up to its share of them behind. A validator may change its vote, for a reset as for new rules. When the consensus or escrow keys change, the new set keeps the escrow's next sequence for the old set's change of the signer list, and no new rules are taken until it is made.
- **Validators' accounts are outside the bound on accounts,** which counts only accounts opened as members', so a change of validators never finds the table full; an account a validator joins with stays open, a payout that would have closed it a plain withdrawal.

A reset writes off every balance, reserve, hold and pending payment, and keeps the accounts, which lock their reserve again from what they next receive. Only the testnet's network ID allows it ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)).

## Consensus

`consensus/` is Simplex (Chan and Pass), without IO and generic over a block's payload, which the ledger fills and checks through the `Application` hooks. Commonware's implementation is the reference and the source of its scenarios; nothing depends on it.

- **What an honest validator signs.** It notarizes at most one block per view, and never one it nullified; it nullifies only its current view; it votes to finalize a block only if it neither nullified that view nor notarized another block in it, and only one block per height. So two conflicting votes, two certificates of one view, or two finalized blocks at one height name only validators that broke these rules: the evidence never blames an honest one.
- **Never two conflicting votes, even across a crash.** Before it sends a step's messages, the host persists the step's batch: the view, every vote it signed above its last finalized block, the heights it voted to finalize, the blocks it notarized and the certificates it holds above it, and whether it declared the hub stalled. Certificates are kept so that a hub whose validators all restart at once can still skip the views they had nullified. A validator that declared a stall never votes again.
- **Timing follows from the region.** Every duration derives from Δ, the region's one-way delay (`consensus/config.rs`): a timestamp is timely within twice Δ of a validator's clock, a block is proposed every interval and final three delays later, a silent leader's view is skipped after the interval and two delays. The hub's margin M is the precision, f skipped views, an interval and three delays. The values are never tuned for the simulation's sake.
- **Leaders** take turns by view, view mod n of the set running the epoch.
- **Epochs.** Each validator set runs an epoch of its own, numbered, with views from 1, and every block, vote and certificate names its epoch, which a vote's signature covers. Once the last block of an epoch (the one before the height a change takes over at) is final, the next epoch runs from view 1 and its first block builds on that block at view 0: every validator hands over at the same place, whichever certificate told it the block was final ([epoch-handover](../decisions/epoch-handover.md)).
- **The end of an epoch is a property of the chain.** The block that schedules a change may be notarized and not final while blocks after it are proposed, so a validator reads where the running epoch ends from the chain a block builds on: the change the application's state after the block's parent names (`Application::scheduled_change`), with any change final blocks scheduled. So no block of the old set at or above the boundary is notarized on any chain, whatever each validator has finalized.
- **The application's states.** The application keeps the state after a block it found valid, final or not, for as long as it likes, and says whether it still holds it (`Application::holds`); the state after the last final block it always holds, and the state after the block it just verified at least until the next call. Before the engine judges or builds a block, it has the application verify, in height order and from the payloads it holds, every block between the nearest ancestor whose state the application still holds (or the last final block) and the parent: a block whose state was let go of counts as not verified. `scheduled_change` is asked only about blocks whose state is held, so its "no change" never stands for "unknown". A block whose payload the engine no longer holds cannot be built on until it is fetched again, as any missing block.
- **A repeat at the end of an epoch.** While an epoch's last block is notarized and not final, the epoch's next leader proposes a repeat of it: the same height, the block as its parent, no payload. Only the old set makes one, only of its epoch's last block on the chain it builds on, and the application never sees it; finalizing the repeat finalizes the block. Without it, votes split by a partition (some to finalize the block, some to nullify its view, neither a quorum) would halt the hub for good, since nothing of the old set may build on its last block. The rule that two certificates of one height are evidence counts a repeat as the block it repeats.
- **Finality before the boundary is judged.** A finalization proves its block and every ancestor final, whatever the block is, so a chain is finalized content first, in order: a block in it may schedule the change that ends the epoch, and the repeats that follow it are only the proof. A chain stops where its epoch ended, instead of asserting it cannot.
- **Catching up, epoch by epoch.** Peers keep the recent finalized blocks with their certificates (`RETAINED_BLOCKS`), and the last block of each of the last few epochs (`MAX_BOUNDARIES`) with what proves it: its own finalization, or a repeat's with the repeats between. A delivery never crosses the end of an epoch, so a validator finalizes the block that scheduled a change, and learns the next set, before it sees anything that set signed; a delivery may end in repeats, which the asker takes once it has applied the blocks before them. A validator further behind than peers keep is handed the last block of its own epoch, or a later one its set signed. A finalization is always checked with a set the engine already knows, and a set reaches it only from data such a set signed: the ledger's report after a block it finalized, or the state after a checked block.
- **The host's contract for a validator far behind.** `Event::Behind(F)` carries a block F already checked with a known set. The host loads the state after F from a validator or member and checks it against the state root F carries, then calls `Engine::jump(F, change)`, where `change` is the change of validators that state has scheduled and not yet made, if any. `jump` checks F again before anything changes. `Engine::reconfigure` is only for a change read from the state after a block the engine finalized.
- **Room for blocks, per view.** A view holds its leader's first proposal and only blocks a verified certificate names beside it, and only blocks whose view is held and whose height one block per view since the last final block allows. So a faulty leader fills only the room of the views it leads, and can never crowd out an honest block.
- **Changes of validators are in the chain.** New rules that fell due in a block take effect two blocks later: the ledger schedules them for that height, and after each final block the engine asks the ledger for a scheduled change and takes it in before it finalizes the next. So the ledger and consensus hand over at the same height, every validator alike however far behind, and no block records votes from before the set in force.
- **A certificate it cannot check** never steers the engine: a message of a later epoch makes it ask a validator of its own set for what it lacks, never the message's sender.

The `hub` simulation runs 4 to 7 validators over a network with loss, duplication, slow links, partitions, crashes and clock skew, with up to f faulty ones (silent, withholding, proposing invalid payloads, equivocating, flooding proposals) or, one run in ten, f + 1 colluding. In a third of the runs, one more validator follows the hub and replaces another at a height the chain schedules, while validators that fall behind catch up across the change, loading states that a peer hands them and the block's root checks. Half of those runs split the network just before the boundary, some with the least notice the ledger gives, and some lose every vote to finalize the epoch's last block itself, so that only a repeat finalizes it: knobs for coverage of the handover, not production values. It checks that honest validators never finalize different blocks at one height, that a fork always leaves evidence and that evidence never names an honest validator, that every honest validator past a change runs the new set, and that blocks keep becoming final within production timings once the network heals.

## The escrow

`escrow/` holds what the hub does on the XRP Ledger, as functions of the rules and the ledger's state, so every validator builds the same transaction from the same block and signs it alone.

- **Keys** come from the node key under `node/hub/<nonce>/…`: a validator's consensus key, escrow key and BIP 32 payout key, and the sponsor's escrow master key. The master key is the sponsor's to keep: the same key makes the same escrow account again after a reset of the test network ([Identity](identity.md#seed-paths)).
- **Setup and check.** The sponsor funds the escrow, then sets its signer list, `RequireDest`, and disables its master key last. The validators take an escrow only with the master key disabled, no regular key, `RequireDest`, the signer list exactly the rules' (each escrow key weight 1, quorum q) and no other objects, all as of a validated ledger, the objects as of that ledger or later: before it, the master key could still have made one.
- **Nothing one party does can stop the escrow's sequence.** A transaction the XRP Ledger refuses as malformed never uses its sequence, and every escrow transaction after it would wait for ever. So `Rules::check` takes only acceptances whose escrow key is a key of the XRP Ledger and not the escrow's own (a signer list cannot name its account), and whose payout key is a secp256k1 point; and the ledger refuses the escrow itself and the zero account as a withdrawal's, a close's or a disbursal's destination.
- **What validators attest** from the escrow's transactions: a deposit is a successful Payment of XRP to the escrow with a destination tag, for what it delivered; for tag 0 the memo names the new account's key, and anything else attests no key, so the hub pays it back. An escrow transaction's outcome is its sequence and whether it succeeded or failed with a `tec`. The old validators' change of the signer list, once a quorum saw it apply, frees its kept sequence, and the reserve account pays its fee. A withdrawal's outcome counts only if the transaction at its sequence is its own Payment, delivering its amount.
- **The payout set** of a final block: in escrow sequence order with no gap, the withdrawals not yet settled and a pending change of signer list, as already signed; the selector, a signer list of every validator's payout key's child at the block's height (two 31-bit levels); and one payout per account with a disbursal address whose balance, reserves and the holds it paid into, less the fee, reach the least payout. Withdrawals and the selector are signed with escrow keys, payouts with the set's own keys. The first selector the XRP Ledger applies decides which set pays: every other set's payouts are refused for keys not on the list (`tefBAD_SIGNATURE`), without using a sequence.
- **The fee** of every escrow transaction is the rules' ledger fee, which must cover a quorum's signatures.

## Fees

A share of every fee goes to the reserve account; the rest waits in the pool of its block until the vote window has passed, and is then split equally among the validators whose votes for that block later blocks recorded (`ledger/fees.rs`). A block records each vote once, and only for blocks within the window. The time a validator's vote was last recorded also decides absence: a validator unrecorded for longer than the terms allow shuts the hub down.

## The validator's service

`service/` is one validator, without IO: consensus and the ledger together (`Validator`). A block's payload is the operations its mempool holds, deadlines first, and the votes for earlier blocks it recorded; a block is verified by executing it on a copy of the state and comparing the root. Members talk to it in self-authenticating messages (`client.rs`): requests, and statements the validator signs, which a member trusts once f + 1 validators signed them alike.

- **Whatever its pool keeps pays when a block takes it, or proves nothing, and is gone after the next final block** (`service/mempool.rs`). A member's operation is taken only if the account can pay its fee and those of everything it already has waiting, and per account only the run of consecutive sequences from its current one is ever selected; an operation past a gap waits in a small set of its own and goes first when the pool is full. Each validator has a bounded share of the pool, and its own operations take at most an equal part of half the places a block does not keep for deadlines, so no validator can fill the blocks. Deadlines have a part of the pool ordinary operations never take, as they have places of their own in every block. After each final block the pool drops what the block refused and what the new state makes impossible, and after a change of validators what no longer verifies.
- **A leader's block always fits.** It fills a block by bytes as well as by count, below the payload's bound by a margin (`payload::OPERATION_BYTES`), and the pool takes no operation one block could not hold, nor a claim with more commitments than the rules allow. Otherwise every leader would build the same block too large to propose, and the hub would stall.
- **Catch-up is for validators.** A delivery can be as large as a consensus message, so the service serves catch-up only to a sender the transport authenticated as the node of a validator in the set in force or the one scheduled; a member reads the state through requests. A message's content takes the largest consensus message (`wire::MAX_CONTENT`), and nothing on the way out is encoded past its bound: statements are split across messages by count and bytes.
- **Shares of an escrow signature come only from escrow keys.** It signs each withdrawal the log gave a sequence and passes its signature to the other validators; it counts a signature only if its key is the escrow key of a validator of the set in force, once per validator, and submits once it has a quorum. Anyone can sign a payment with keys of their own, and a quorum of such signatures would make it submit a transaction the XRP Ledger refuses.
- **A member's tally of statements is bounded per validator** (`client::Agreement`), so no validator can push out another's signatures: each has a bounded number of signatures on statements still waiting for f + 1, and one over that drops its own lowest. A statement further above the height f + 1 validators have reached than any honest one could be is refused. What is dropped or refused of a statement still waiting is reported, so the member reads that part again rather than count an answer it no longer holds whole.
- **What it answers.** It takes operations to submit, and answers for an account or a hold by key or number, and for what touched an account since a height (`Since`), each as of a final block.
- **The contract it keeps for `Since`:** every member operation that touches the account from the height asked (its own, a transfer, hold or close toward it, and any operation on a hold it pays into or takes from), in the log's order, each named by its height and its place in its block, then the newest final block it covered. Deposits credited and withdrawals returned show in the account's state, not in `Since`. An answer that grows long stops before a block, but always carries at least one block whole, over as many messages as it takes, so a block that touches an account more often than one answer holds never stalls the member. A `Final` is its word that it sent everything up to it. What a block touched is worked out from the state before it, since an operation can change whose a hold is.
- **Its history is bounded,** by blocks and by bytes: it keeps the recent blocks that touched any account (`KEPT_BLOCKS`, `KEPT_BYTES`), and answers a `Since` from further back with `Trimmed`, since it can no longer vouch for what happened there. A member then reads its state anew. A final block that leaves before it was kept, as when more become final in one step than the chain holds, raises that height too: nothing is dropped silently. The states kept for blocks not yet final are copies of the ledger, at most `MAX_EXECUTED` of them; while finality stalls the lowest go first, and such a block is executed again from the final state once it is final.
- **A final block it cannot follow stops it.** A block finalized while it was behind is executed from the final state; one that does not decode or execute as certified means more than f validators are faulty, or a bug. The validator then follows no further block, never votes again and signs the payout set of the last block it executed, as at a stall, rather than crash.
- **What it pushes vouches for nothing:** statements it sends unasked carry no `Final`, so they never count toward what a member read up to a height: a member that missed a push would otherwise count a later one over what it missed.
- **A snapshot of the ledger** (`ledger/snapshot.rs`) is its whole state as bytes, with one encoding per state: every table in strictly ascending order, and what is derived from the tables, such as the index of keys, rebuilt rather than read. `Ledger::restore` takes the root the state must have, the one a certified block carries, and refuses a snapshot whose root differs, so no caller can rely on a state before its root is checked.
