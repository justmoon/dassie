# Settlement

How a node moves money with its peers on an underlying ledger, and how its books learn about it: the settlement scheme interface, the external assets schemes settle through, the credit a node extends to each peer, when it asks a peer to settle and how much it settles when asked, and how settlement messages are delivered exactly once.

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-settlement/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/settlement.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/trust.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/ledgers.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/schemes.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/links.rs`, `crates/dassie-peer/src/node/settle.rs`, `crates/dassie-peer/src/channel.rs`
**Read with:** [Ledger](ledger.md), [XRPL](xrpl.md), [Network](network.md), [Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md), [Connector](connector.md)

Each node keeps its own books and decides from them alone: whether it takes a settlement, when it asks a peer to settle, how much it settles, and when it ends a peering. What a peer says is an input to these decisions, never trusted past the node's limits. However a peer behaves, and however far its books are from ours, **it costs us at most the credit we extend, plus what it has in flight** ([Connector](connector.md#money-in-flight-with-a-peer)). The node does not know the credit a peer extends to it, does not model the peer's books, and does not measure drift ([own-books](../decisions/own-books.md)).

## Schemes and ledgers

A settlement scheme serves one internal ledger, and a ledger has at most one active scheme. The node's registry of schemes and of the [external assets](#external-assets) they settle through is in `engine/core/schemes.rs`. **A ledger runs only in its node's realm:** a scheme activates only on a ledger of the node's realm, and the node offers the owner only its realm's schemes, so test and live nodes never share a scheme and never peer ([mainnet-schemes](../decisions/mainnet-schemes.md)). A settlement hub's scheme is of its hub's network's realm, the testnet's of the test realm and mainnet's of the live realm, and takes only rules of a hub on that network ([hub-scheme-names](../decisions/hub-scheme-names.md)). A stored scheme of the other realm does not run: the node starts without it and tells the owner. The realm is fixed at setup, so only a database from before that rule can hold one. **A disabled scheme** (`DISABLED`, test RLUSD) is neither offered nor added, and one stored does not run either, the owner told: its books, its checkpoint and its asset's state stay as they are, so nothing is written off and nothing it held is booked anew ([XRPL](xrpl.md#rlusd)). **A stored scheme that can no longer be made** from its config or its state, one this version knows, such as a hub's whose rules no longer decode, or whose account's state is of another hub than they name ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)), does not run either: the node starts without it and tells the owner, rather than stop on one scheme the owner could not then remove. Only a known scheme that cannot be made is left out; one this version does not know, or one that fails once made, still stops the start, so that a release that cannot run the database is rolled back ([Release](release.md)). The owner may add it again with a config that works, which replaces the stored one, or remove it. **The methods on a scheme the node does not run are dropped,** whether it is disabled, of the other realm or no longer made: no packet crosses them and no settlement on them can be booked, so a method on one could never be paid. A peering keeps its other methods, each on its own slot in the peer's endpoint, and the dropped method's slot takes no new packet while those in flight on it resolve there ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods)); one left with none ends at once, collecting nothing, as for a ban, but without a cooldown, so the two may peer again on a scheme both run ([Network](network.md#termination)). The peer's accounts on the scheme's ledger keep what they show. **Nothing else on its ledger moves either:** our settlements pending on it stay pending, for the scheme to reconcile when it runs again, and its BTP tokens stay but connect no client ([Connector](connector.md#btp-tokens)). A ledger of the live realm activates only where the live realm may run, which it may not but by the dev switch ([XRPL](xrpl.md#real-money)).

Activating a scheme, when the owner adds it or at start, makes the external asset it settles through if that is not active yet, then the scheme, each from its config and its last [checkpoint](#checkpoints-and-activation), opens the ledger's common accounts, books the activation entry, and gives the ledger its STREAM receiver and payer ([Connector](connector.md#local-endpoints)). Removing a scheme is refused while anything still depends on it: tokens, outgoing payments on its ledger, or, for a scheme over a hub, a move between the hub and the owner book of its asset on its way ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)). **Peerings that settle on it do not keep it:** its removal winds each of their methods on it down, collected once and dropped within a bounded time, as a method is dropped ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods), [settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)). Meanwhile the scheme is offered to no peer, so no method or peering is made on it, and still runs, so what the peers settle on it is booked, and over a hub it hears what becomes of each packet in flight on a dropped method's slot, with the claims either way ([Connector](connector.md#settlement-method-slots)). **It goes only once nothing can still come on it:** no peering has a method on it, no packet is in flight on a peer's slot on its ledger, and a wait has passed since its last method went (`Core::removal_wait_ms`), the longest a packet is held, for a claim over a hub that follows a packet's end, plus `depeer_wait_ms`, for a settlement the peer had on its way to reach its ledger and be announced, plus the longest wait between a settlement message's tries, for that message to go again; and nothing else keeps it. A scheme no method is on, with nothing in flight on its ledger, goes at once. Removing a scheme being removed succeeds, and changes nothing. The removal is kept across a restart, which carries it on, counting the wait from the start, and the owner sees it under way, with the methods left. Its accounts and checkpoint stay, so adding it again picks up where it was, including the settlements still pending. An asset no scheme settles through any more is let go, its connection closed and its checkpoint kept.

## The scheme interface

A scheme is sans-IO: its methods return effects (`Effect` in `scheme.rs`) for the node to book or carry out, rather than calling back into the node. The contract the node relies on:

- **Prepare submits nothing.** `prepare_settlement` returns an ID unique within the scheme, an amount of at most what was asked in whole units of its ledger, and the message for the peer. The node books it before anything leaves ([below](#outgoing-settlements)).
- **Its asset is handed to it** whenever it may sign, submit or look something up (preparing, executing, discarding, reconciling), and after the asset heard from its ledger (`on_asset`). It keeps nothing of the asset but what the asset told it.
- **Execute only once the pending transfer is on disk,** and report `Finalize` or `Cancel`, now or later. After a restart, `reconcile` gets the settlements still pending and reports the same for each.
- **The message can be made again** from the settlement's ID, so a restart that lost it can still announce the settlement.
- **Peering keeps nothing until it is made.** Creating, accepting and finalizing a peering request only check the other side's data; the scheme keeps what it needs of a peer when the node tells it the peering is made (`on_peered`), after the peer's accounts are open. So attempts that fail cost the scheme no room.
- **Peering waits for the first sync.** A scheme's first sync books what its account holds as the owner's deposit; only after it can the scheme tell a peer's settlement from a deposit (`has_synced`). Until then the node neither offers, asks for nor accepts a peering on the scheme: it leaves the scheme out of its link state, picks no candidate on it, and refuses requests on it as for a scheme not active here ([Network](network.md#asking-and-answering), [synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md)). The first sync is kept with the scheme's state, so a restart with the ledger out of reach peers as before, and peerings already made stay. A scheme without a ledger to follow has synced from the start.
- **Reports** (deposits, withdrawals, ledger fees, reconciliations) are recorded even past the owner's limit ([Ledger](ledger.md#what-the-ledger-refuses-and-what-it-records)). A reconciliation names its cause, which changes only how it is reported: a change of the ledger's reserve is expected and logged as information, any other difference as a warning. An incoming settlement is booked Dr `assets/settlement`, Cr `assets/interledger/<peer>`.
- **`Unavailable`** means not now (its ledger not seen, no funds to spare, too much in flight, an issuer's freeze). It is asked again at the next check as if nothing happened.
- **Whether peers can pay us** (`can_receive`): a scheme over a hub says it cannot once its hub is shutting down, stalled or ended ([below](#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)).
- **The peer half answers as an owner book's by default:** a scheme that does not override the peering steps and settling gives no peering info, refuses every peering step and every settlement (`Unsupported`), and cancels a settlement it is asked to execute ([Owner books](#owner-books)). A scheme that peers overrides each.
- **The settlement check** (`on_check`) lets a scheme read what it needs of its ledger at every check, through its asset, such as an issuer's account ([XRPL](xrpl.md#rlusd)); what it learns comes as news.
- **The scheme's reserve** is what its ledger holds back from the balance: never booked, so never spendable.
- **Its status,** as the owner sees it, is its asset's until the asset is synced, then its own.
- **Promises per packet** (`commitments.rs`): a scheme whose peerings carry them, as a settlement hub's do, signs a commitment with each Prepare, checks one with each Prepare it takes, and signs a claim into each reply to a Fulfill; as payer, it decides from its ledger the packets whose Reject upstream waits for it ([Connector](connector.md#responses-and-expiry)). A scheme without them settles as every other.
- **Unlimited trust** (`unlimited_trust`): a scheme whose peers trust each other without limit, as the stub with unlimited trust's do for tests, has the node extend each peer on it the unlimited credit, never ask one to settle on its own account and settle nothing when asked ([below](#the-stub-scheme)). No scheme over a hub does.
- **The owner's payments** (`pay_owner`): a scheme may pay the owner's funds out of its account to an address its ledger pays, one at a time, as the node moves them between two of its ledgers ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)). The payment has an ID, and the scheme keeps with its state the one on its way and what became of the last, so it answers for it across restarts: on its way, applied, failed (its ledger recorded it as failed: it moved nothing but its fee, and the scheme says whether it was too little to create the account it paid), cancelled (it never reached its ledger, which can never apply it now: it moved nothing, not even a fee), or unknown, when the scheme never read its outcome and only where the money went can tell. **It is held against the owner's funds while it is on its way:** the node books it as a pending transfer out of the owner's group on the payment's ledger (Dr `equity/owner`, Cr `assets/settlement`, an `owner_payment` keyed by the scheme and the payment's ID) in the batch that keeps the payment, with a second hold for the most its fees may cost, and the books refuse them, before anything is signed, when the owner's funds do not cover both. So conversions, payer and BTP tokens and another scheme's gas see the owner's funds less it from the moment it leaves, and the owner's funds are never below zero when it lands. The scheme reports once it is no longer on its way (`Effect::OwnerPaid`): applied, the hold is posted; failed, cancelled, or of an outcome the scheme never read (its ledger's balance then said what it did), it is voided. The fee hold is voided either way, and the fee the payment cost booked. The hold is journaled while pending, as a settlement is ([Ledger](ledger.md#the-journal)); at a start, and when a scheme is activated again, each one the scheme no longer has on its way is posted or voided by what the scheme says became of it. The ledger the money reaches books it as the owner's deposit when it sees it.
- **What a peering over a hub costs beyond its hold** is a loss on the peer (`Effect::PeerLoss`): Dr `expenses/losses`, Cr the account the money left, as its cause says, recorded whatever the owner's funds ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#packets-promises-and-claims)).

**A scheme's effects are booked all or none.** Whatever hands them over (the scheme's ledger or timers, a peer's settlement, executing or reconciling a settlement, the owner's stub deposit or request to a faucet), the books first check every transfer the effects make, in order, each as it would find the accounts with the ones before it booked (`Ledger::validate_posted`). If they refuse one, none of the effects is carried out, and the scheme's checkpoint does not move, so it still explains exactly what the books hold. Nor does anything its asset asked of its ledger with that input go out once no scheme on the asset runs: no message, no new connection and no request to a faucet. Posting or voiding a settlement of ours is not checked: it raises no total and tightens no limit, so it cannot make a later transfer fail.

**A scheme that fails stops alone.** Effects the books refuse stop the scheme, shown as failed, rather than the node, under the same policy wherever they came from. A stopped scheme is fed nothing more (nothing its asset learns, no settlements from peers, executions, reconciliations, deposits or requests to a faucet) and its checkpoint is no longer written, so a restart reads from that checkpoint again what the books refused, and books it then or stops again, never twice. Once no scheme on an asset runs, the asset's checkpoint is not written either, so the two stay as they were written together, and its connection is closed, so the driver does not make it again for ever; so is the connection of an asset that stopped by itself, with every scheme on it. A scheme activated on the asset, or a restart, connects anew. Nor is a stopped scheme peered on until the restart: the node leaves it out of its link state, asks for no peering on it and refuses requests on it, as before its first sync ([Network](network.md#asking-and-answering)). Peerings already made on it stay, and a settlement a peer announces on it is refused (400) until the restart; the peer sends a channel's settlements one at a time, again until one is taken, so the rest of that channel waits behind it until then ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#message-set)).

## Owner books

A scheme need not peer. One that does not is an **owner book** (`SettlementScheme::peers`): it keeps the books of what the node holds on its ledger, as any scheme does, with the owner's deposits and withdrawals, its ledger's fees and reserve and the gas other schemes on its asset spend, and it pays the owner's payments (`pay_owner`); it peers with nobody. So the node can hold an asset, and move it, without settling with peers in it, as a settlement hub's funding needs: the hub's deposits leave the owner book of the hub's asset and its withdrawals reach it, whether or not the node peers on that book ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)).

- **The node never peers on it.** It leaves it out of its link state, picks no candidate on it, and refuses a request on it as unsupported, as on a scheme not yet synced ([Network](network.md#asking-and-answering)); the scheme refuses every step of a peering too, as the interface's peer half does by default. A peering stored from when the XRPL schemes peered may still have a method on one, which would settle directly: nothing can be settled on it, since the scheme prepares no settlement, so the node takes no packet over it and asks nothing on it, and drops it at its first review after the start, without collecting, as a method on a scheme it does not run; a peering left with none ends at once ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods)). What the method's accounts hold stays on them, settled by nobody: what the peer owed there, and what was owed it, is lost to each side, as on any method dropped without collecting. A settlement message on it, as one a channel still carries, is acknowledged as a bare notice that books nothing, so the channel moves on ([XRPL](xrpl.md#owner-books)).
- **Whether a scheme peers is the scheme's,** fixed while it runs. Every XRPL scheme is an owner book, whatever its config says ([XRPL](xrpl.md#owner-books)); every other scheme peers.
- **A scheme that would settle directly is an owner book.** One neither over a hub nor of unlimited trust peers with nobody, and a settlement message on it books nothing: the node asserts both as it starts the scheme, so no peer's accounts ever open on such a scheme, for a peering, a method or a downlink's settlement ([hubs-only](../decisions/hubs-only.md)).

## External assets

An external asset is something the node holds outside its books, of a type: an account on the XRP Ledger ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-account)) or the node's account in a settlement hub ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#the-members-side)) today, later others. A scheme names the asset it settles through, and several schemes may share one ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)). The types and IDs are in `dassie-settlement` (`asset.rs`): a type and its number name an asset of a type the node may hold several of, written `<type>/<number>`, as `xrpl-testnet-account/0`, and a type alone names one it holds one of at most, as a hub account, whose type is the ID of the scheme over its hub, `hub-xrpl-testnet-xrp` ([hub-scheme-names](../decisions/hub-scheme-names.md)). No type's name holds a `/`, so no text names two assets.

- **It owns what its schemes would otherwise race for:** its keys, its connection to its ledger, what it signs and the order it signs in, and what it holds.
- **What it learns, every scheme on it is told,** each booking all or none, before the asset forgets it. Each scheme keeps its own place in what it learns, written with what it books, so a scheme that stopped reads again what it missed, and the others are not held back. What concerns a scheme's own transactions while it is not on the asset (stopped or removed), or what the asset told it in the input whose bookings the books refused, the asset keeps for it until it is back and has read it, or has told it what it could not read again, which the scheme then looks up and books as one it never read, so its settlements are booked once, neither twice nor never; what it tells again for that scheme alone, the others booked when they were first told. An asset written off meanwhile, everything it held gone (a testnet reset), is written off by the scheme too, once back and before it follows the asset again: the asset counts its write-offs, and each scheme keeps the count it last saw with its state, so a place on a chain gone never holds the asset back.
- **A scheme that joins an asset that has heard from its ledger** has the node connect the asset anew, so it reads again from the earliest place of its schemes, the new one's included, and tells them all it has synced; one whose connection was closed is connected again.
- **The ledger is authoritative for what it holds.** The node shows each holding as the ledger last said (`asset_views`, which the owner sees on the wallet's External Assets page, [Wallet](wallet.md#pages)), never as the books add it up. The books keep `assets/settlement` only to book against, in step with the holding the ledger's scheme settles in ([Ledger](ledger.md#chart-of-accounts)).
- **It is active while a scheme settles through it:** made with the first, from its checkpoint, and let go with the last.
- **Its config is its own,** the owner's, set apart from any scheme's (`SetAssetConfig` over [RPC](rpc.md), `dassie setup --asset`) and kept with the books: for an XRPL account, its server, its faucet and its validator list publishers ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-account)). A scheme's config says only what is the scheme's, and one that names the account's is refused. An asset made after a change takes it at once; one active then keeps the config it was made with until the node's next start, and the owner sees that another waits. A settlement hub's account is the exception: its config is the hub's rules, which its scheme names ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)).
- **Its number.** An XRPL account has one, the index of its seed path. Only the first of each kind is built, number 0; the number stays in its ID, so that a further one of a kind, at a seed path of its own, takes an ID of its own without renaming what the first's keeps. A hub account has none: the node is in one hub per asset, with one account in each, so the scheme over the hub names it.
- **A holding is on the books only on the ledger whose scheme settles in it.** Another scheme on the asset may spend it as gas, such as the XRP an RLUSD scheme pays fees and reserves with: where a scheme on the asset settles in that holding, the gas is booked on that scheme's ledger, as a ledger fee or a reconciliation, and spent only within the owner's funds there, which the node tells the asset before each call that may sign (`owner_funds`); where none does, the gas is off the books, and the asset only keeps it above its ledger's reserve ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)). A scheme the books stopped still holds what it settles in on its books, so until it runs again after a restart no other scheme spends that holding; one the owner removed holds it no longer, as after a restart, when the asset cannot know it was there. The node tells the asset which it is (`detach`).

### Assets that follow a ledger

An asset with a ledger to follow names a WebSocket URL; it says what to send and the node feeds it what comes back, then tells its schemes what it learnt and carries out what it asks. The driver (`engine/links.rs`) runs one connection per asset, however many schemes settle through it, bound to an epoch, so a message from a connection the engine has replaced is dropped. A connection whose queue overflows drops messages, and the asset, hearing nothing, connects anew; so does one whose message is longer than the bound, which fits the longest answer an XRPL account reads, a proof server's. What a connection has read waits for the core within a byte bound of its own; at the bound it reads nothing more until the core takes some, so a server that floods it fills its own socket, not the node's memory. Names resolve under the node's outbound rules: public addresses only, unless private ones are allowed (dev and tests).

Beside its connection, an asset may ask the node to fetch a document, such as a validator list from its publisher's site (`AssetEffect::Fetch`): an HTTPS GET under the same rules, following no redirect and reading a bounded body, whose body, or nothing on any failure, comes back on the connection it was asked on (`LedgerInput::Fetched`). A connection made anew drops it, and the asset asks again. An asset that waits for something, such as a list it has none of, says what (`waiting`), and the node shows it beside the asset's status and its schemes'.

### Assets that talk to several endpoints

An asset whose ledger is several parties it talks to apart, a settlement hub's validators, names their endpoints instead (`endpoints`), in an order that does not change while it runs. It sends to one by its index, and the node feeds it what came back from that endpoint under the same index, so the asset may take who sent it as said: the driver authenticates each endpoint. The asset asks again on its own timers, so a message lost costs nothing but time. The node's driver for them is not built yet ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#the-members-side)).

## Checkpoints and activation

The books keep, for each scheme, a **checkpoint**: the balance they record for it and its state; and for each external asset, its state. Each is written in the batch it explains, and a scheme's state and its asset's are also written with the pending transfer of each settlement it prepared, so what the asset signed is kept.

Activation books only the difference between the scheme's balance and the last checkpoint (`activation.rs`): with no checkpoint, the whole balance is the owner's first deposit; after that, a gain or a loss goes through `equity/suspense` as a reconciliation. So a restart never books the same money twice, and money that moved while the node was down is the owner's.

## Credit

What a node lets a peer owe it. The node books it as `trust` transfers between the peer's `contra/trust` and `assets/interledger` accounts (`crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/trust.rs`), and the trust account stays when a peering ends, so a later peering keeps it rather than post it twice.

- **Over a hub, the credit is the hold.** The credit a node extends to a peer over a settlement hub is its own hold toward the peer and its settlements to the peer on their way: its money set aside, from which the peer is paid whether the node cooperates or not, all of the credit and nothing more ([below](#settlement-hubs), [hubs-only](../decisions/hubs-only.md)). It starts at nothing, whichever node asked to peer and for a downlink alike, and follows the hold from then on.
- **The default credit sizes a request.** A payer over a hub asked to settle funds its hold with what it would have paid had it extended the default credit (`peer_credit` in `Policies`, `crates/dassie-node/src/node/mod.rs`), counting its hold as what it paid in advance: half the way from there down to its trigger ([below](#settling-on-request)). So the default credit sets how much one request funds a hold by, and with it the least a node keeps spendable in its hub ([below](#positions-and-thresholds)). It is a policy of the node, not an owner setting, and one value for every ledger ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#not-built-yet)). The node refuses to start with a default credit below the least that keeps a hub link settling after one crash where the asker holds nothing toward the payer (`policy::least_credit`, [below](#positions-and-thresholds)): the payer counts the default credit when asked, so with less, a crash's drift leaves it nothing to fund, and its payee asks for ever. So, without a crash too, a request to a payer with nothing held funds at least the least settlement.
- **With unlimited trust,** for tests, the stub extends every peer the unlimited credit instead ([below](#the-stub-scheme)).
- **Nothing is announced.** The credit a node extends is its own business: no message of the peer protocol carries it ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#versions)). More credit only means the creditor asks later.
- **Per method.** A peering extends credit on each of its settlement methods, in that method's ledger's units, as on a peering's only one: over each method's hub, the hold there, a method added later included ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)). No budget spans methods, which would need exchange rates in the credit rules.

How credit worked when peers settled directly, the node that asked extending credit and the node that accepted none, growing with the fees a peer earned, and what a method would need to bring it back, is in [Direct settlement](../designs/direct-settlement.md).

**What a link survives.** Asked at the asker's trigger, a debtor settles only if its own books leave it its trigger plus twice the least settlement ([below](#settling-on-request)). So a link survives drift of up to the credit the debtor counts as extending when it answers, plus the credit the asker extends, less two triggers and twice the least settlement. Over a hub the debtor counts the default credit, whatever its own hold, as above, so a hub link survives drift of up to the default credit plus the asker's hold, less the same. A payee over a hub also asks once the payer's hold toward it runs low, as its scheme reads it, while its books still show room; the payer answers that from its books too, so past the same bound it has nothing to settle then either. Past it, the creditor's room for its debtor stays at its trigger while the debtor's books leave it nothing to settle ([crash-drift](../decisions/crash-drift.md)). One crash drifts the books by at most the cap on money in flight ([Connector](connector.md#money-in-flight-with-a-peer)). A link cut one way drifts no more than a crash: a node takes no new Prepare from a peer its messages surely do not reach, so its Fulfills that cannot arrive are only those in flight when the cut began ([unreachable-peers](../decisions/unreachable-peers.md)).

## Positions and thresholds

A node knows only the credit it extends to a peer, not the credit the peer extends to it, so only the creditor can tell when a peer should settle. It asks; the debtor decides how much ([own-fulfills](../decisions/own-fulfills.md)). Both rules are in `crates/dassie-settlement/src/policy.rs`.

```
position = what the peer may still owe us: the credit we extend, plus what we owe it,
           net of our settlements in flight (from assets/interledger/<peer>)

ask the peer to settle   when position ≤ trigger
when asked, settle       (position − trigger) / 2, or nothing if that is less than the least settlement
```

Every threshold of a link is counted in its [largest packet](connector.md#the-largest-packet) `P`, not in the credit, which differs on each side and over a hub follows the holds ([thresholds-in-packets](../decisions/thresholds-in-packets.md)). So a node asks at the same point whatever credit it extends, none included.

**Each settlement method of a peering stands on its own:** its position is read from the peer's accounts on its own ledger, and its thresholds apply to it alone, so a peer short on one method is asked to settle on that one, whatever it holds on another ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)). Over a hub, the credit a method extends follows the holds on that method's hub.

| Threshold | In `P` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger to ask | 1.5 | Below the cap on money in flight, so a busy peer can still have a packet refused (T04) while its settlement is on its way; a trigger at the cap left too little credit for one crash's drift. |
| Least settlement | 1 | A peer that keeps asking cannot make us pay a ledger fee for every few drops. |
| Money in flight with a peer, each way | 2.5 | [Connector](connector.md#money-in-flight-with-a-peer) |
| Room below which a peer is no longer advertised, past a grace | 1 | [Network](network.md#advertised-peers) |
| Least default credit | 7.5 | Two triggers, twice the least settlement and one crash's drift at the cap on money in flight: a hub link survives a crash even where the asker holds nothing toward the payer, and a request to a payer with nothing held funds at least the least settlement ([above](#credit)) |
| What a payee over a hub lets claims add before it redeems | 2 | Each redeem costs the hub's fee; what is not taken is lost if the hub fails ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#packets-promises-and-claims)) |
| What a node keeps spendable in its hub, per peering over it | 4.75, at least the least settlement, at most 50 in all | A payer over a hub asked to settle funds its hold as if it extended the default credit (11): at most half the way from it down to the trigger (1.5), 4.75 ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)). Kept whatever the holds, so no settlement asked of us waits for a deposit. At most half a step of what it keeps, so a withdrawal down to it still carries many packets' worth |
| What a node keeps in its hub, its own holds included | 1000, moved a tenth either way | More than its holds are likely ever to need, so only funds truly in excess stay on the owner's own account; peers' holds toward it count nowhere until it takes from them. It deposits once 100 below, back up to it, and withdraws once 100 above, back down to it, so a move carries many times its fees ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node), [generous-hub-target](../decisions/generous-hub-target.md)) |

Amounts are rounded down to whole units of the scheme's ledger; what is less than a unit stays owed. Both rules are deliberately simple, to be tuned later (expected settlement latency, fees, volume on the link).

## Asking a peer to settle

The node asks a peer whose position on a settlement method has fallen to its trigger, naming only that method's scheme ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md)), as soon as a Prepare it takes from the peer on that method leaves it there, in the step that took it; and at every settlement check, the backstop, for a position that fell otherwise or a peer that was not due to be asked when it fell ([settle-at-the-trigger](../decisions/settle-at-the-trigger.md)). Each method of a peering is asked on its own. Nothing about a request is kept: a restart looks again.

- **It asks again** on a method once its books show a settlement from that peer on it, not on the `settlement` message alone: on the XRP Ledger the message books nothing, so asking on it would make the peer pay twice. It also asks again after a 409, since our acceptance may not have reached the peer yet, and after a request that surely never reached the peer (not delivered, [Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#outbound-requests)), which costs the peer nothing and must not use up its grace before it is withdrawn ([Network](network.md#advertised-peers)); either says so of the peering, so every method of it may be asked again. Each is asked again at the next Prepare that finds the peer at its trigger, or the next check. Otherwise it asks on a method at most once a minute, so a peer that does not answer, refuses (400, 401), is overloaded (503) or has nothing to settle with is not flooded: a payer that could not fund a request funds it once it can, without being asked again ([below](#settling-on-request)).
- **One request per method in flight.** The node sends no request to settle on a method while one to that peer on it is in flight, until it is answered, refused or given up, so however many Prepares find a peer short, it has one request on a method outstanding at most.
- **Only while its scheme is synced.** Until then, what the peer pays is not booked, and the node cannot tell whether it paid. An honest peer settles only when asked, and no peering is made before the scheme's first sync ([above](#the-scheme-interface)), so what a peer settles, asked routinely or by a node ending the peering, reaches the ledger after that sync booked what the account held as the owner's deposit, and is booked as the peer's settlement.
- **Only while the peer's settlement can reach us** ([below](#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)).
- **A new method is asked at once:** over a hub the credit starts at nothing ([Credit](#credit)), so the node asks at the first check after the method is made, unless one of the pair is still to be paid into the hub ([the design](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#5-joining)), and again once what the peer paid into its hold toward us is down to the trigger.
- **A peering the node is ending** is asked once more, by the peer node itself, before the peer is told, whatever its position; then no more ([Network](network.md#termination)).

### When peers cannot pay us

A scheme can lose the means to take a peer's settlement while it still runs: a scheme over a settlement hub cannot once its hub is shutting down, stalled or ended, since the hub then moves no peer's money to us ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#a-hub-that-failed)). The scheme says so (`can_receive`) for as long as it lasts, which may be for good. A frozen hub is no such hub: it makes blocks, and its method is unavailable only until the thaw ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#a-frozen-hub)). An owner book is never asked, since nothing peers on it ([Owner books](#owner-books)). Meanwhile:

- **The node asks no peer to settle on a method on it,** as while the scheme does not see its ledger. Its other methods are asked as usual.
- **A method's time short of room withdraws it but never ends the peering** ([Network](network.md#advertised-peers)). It grows as usual, so a method short of room past the grace is withdrawn, since no settlement can restore its room while it lasts; but it does not count toward ending the peering for room, so no peer is ended for what it could not pay ([ended-for-room](../decisions/ended-for-room.md)). The grace stays, so our own packets toward a peer that dips below its room only briefly can restore it before it is withdrawn.
- **The link state leaves the scheme out,** so no new peering is made on it ([Network](network.md#asking-and-answering)). This is a rule of its own: a scheme that only lost its ledger stays in the link state once it has synced.

Peerings already made stay. **A method on a scheme that has not been able to receive for as long as a silent peer is kept is dropped,** at once and without collecting, since no settlement on it could arrive; a peering left with none ends ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods), [settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)). The time is counted from the node's start at the earliest, as a peer's silence is, so a restart starts it again.

## Settling on request

A request says that the debtor stands near the asker's trigger. The debtor settles half the way from where it stands to its own trigger, which lands near the middle of the two.

- **It never pays itself into asking back:** settling lowers its own position, however far two nodes' books have drifted apart.
- **One settlement at a time.** It settles nothing on a method while a settlement to that peer is on its way on it (prepared, executing, pending in the ledger, or announced and not yet acknowledged): that one is the answer. Nor while a cancelled settlement on that method holds it off ([below](#outgoing-settlements)). The settlement channel is the pair's and carries one message at a time, so a settlement on another method waits for the channel, never for the method.
- **The answer says what is on its way:** the settlement it makes now, or the one already under way, or nothing. So an asker can tell a settlement still coming from none ([ending-a-peering](../decisions/ending-a-peering.md)).
- **A lying asker gets at most the credit the debtor extends to it.**
- **What it cannot fund now, it funds as soon as it can.** A request the debtor's scheme cannot fund in full when it comes, for want of what it can spend or of a free method or channel, is kept, one per method of a peering, in memory. Once the scheme can spend the least settlement and the method and the channel are free, the debtor funds it as the rule then stands, as if asked at that moment; it forgets it once funded in full, once its own request interval has passed since the request, which is when its asker asks again, or once the peering is being ended, the method wound down or the peering gone ([settle-when-funds-arrive](../decisions/settle-when-funds-arrive.md)). A scheme that could fund less than the least settlement funds nothing now, and the request in full once it can. The scheme says what a settlement could move (`SettlementScheme::spendable`), and the debtor tries again only once that changed.
- **A node settles only to answer a request,** at once or, when it could not fund it in full then, as soon as it can within its asker's interval: never on its own, not even at a peering's end.

## Settling at a peering's end

Nothing is settled for a peering's end ([ending-a-peering](../decisions/ending-a-peering.md)). From the moment a node knows a peering is ending, by deciding it or by being told, it settles nothing more with that peer: neither side has a reason to pay at the end. The node that ends it collects first: it waits for what the peer had on its way when last asked, so no settlement crosses the end between honest nodes ([Network](network.md#termination)). A ban collects nothing: the node tells the peer at once. What each side's books show stays on the peer's accounts, and a later peering with the node settles it as usual. A settlement already under way still arrives, and is credited once ([below](#incoming-settlements)).

## Outgoing settlements

The order makes a settlement reach the books exactly once, whatever crashes:

1. **Check.** The node validates the pending transfer (Dr `assets/interledger/<peer>`, Cr `assets/settlement`) before it asks the scheme, so a refusal leaves nothing prepared behind.
2. **Prepare.** The scheme prepares the settlement, for at most that amount.
3. **Book.** The node books the durable pending transfer and checkpoints the scheme. Nothing is booked between the check and this step, so the books always take it: a refusal here is a broken invariant, which stops the engine. So a prepared settlement is never discarded.
4. **Execute,** only once that transfer is on disk. A restart in between hands the scheme its pending settlements to reconcile.
5. **Finalize.** Only a settlement that executed is posted, and it is posted as the scheme finalizes it, in the batch that keeps the scheme's state without it: one held pending past that, a restart would hand back to a scheme that forgot it, to cancel though its ledger paid. The peer is told after every earlier message to it arrived, in order, and the message states what the settlement moved. On a scheme whose messages book nothing, a hub's, the bare notice waits for the channel if it must, in memory: one a restart drops books nothing, and the peer's ledger says what arrived. Until it arrives, the settlement is on its way, as the answer to a request to settle on its method says. So settling over one hub never waits for a settlement over another. On a scheme whose messages book what they state, the stub's, the message goes in the batch that posts it, so it must find the channel free: the node settles on such a scheme with a peer only while no settlement of ours to it is pending on any method and nothing is on its way or waiting, and on no other method with it until that one is posted. Should one find the channel busy all the same, as one pending from before a restart can when its scheme finalizes it beside another method's message, it is posted and reported, and its message waits as a notice does: the node never stops over it, and a restart before it goes loses it on the peer's side.
6. **Cancel.** A cancelled settlement is voided, and the node backs off settling with that peer on that method, exponentially; a settlement posted to the peer on it ends the backoff. A method whose ledger keeps failing holds up no other.

After a restart, settlements still pending in the journal are handed to their scheme when it activates, to post or void as its ledger says. One the scheme no longer holds is announced with a message it makes again from the ID.

## Incoming settlements

- **The books first.** On a scheme whose messages book what they state, the node checks that it can book a settlement before the scheme sees it, so the scheme's balance never moves without the books. Otherwise it answers 400 and nothing changes.
- **The scheme decides what it is worth.** The stub books the amount stated. A scheme whose ledger says what arrived, as a hub's, takes its message as a bare notice, which carries no data and only moves the channel on (`SettlementScheme::books_messages`): the node neither checks nor books the amount one states, and the scheme takes it whatever it states or carries, so it never refuses what the channel delivers. However the pair's books differ, a notice is taken, never sent again for ever with the channel stuck behind it.
- **From a channel, not a peering.** A `settlement` is taken from any authenticated node we hold a [settlement channel](#settlement-channels) with, open or closed, so a settlement that arrives after the peering ended, or after its channel closed, is still credited.
- **One on a scheme the node does not run is acknowledged, and books nothing,** as one its owner removed, which ran until such a settlement had its time ([Schemes and ledgers](#schemes-and-ledgers)), or one stored and left out at start. Refused, it would be sent again for ever, and the channel carries one settlement at a time, so the pair could never settle again. What it moved, if anything, stays on the scheme's ledger, which the scheme reads when it runs again; over a hub the message is a bare notice anyway. A scheme that stopped still refuses one (400) until the restart ([above](#the-scheme-interface)).
- **A downlink's first settlement opens its accounts,** in the room kept for them ([lazy-downlink-accounts](../decisions/lazy-downlink-accounts.md)). Until then it stands as one with nothing on them: owing and owed nothing, extended no credit, so it is asked to settle at once.
- A settlement the scheme sees on its ledger from any other node without accounts there cannot be from a peer: it is booked as the owner's deposit.

## Settlement channels

The node keeps a settlement channel with every node it has ever peered with (`crates/dassie-peer/src/channel.rs`). A channel belongs to the pair of nodes, not to a peering: it outlives the peering and the node's entry in the node table, and a new peering uses it again. It holds the sequences and our message on its way.

**Delivery is exactly once.** Each `settlement` message carries a sequence per sender and recipient that never resets. The sender sends one at a time, in order, again until it is acknowledged, across restarts, never dropping it; one that got no answer goes again as soon as the node answers us at all ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#message-set)). The receiver applies only the next sequence, recording it in the batch that books it, acknowledges anything at or below the last one without applying it again, and refuses anything else. So a copy from an old peering is never applied twice, and none is lost. A bare notice, as a hub's, takes its place in the same order: it books nothing, so being applied twice would cost nothing, but the sender holds the channel until it is acknowledged, which is why the receiver takes every one.

**Open channels are bounded:** with core nodes under one bound, and with downlinks under their own ([Network](network.md#downlinks)). Once all of a kind are taken, peering with a new node of that kind is refused for capacity; nodes it holds an open channel with can still peer. So is peering with a node whose accounts would leave the ledger less room than the node's own accounts may need, since peers' accounts stay when their channels close ([closing-channels](../decisions/closing-channels.md)), or take more than its kind's share of what they leave: core peers and downlinks each have half, and accounts count as the kind they were opened as, for good, so however many downlinks come and go, the room for peering with core nodes stays ([account-budgets](../decisions/account-budgets.md)).

**A channel left idle closes, to disk** ([closing-channels](../decisions/closing-channels.md)). Once it has been idle for as long as a silent peer is kept, with a node we neither peer with nor ask, with nothing on its way and nothing of the node pending in the books, every scheme the node has accounts with seeing its ledger, and no scheme still needing it (`keeps_channel`, as a settlement hub does for a hold with funds), it leaves memory and frees its place. Its balance does not matter: the node's accounts stay with what they show, for a later peering to settle. Its row stays on disk with its sequences and whether the node once paid us a settlement of at least one largest packet, which tells a free receiver from a downlink that paid ([free-receivers-half](../decisions/free-receivers-half.md)). So delivery stays exactly once:

- **A peering opens it again where it was,** so our next message follows the last one the peer applied.
- **A settlement over it closed** is read against the sequence that stayed: applied once, and acknowledged again as a copy. The channel stays closed.
- **The schemes forget** what they kept of the node for its settlements (`on_closed`), and their state version changes with it, so the node writes the state without it and a restart does not bring it back: a ledger payment from it after the close is the owner's deposit.

## Settlement hubs

A peering over a [settlement hub](hubs.md#the-members-side) settles by funding the payer's hold toward the payee, which is all the credit the payee extends: it extends nothing beyond it ([settlement-hubs](../decisions/settlement-hubs.md)). Settling stays [on request](#settling-on-request), so the node's rules for settling are the same:

- **Funding a hold is a settlement.** The payer books it as its outgoing settlement, finalized once agreed statements show the hold grew. The payee books it as an incoming settlement only then, from what the hub says, as the XRPL scheme books what its ledger reports: the settlement message is a bare notice, with no data, which books nothing ([Incoming settlements](#incoming-settlements)).
- **The payer's hold is its credit.** The credit the payer extends to the payee is its hold toward it and its settlements to it on their way ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)): a `trust` transfer raises it before the funding's pending transfer is booked, so the books take a settlement the payer does not owe, and one back lowers it, Dr `assets/interledger/<peer>`, Cr `contra/trust/<peer>`, as the payee takes from the hold, never past what the peer may still owe us. Its settlement thus moves the payer's money into the hold without moving its room for the payee, and the payee's room for the payer grows by it.
- **What a payee takes moves nothing in its books.** The holds toward it from its peers are part of what it holds on the hub's ledger, so a redeem or a claim moves money within `assets/settlement`, and books only its fee.
- **A hold's reserve is the ledger's,** never booked, as the XRP Ledger's reserves are; opening a hold reconciles it out of the books.

## The stub scheme

Test money without an underlying ledger (`crates/dassie-settlement/src/stub.rs`). Its "ledger" is a number the checkpoint keeps; its first activation books a starting balance as the owner's deposit, and the owner may deposit more. It comes in two kinds, which its config chooses, and peers only with a stub of its own kind: **with commitments,** whose settlements fund holds as a hub member's do, for the tests of settlement, and **with unlimited trust,** which never settles, for every other test. No stub settles directly ([hubs-only](../decisions/hubs-only.md)).

Anyone peered over the stub can mint money: with unlimited trust, a peer spends without ever settling; with commitments, a peer's settlement is believed as stated. That is why it only ever holds test money, and why only a build with debug assertions runs it: dev, the end-to-end tests, the tests and the simulations. A release neither offers nor activates it, and one stored by a build that ran it is left as a disabled scheme is ([stub-in-debug-builds](../decisions/stub-in-debug-builds.md)).

**With commitments,** for tests of peerings over a settlement hub without a hub: given `{"commitments": N}`, the stub signs and takes promises per packet as a hub's member does ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)), so the node sends a commitment with each Prepare to a peer on it, checks one with each Prepare from it, and carries a claim in each reply to a Fulfill. Every stub with commitments is a member of one pretend hub, with an account in it, so nodes on it peer over it. It finalizes a settlement as it executes it; one still pending after a restart was never executed, so it is cancelled.

- **Packets.** It holds at most N of a peer's packets in flight, and N of ours to it, and refuses past that; a commitment is the Prepare's amount and condition, and a claim is fixed; it keeps a fulfillment until the payer's claim covers it, a bounded number per peer; and a packet that waits for the hub it decides at once without a fulfillment, as a hub whose final block passed the commitment's expiry with no claim for it. What it holds of packets is in memory, as the connector's packets are, so a restart forgets both together.
- **Holds.** It emulates the holds as each side sees them: our settlement to a peer funds our hold toward it once it executes, with at most what the stub can spend, which, as for a hub's member, leaves out what the peers' holds toward us hold and we have not taken, and the peer's settlement funds its hold toward us; a fulfilled packet is taken from the payer's hold as a redeem takes it, on the payer's side when it writes its claim for a Fulfill its books took, and on the payee's side when that claim arrives, or when it takes the packet as claimed on the hub. The room of a peer's hold toward us is what is left in it, less the packets of it taken and fulfilled that no claim covers yet, as a member reads it. Its state keeps the holds across restarts; a stub with unlimited trust has no state, and refuses one.

**With unlimited trust,** for tests whose subject is not settlement (the peer protocol, routing, payments, the wallet, the RPC): given `{"trust": "unlimited"}`, or an empty config, so that a stub added without one never settles, the stub says so (`unlimited_trust`), and peers on it never run out of room, never ask each other to settle on their own account and never settle. It has no commitments and no state.

- **Credit.** The node extends every peer on it the unlimited credit (`policy::UNLIMITED_CREDIT`), whichever node asked to peer, a downlink's accounts included; a trust account that holds less, from an earlier peering or a stored one from when the stub settled, is raised to it, as the scheme starts and as a peering is made, so no peering on it runs short. It is still bounded: at most an eighth of what an account's counters hold, so a peer's account takes far more payments than any test makes before they would overflow.
- **No settlement.** The node never asks a peer on it to settle on its own account. The one request at a peering's end ([above](#settling-at-a-peerings-end)) still goes out, and is answered that nothing is on its way: the node settles nothing when asked, so the end collects nothing. The stub prepares no settlement, and refuses a peer's.
- **Only with its own kind.** It writes a fixed marker as its peering info, request and response, and refuses a peer that does not write it: a node on it never peers with a stub that would ask it to settle, or wait for its settlements.

## Decisions

- [owner-ledger](../decisions/owner-ledger.md): the default trust line becomes the default credit.
- [real-money](../decisions/real-money.md): only test-realm ledgers activate.
- [external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md): what the node holds apart from how it settles, and the ledger authoritative for holdings.
- [peering-lifecycle](../decisions/peering-lifecycle.md): channels kept after a peering, and their limit.
- [closing-channels](../decisions/closing-channels.md): a channel left idle closes to disk, whatever the balance.
- [account-budgets](../decisions/account-budgets.md): core peers' and downlinks' accounts each in a share of their own.
- [lazy-downlink-accounts](../decisions/lazy-downlink-accounts.md): a downlink's accounts open once value moves with it.
- [ending-a-peering](../decisions/ending-a-peering.md): nothing settled for a peering's end.
- [synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md): no peering on a scheme before its first sync.
- [own-books](../decisions/own-books.md): each node decides from its own books, and the bound that holds whatever a peer does.
- [hubs-only](../decisions/hubs-only.md): peers settle only over hubs, so the credit is the hold, and nothing is extended beyond it.
- [ended-for-room](../decisions/ended-for-room.md): a pair parted for room stays apart for the owner's cooldown.
- [own-fulfills](../decisions/own-fulfills.md): the creditor asks, the debtor decides how much.
- [packets-in-flight](../decisions/packets-in-flight.md), [thresholds-in-packets](../decisions/thresholds-in-packets.md): thresholds in the largest packet.
- [settle-at-the-trigger](../decisions/settle-at-the-trigger.md): a peer is asked as soon as its room falls to the trigger, with the check as the backstop.
- [settle-when-funds-arrive](../decisions/settle-when-funds-arrive.md): a request the debtor could not fund in full is funded as soon as it can be.
- [unreachable-peers](../decisions/unreachable-peers.md): a link cut one way drifts no more than a crash.
- [hub-named-when-added](../decisions/hub-named-when-added.md): a hub scheme stores its hub's rules when it is added, so no later default moves it.
