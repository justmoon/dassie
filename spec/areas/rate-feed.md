# Rate feed

A core node's public, cheap statement of what it converts at: its snapshot, the node's quotes at one moment per settlement method its peers pay it on, each with a ladder of how far a swap can go before its price falls by more, signed by its node key ([peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md)). The node makes one at most once per change and caches it. Its peers get it over the peer protocol ([Network](network.md#snapshot-subscriptions)); anyone else asks once over UDP, with a request padded to the largest snapshot, and gets the cached bytes. Readers swap through nodes whose quotes make it worth it, which moves those nodes' holdings back toward balance.

`dassie-feed` holds the contract without IO: the snapshot's format, its numbers and its signature, the request and whether to answer it, a reader's side and how it prices a swap, and when to make a snapshot. A core node makes its snapshot in its engine, from its price table, answers requests on a UDP socket of its own, and names the socket's port where readers look ([the node side](#the-node-side)). `dassie market-maker` is a reader that trades on what it reads ([below](#market-maker)).

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-feed/`, `testdata/feed/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/own_snapshot.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/feed.rs`, `resources/systemd/dassie-feed.socket`, `crates/dassie-cli/src/market_maker/`
**Read with:** [Connector](connector.md#quotes-and-ladders), [Network](network.md#snapshot-subscriptions), [Identity](identity.md), [Encoding](encoding.md), [Node](node.md#runtimes), [Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#link-states), [Payments](payments.md#paying-stream-credentials), [XRPL](xrpl.md#amm-pools), [Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)

## What a snapshot says

- **When:** a sequence in milliseconds that grows with every snapshot the node makes, even across a clock that went back, and a time after which the snapshot no longer counts. No key: a reader knows the node it reads, from the peering or from where it learned of the node, and checks the signature against that node's key.
- **The grid:** the drops each ladder's amounts are for, strictly ascending and each below 100%. It is in the snapshot, so tuning it needs no new version.
- **Per method** the node offers its peers, a tag of its scheme's ID, a bid, an ask, and a ladder for each. A method the node offers but does not convert on now, for want of a fresh rate or a dollar token off its peg, is listed with both sides closed, so a reader tells it from one the node does not offer:
  - **The tag** is a truncated SHA-256 of the scheme's ID under a domain of its own (`method_tag`). A reader can use only methods whose schemes it runs, so it knows their IDs and computes their tags. Methods are in ascending order of their tags, each once, and a node never offers two methods whose tags collide.
  - **The bid** is the least the node gives, in its virtual accounting unit, for a unit of the method's ledger that comes in; **the ask** the most it asks for a unit that goes out; its fee and spread folded in. A quote of zero closes its side: the node does not convert it.
  - **The bid ladder** says, for each drop of the grid, the most that can come in on the method with its last unit still given at least the bid less the drop. **The ask ladder** says the most that can go out with its last unit still costing at most the ask over one less the drop: what a unit of the virtual unit buys, less the drop. A swap's position on the ask ladder is what has come in, valued at the top-of-book price: the amount in times its method's bid over this ask, in this method's units. So both ladders are read by what has come in, and where a swap stands on each needs nothing but the quotes. A closed side's ladder is all zero.

The promise is only that a pair's bid over ask never says more than the node gives, at any size both ladders cover ([peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md#decision)): a packet gets at least the rate of the levels its last unit falls in, counting the swap's packets before it, since a node prices a packet at its last unit. How a node keeps it is its own; the format carries no curve, no spot and no fee.

## Numbers

Every number is defined by integer arithmetic alone, so every reader decodes a snapshot to the same values, and each has one encoding, which decoders check (`src/number.rs`):

- **A quote** is a 32-bit unsigned float of Dassie's own (`Quote`), normalized, its word zero the closed side. A bid rounds down and an ask up, so neither says more than the node gives.
- **A ladder** is a 16-bit float for its first amount and an 8-bit step for each later one: a power of two in sixteenths of the amount before, through a fixed table in fixed point (`STEP_FRACTIONS`, which a test checks against its definition), rounded down. The encoder takes each step from the amount before as decoded, the largest that does not exceed the true amount, so rounding never compounds; of the steps that give one amount, the smallest is its encoding. An amount above a later one is taken as the later one. So a ladder's amounts never decrease, fit a `u64`, and are never above what the node said, at most a step low; one whose first amount rounds to zero is all zero.

## Wire format

Canonical OER ([Encoding](encoding.md)), starting with a version byte; `src/snapshot.rs` has the layout. There are two datagrams, a request and a snapshot. A snapshot's bytes are the same whether a node answers a request with them or sends them to a peer, so it signs one and caches it.

- **One datagram.** Every datagram fits in `MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES`, which one packet carries on any path IPv6 runs on, so a snapshot is never fragmented. The bound on methods is the most for which the largest snapshot fits, which constant assertions check: one method more would not fit. A node lists at most one method per ledger of its realm, and a constant assertion says those fit.
- **Every value has one encoding.** Decoding refuses anything else, so a snapshot's bytes are exactly what was signed, and a decoded datagram re-encodes to its bytes (a property test). Decoders never panic, whatever the bytes, and every list is bounded before it is read.
- **Versions.** A datagram of another version is refused. Within a version, no layout may change once a release carries it: readers such as market makers run software of their own, outside the node's release ([overview](../overview.md#frozen-surfaces)). A change is a new version byte, and a snapshot of the new version is signed under a new OID. The snapshot is version 2, which took the place of an earlier feed's layout while no release carried it ([peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md#decision)).

## Signing

A snapshot is signed by the node key (Ed25519) over the OID of its version, `…1399.3.2` ([Identity](identity.md#object-identifiers)), followed by every byte before the signature, the version byte first, so its signature never passes for a link state's or anything else the node signs. The OID is not sent: readers know it.

A reader verifies by ZIP-215 over exactly the bytes received ([Identity](identity.md#node-key)), refuses a weak key, and checks against the key of the node it reads: it knows the node's identity, both keys checked against its ID, from the peering or from where it learned of the node, a link state or the discovery document, so the snapshot carries no key at all ([post-quantum-node-id](../decisions/post-quantum-node-id.md)). It then takes a snapshot only if its sequence is after the last one it took and it has not expired (`SnapshotReader`, or `take_snapshot` for a caller that keeps the last sequence itself), so a replayed or delayed snapshot never sets quotes back.

A snapshot is public, signed but not encrypted: it says nothing the node's packets would not.

## Requests

Anyone may ask a node for its snapshot over UDP, once. A request is padded with zeros to exactly the largest snapshot's size, and the answer is the cached snapshot, so a node never sends an address more than it got from it: a spoofed request reflects one to one, and nothing amplifies it. The node keeps nothing per address, no cookie and no table; a budget on answers per second, a token bucket on the caller's clock, bounds what all requests together cost it (`src/answer.rs`). A datagram that is not a request, of another size, with padding that is not zero, from an address an answer cannot go to (port 0, or an IP that is unspecified, multicast or broadcast), or over the budget, gets nothing, and only a request answered costs budget. A node that offers no swaps, a periphery node among them, answers no request.

## Reading a snapshot

A reader prices a swap from one method to another as the one's bid over the other's ask, walked along the one's bid ladder and the other's ask ladder as steps (`src/swap.rs`), each indexed by what has come in: the bid ladder by the amount, the ask ladder by its worth at the top-of-book price. Within the levels both have reached, a unit in gets at least the bid less its level's drop, times one less the other's, over the ask; a step ends where a level of either ladder does. Every step rounds against the reader. `Snapshot::swap_out` gives the least an amount gets, and nothing for an amount beyond what both ladders cover; `Snapshot::swap_at_least` gives the largest swap whose every unit still gets a rate the reader names, such as its own curve and costs ask for.

A node prices a packet at its last unit, so the walk's sum is what a swap gets sent as one packet per step, not as one packet across steps. A packet across steps gets at least the rate of the step its last unit falls in, on all of it: `Snapshot::packet_out` gives that, the minimum a sender sets on each packet, from where the packet starts in the swap and its size, and `Snapshot::step_end` where the step a packet starts in ends, so that a sender keeps each packet within one. Property tests check them against the ladders' promise exactly.

## Publishing

A node's quotes move with every conversion, but a snapshot costs a signature and a push to every subscriber. So a node makes one at most once per `FEED_MIN_INTERVAL_MS` while its quotes change, and otherwise a heartbeat every `FEED_HEARTBEAT_MS`, so readers know the node is alive and its quotes still hold. A change within the interval is made when the interval ends, never lost. A snapshot counts for a few heartbeats, so that one or two lost on the way do not leave a reader without quotes (`src/schedule.rs`). The engine pushes to its subscribers on the same schedule ([Network](network.md#snapshot-subscriptions)).

## The node side

The engine makes the node's snapshot, and the node answers requests with it on a socket of its own.

- **Only a core node that offers swaps has a snapshot:** its owner lets it (`offer_swaps`), and it is no periphery node, which forwards nothing. A node whose owner turns swaps off has none from its next step, and answers no request.
- **Made in the engine, deterministic.** What a snapshot says is a pure function of the price table, the methods and the time (`own_snapshot.rs`, `Content::of`); `OwnSnapshots`, the engine's `SnapshotSource`, decides from its inputs and the engine's clock alone when to quote again and when to sign, and the core asks it at the end of every step. A simulation sees every snapshot a node makes.
- **Counted, not logged.** Every datagram taken, request answered, request over the budget, datagram ignored and send that failed is counted (`FeedStats`), never logged: a flood of requests costs the node its bytes and a decode each, and no journal lines. The node logs only whether it has the socket, in the line that says it listens ([Node](node.md#logging)); its owner reads the counters instead ([below](#what-the-owner-sees)).

### What it publishes

- **The connector's quotes and ladders** ([Connector](connector.md#quotes-and-ladders)), from the price table as the connector converts at and what the owner held when it was built, on the default grid (`DEFAULT_GRID_PPM`), in basis points, each drop rounded up, since a drop said smaller would promise more than the ladders give. Each bid becomes a quote rounded down, each ask one rounded up, and each ladder is rounded down.
- **Per method the node offers its peers:** the schemes of the settlement offers in its link state ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#settlement-offers), [Network](network.md#settlement-methods)), by their tags. One on a ledger the table leaves out, for want of a fresh rate or a dollar token off its peg ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-peg-guard)), is listed with both sides closed: quotes of zero and all-zero ladders. An owner book, which peers with nobody ([Settlement](settlement.md#owner-books)), is not listed, though the table prices it, nor is any ledger no peer can pay the node on. Two methods whose tags collide, or two the table prices as one asset, are neither listed, since a bid over an ask cannot price them ([peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md#accepted)); a test checks that no two schemes' tags collide.
- **Made again at most once per change.** The engine compares what the quotes are made from, the table, the methods and the largest age of a rate, at every step, and quotes again at most once per `FEED_MIN_INTERVAL_MS` however often they change; it signs only when the quotes changed or the heartbeat is due, and hands out the same bytes in between. A change it holds back sets the engine's next wake, so it is made when the interval ends, without a step to prompt it.
- **Its validity** is the schedule's, or the moment a rate the table uses ages out, whichever comes first. A snapshot made after that lists every method closed until the engine builds the table again. While the engine is down, the node has no snapshot, and a reader's last one runs out.
- **The sequence** is the engine's clock in milliseconds, made to grow ([Publishing](#publishing)). Nothing persists it: across a restart of the node the clock has moved on, and a clock set back across a restart holds a reader's next snapshot back until the clock passes the last sequence it took, which a heartbeat later replaces.

### What the owner sees

Nothing in the journal says whether anyone asks, so the node's status does: `GetStatus` carries the endpoint ([RPC](rpc.md)), and `dassie status` and the wallet's network page show it ([Node](node.md#the-dassie-command), [Wallet](wallet.md#pages)).

- **Whether it answers, and where:** the socket's address, and whether the node has a snapshot that counts now. A node without the socket reports none; a periphery node reports one that does not answer, and `dassie status` and the wallet leave it out, since a periphery node answering none is no fault.
- **The counters since the node started:** datagrams taken, requests answered, requests over the budget, datagrams ignored, and sends the socket refused.
- **Cheap and bounded.** The endpoint adds to atomic counters as it goes, and the status reads each on its own, with no lock on the datagram path, so two may be a datagram apart. It is a fixed set of numbers, and names nobody's address.
- **They live as long as the socket,** so a restart of its service keeps them, and a restart of the node starts them over. Nothing of them is persisted.

### Bounds

Everything the endpoint holds is bounded by a named limit: a datagram it reads by `MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES` (a longer one is read one byte over and refused); the answers by the budget, `ANSWERS_PER_SECOND` and `ANSWER_BURST`, in `Policies` so tests can shrink them; and what it caches by one snapshot. The methods of a snapshot fit its bound by construction: the node lists at most one method per ledger of its realm, and a constant assertion says that the most ledgers a realm has fits; a snapshot with more is an assertion that fails, never a list cut short.

### The socket

The endpoint has a UDP socket of its own, apart from QUIC's: its datagrams are not QUIC, and a socket of its own keeps the two from ever reading each other's traffic. Its default port is `DEFAULT_FEED_PORT`, 7768: a fixed port, so that owners open one rule in their firewalls and readers find a node that names no port at all; above 1024, so that a node without privileges binds it; and one no service is registered for.

- **Under systemd** it comes from `dassie-feed.socket` (`ListenDatagram=7768`), taken by name like the others ([Node](node.md#runtimes)). Unlike QUIC's, it is optional, since its port may be taken: `dassie.service` only wants it, a node without it warns and runs, and an update that brings the unit enables it. Without it, the node's peers still get its snapshot over the peer protocol.
- **Standalone,** the node binds the HTTPS listener's address at that port, or where `--feed-listen` says: the dev environment and its tests give each node a port of its own (`6000+N`, moved by the port offset), since many nodes share one host.
- A socket that cannot be bound leaves the node without the endpoint, and running.

### Finding a node

A reader needs the endpoint's port and the node's key; it takes the host from the node's URL.

- **The discovery document,** `/.well-known/dassie/node`, names the port, as `feedPort`, while the node answers: it has the socket and a snapshot that counts, beside the node's ID and keys ([HTTP](http.md#the-nodes-discovery-document)). `dassie market-maker` reads it there.
- **The link state** names it too, in extension type 3 ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#link-states)), so a node or tool that reads link states finds every core node's without asking each one: only a node with a URL whose owner lets it offer swaps names one, at its URL's host.
- Neither names a port the node does not serve: a node without the socket, without a URL, or whose owner offers no swaps leaves `feedPort` out and issues its link state without the extension.

## Market maker

`dassie market-maker` trades through one or more core nodes, its targets, when what a target's snapshot says a swap between two methods gives and an AMM pool on the XRP Ledger disagree by more than a margin, and so moves that target's holdings back toward balance, as passive rebalancing intends ([passive-rebalancing](../decisions/passive-rebalancing.md)). Peers settle only over settlement hubs ([hubs-only](../decisions/hubs-only.md)), so its nodes and its targets are members of the hubs of the pool's two assets, and a target converts between the two hubs' ledgers, which its snapshot quotes by their methods. An end-to-end test runs the whole loop against the fake ledger's hubs and pool ([Testing](testing.md#end-to-end)), and a VM of our own runs it on the testnet ([Market maker VM](market-maker-vm.md)). It is a client of nodes, not one: it holds no books, and every amount it moves is moved by a node or by the ledger. It reads one JSON config, checked whole before anything connects (`config.rs`), which names its targets, at most as many as a node pins, each once, and for each of its nodes the hub's ledger it trades on, whose scheme is the method a snapshot quotes it by, and the owner book that funds the hub; the decision is a pure function of the snapshots, the pool, the holdings and which targets both nodes are peered with (`strategy.rs`), and everything around it is bounded in time and size. Its redesign as one node that swaps with its targets is on the [roadmap](../roadmap.md#peer-swaps).

A target's curve counts each asset whole, the ledger of its hub with the owner book that funds it ([Connector](connector.md#inventory-pricing)), so its snapshot quotes more than spot for the asset it holds least of, wherever its own moves left that asset, and a trade the market maker makes through it moves it toward balance.

### Two nodes, each in one hub, pinned to every target

The market maker owns two periphery nodes, one for each asset of the pool: XRP on one, the pool's token on the other. Each is a member of the settlement hub of its asset, the testnet's XRP hub or its DSUSD hub, and runs the owner books that hub needs, which peer with nobody ([Settlement](settlement.md#owner-books)): the owner book of its asset on its XRP Ledger account, which funds the hub, and on the token's node the owner book of XRP too, which pays that hub's gas ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)). Both pin every target as an uplink, and no other node ([Network](network.md#pinned-uplinks)), and each peers with each target over its one hub, a method of their peering ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods)). A node's own endpoints never convert, so one node could not pay in one asset and be paid in the other through a target; two nodes, each peered with the target over the hub of its own asset, make the target convert between the hubs' ledgers, as its snapshot prices them. The targets are members of both hubs.

Two nodes suffice for any number of targets, since each keeps an uplink to every target. A trade picks one: the receiving node's incoming payment, on its hub's ledger, names its address beneath that target ([Payments](payments.md#receiving)), and the paying node, peered with that target itself, pays from its hub's ledger and reaches the address through it first. So the payment goes from one node to the target over one hub and from the target to the other over the other, and is converted by that target and no one else. Two nodes per target would each hold funds that only that target's trades use; two in all share their funds, and their bands, across the targets.

Each node's passphrase is in a file the config names. The market maker derives from it the node's XRPL account exactly as the node derives it ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-wallet)), refuses to start unless that is the account the node reports for its owner book, the token node's book one of the pool's token's issuer, so that what a rebalance delivers is booked there, and the hub's ledger the config names one of the same asset code and scale, since a hub's ledger reports no issuer. It keeps nothing of the phrase but the signing key in memory: no log line or error names a phrase.

### The loop

- **The snapshots,** asked for from each target, each on a socket of its own. It reads a target's node ID and key from its discovery document, refuses an ID that is not the key's, and takes the port from there, or from the config for a target that names none. It sends a request every `POLL_INTERVAL_MS` ([Requests](#requests)), takes datagrams only from that target's address, and takes a snapshot only verified under that target's key, newer than the last it took from it and unexpired (`feed.rs`, through `SnapshotReader`), so one target's datagrams never set another's quotes. It prices the swap between the two hubs' methods, each the scheme of the hub's ledger its config names, by their tags. A snapshot counts until its validity ends; with none current from any target, it holds, and decides again as soon as one arrives rather than a tick later.
- **Targets that do not answer.** A target whose discovery document fails is asked again a minute later, while the others trade, so one core node down at the start stops none of them. A target that turns out to be the node of an earlier one is traded through once.
- **The pool, the holdings and the peerings.** Each tick it reads the pool with `amm_info` at a validated ledger, over a WebSocket of its own; both nodes' holdings, with `ListLedgers` ([below](#bands)), and, while a rebalance waits to be booked, the paying node's own move between hub and book on its way, with `GetHubStatus`; and which targets each node lists as its neighbors with `ListPeers`, which is when the node takes an incoming payment beneath one.
- **One decision a tick,** logged in one line that names the target a trade goes through, and at most one action. It scores the trade through each target by what that target's snapshot says the trade's amount gets at least (`Snapshot::swap_out`), and takes the one that gains most beyond the margin; an amount beyond what the snapshot's ladders cover is not priced, and not traded. Targets that pay alike, as core nodes whose curves are at their cap do, take turns: of those, the first after the one it last traded through, so its trades spread over them rather than wait on one target's settlements. A target that is not a confirmed uplink of both nodes yet is passed over: the payee would refuse a payment beneath it, and the payer could reach it only through another target, which would convert first.

### What it trusts

- **The snapshot, only to decide to quote.** A snapshot says the least a swap sent as a packet per step gets; a STREAM payment's packets are cut otherwise, and fees and rounding fall per packet. So a trade that clears the margin by the snapshot is only quoted.
- **The quote, to decide to pay.** The receiving node creates an incoming payment on its hub's ledger beneath the target; the paying node quotes its STREAM credentials from its own hub's ledger with a fixed debit ([Payments](payments.md#paying-stream-credentials)); and the market maker pays only if what the quote delivers, swapped back on the pool, still clears the margin. The quote's rate is the least every packet must deliver, so the payment cannot do worse than the quote it was paid on. It follows the payment to its end, within a bound, and completes the incoming payment either way.
- **`SendMax`, to bound a swap.** A rebalance is one Payment from the full node's XRP Ledger account, the one its owner book is on, to the other node's, delivering the other asset with a `SendMax` of its own ([XRPL](xrpl.md#amm-pools)): it delivers all or nothing, and never spends more than `SendMax`, however the pool moved. What it asks the pool for is what a slippage's share less than `SendMax` buys at the price read, so it fails rather than pays a worse price, rounded down to whole drops or millionths of the token, as the other node's owner book books it: an exact price takes every digit a token's value has, and an amount that leaves the line or the pool more digits than the ledger keeps is rounded by the ledger, and refused by the fake ([XRPL](xrpl.md#pools-in-the-fake-ledger)). It signs only for a server on the testnet, checked at start and before each rebalance, as the node's account does ([XRPL](xrpl.md#real-money)): the nodes' keys open accounts of the same address on every network.
- **The pool's price, exactly.** Both checks price the swap back with `dassie-xrpl`'s exact pool arithmetic, its trading fee included, and round every conversion to the ledger's units down, so the market maker never counts on more than it gets.

### Bands

Each node has a band of what it holds of its asset, in its ledgers' units. What it holds is its owner's funds in its hub and in its owner book: the node moves funds between the two by itself, between the hub's floor and ceiling ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)), since its owner keeps what it receives on the own account, which the hub's funding sweeps beyond the ceiling into the book ([Payments](payments.md#where-received-money-is-kept)), so neither alone says what it holds. Kept in the hub, what a node is paid would pile up there, and its rebalances, which spend only the book, could spend nothing once its first book was spent. A move between them counts on the ledger it leaves until that ledger books it out, and on the other once that one books it in: a deposit is held in the book until the XRP Ledger applies it and credited by the hub after, and a withdrawal is held on the hub's ledger until the hub executes it and booked in the book once the account sees it arrive. The two ledgers book on their own schedules, so a move in flight is counted on neither rather than risk counting it twice: between the two, the holding reads short of it, never above. The band is the node's, whichever target a trade goes through, so the targets share it, and a rebalance is decided as with one target, before any trade and without looking at the targets. A trade never sends a node below the bottom of its band, and is made smaller to stay above it; it pays from the hub, which the node funds from its book as it pays. A node above the top of its band is rebalanced to the band's middle before any trade, spending at most what its owner book's account holds on the ledger now and its owner may spend there, less the fee's headroom for XRP: what is in the hub, or held for a deposit on its way, the account cannot spend. A band's middle above the most a node keeps in its hub leaves what a rebalance spends in the book. It delivers no more than the other node's band has room for, so the two never swap the same funds back and forth, paying the pool's fee each way. A rebalance that can move nothing, since the other node has no room or what it would deliver rounds to nothing, does not stop the trades, or the market maker would hold while the trade that makes room clears the margin: it trades instead, but only from the other node into the one above its band, and only while the other is within its own band. That trade lowers the node without room, so at the next decision the rebalance has room and comes first again; it never takes the other node above its band, so it never leaves both above theirs; and like any trade it takes no node below its band. With both above their bands, it holds. After a rebalance that paid, or may still pay, it decides nothing until the paying node's owner book has booked it, within a bound, since until then the node's holding still counts what it spent. It takes the rebalance as booked once the node's holding has fallen by at least the least it can have cost, its most less a slippage's share twice over, and by all of what the node's own move between hub and book has on its way besides, as `GetHubStatus` says: such a move lowers the holding by up to its amount while it is on its way, so it never passes for the rebalance. A move that was on its way already when the rebalance paid may then hold the next decision back until the wait's bound, never longer; a submit whose answer was lost is followed by its hash like any other. The two nodes' signing keys are the market maker's, so the account that withdraws is the one that funds the hub its node is paid on, and its node rarely signs anything itself; a sequence the two both took is rare, and the node recovers from one ([XRPL](xrpl.md#in-flight)).

## Test vectors

`testdata/feed/snapshots.json` pins the snapshot's wire format: tags, quotes, first amounts, the step table, ladders, the request, snapshots up to the largest the bounds allow, swaps priced from one, and datagrams refused, each with its reason, all signed with a fixed key. There is no other implementation to take them from, so the crate's own generator makes them (`examples/snapshot_vectors.rs`, which says how to run it), read by `tests/snapshot_vectors.rs`; Ed25519 signs deterministically, so running it again gives the same file. While no release carries the snapshot, it may still change with its vectors; after that, a changed vector is a new version.

## Decisions

- [passive-rebalancing](../decisions/passive-rebalancing.md): inventory pricing anchored at spot, a public statement of a node's prices that never says more than a packet gets, and market makers as periphery nodes.
- [peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md): snapshots of quotes and ladders per method, to peers over the peer protocol and to anyone on a padded request, in place of a feed of rates per pair with subscribers.
- [tigerstyle](../decisions/tigerstyle.md): deterministic, bounded, every decision a value.
