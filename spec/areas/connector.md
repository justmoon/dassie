# Connector

The node's ILP connector: it knows the node's endpoints (BTP clients, local endpoints, peers), routes each Prepare, answers ILDCP, swaps with its peers and decides when, books the packet's transfers in the [ledger](ledger.md), charges transit, limits what may be in flight, and resolves the transfers when the response comes back or the packet expires. This doc also covers the exchange rates it converts at, how the owner's inventory prices them, the BTP server and its tokens.

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-connector/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/packets.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/swaps.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/swapping.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/engine/core/tokens.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/http/btp.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/store/peer_settings.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/rates.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/node/rates.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/store/rates.rs`, `crates/dassie-node/src/rpc/rates.rs`
**Read with:** [Ledger](ledger.md), [ILP](ilp.md), [Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md), [Network](network.md), [Payments](payments.md), [Settlement](settlement.md)

The connector is sans-IO and bounded ([engineering](../engineering.md)), and nothing on the packet path panics on input. Its bounds and timing are `Config` in `config.rs`.

## Addresses and endpoints

Every endpoint has an ILP address and its slots, each a ledger and the [ledger](ledger.md) account its packets debit and credit there (`endpoint.rs`): a child has one slot, a peer one per settlement method of its peering. ILDCP answers each endpoint the address that is routed to it and the asset of the ledger its request came on.

- **Children** lie below the node's own address `<scheme>.das.<node ID>`: a random segment per BTP connection, and per ledger a STREAM receiver and a payer ([Payments](payments.md)). Children's addresses never overlap: neither is a segment prefix of the other.
- **Peers** are reached through the route table; a peer's address is only what ILDCP tells it, `<scheme>.das.<node ID>`. A peer's endpoint ID is never reused, so a late response cannot resolve a later peering's packet.
- **Downlinks** ([Network](network.md#downlinks)) are peers at a child address beneath ours, `<our address>.<node ID>`, which ILDCP tells them and which routes to them as to a child: the rest of the network reaches it through us, with no route of its own. A downlink sends only its own packets, so it has a child's share of the packets in flight, not a peer's; otherwise it is a peer like any other, for fees, conversion and the money in flight. A peer's kind changes in place (`set_peer_kind`): a downlink that turns core gets a peer's share and keeps its child address while its peering lasts, beside the routes to its own; a core peer that turns downlink moves to its child address. A core peer at its own address is reached beneath ours too (`set_beneath`), as after a restart one that was our downlink is, so that payment requests made out under its child address keep working for as long as it is peered ([promotion-to-core](../decisions/promotion-to-core.md)).
- **Request IDs** are unique per endpoint while in flight, whatever the slot. A peer's first outgoing request ID is random, so that after a restart new packets do not reuse IDs the peer still holds from before. A source that reuses an ID still in flight gets nothing done.

### Settlement-method slots

A peering has settlement methods, each on a ledger of its own ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)), and the peer's endpoint, one per peering, has a slot per method, at most `MAX_SLOTS` in use, which the node checks equals the peer protocol's `MAX_METHODS`, and at most `MAX_HELD_SLOTS` with those of dropped methods that wait for their packets. Each slot has its ledger, the peer's account there (a downlink's may open later, [below](#booking-a-packet)), its own [money in flight](#money-in-flight-with-a-peer), whether the method is over a settlement hub, its weight in the [draw](#routing), and whether it is available.

- **Every method carries packets.** A Prepare from a peer names the method it came on, and books on that slot; one on a method the endpoint has no open slot for gets T04. Our packets go out on the slot the [routing](#routing) picks, and its response comes back on the same.
- **Available** means the node may send packets on it: its scheme runs, sees its ledger and can receive a settlement, and the method is neither withdrawn for room nor being wound down ([Network](network.md#advertised-peers)). A hub scheme without an account of its own sees its hub toward a peer with one, which pays it in, so a newcomer spends what that peer owes it ([Hubs](hubs.md#the-members-side)). A hub scheme whose account only lags in reading the newest blocks it heard of, as it does for a moment after nearly every block, sees its hub too, so a packet does not leave its ledger for that; not while the hub is frozen ([Hubs](hubs.md#a-frozen-hub)), nor while the account catches up after a start, after falling so far behind that it reads anew, or while it reads its state anew. Only which method a packet goes out on reads it; a peer's Prepare on a method not available is still taken, but for one on a method being wound down, which the node refuses (T04) before the connector sees it ([Network](network.md#settlement-methods)). The node sets it from the peering's state whenever that or a scheme's state changes, and after each settlement check.
- **Methods come and go with the peering.** A method added gets a slot. A method dropped takes its slot out of the draw and refuses new Prepares on it; the packets in flight on it resolve or expire as any packet does, and the slot goes with the last of them, within the longest hold time. A method added again before then takes its slot up again, with its packets, and the slot comes last, as the method does in the peering. Over a hub, the scheme of a dropped method still hears what becomes of each of its packets, found by the slot's ledger, since a scheme its owner removes runs until no slot on its ledger holds a packet, and a while after ([Settlement](settlement.md#schemes-and-ledgers)): a payee keeps a fulfillment until the payer's claim covers it, the payer's reply to a Fulfill carries the claim, a Reject releases the commitment, and a packet that timed out still waits for the hub's decision, as on any method over a hub.

## Routing

- **Under `peer.`,** addresses are the link's, never routed: `peer.config` is ILDCP, answered from every endpoint, peers included; `peer.swap.<scheme ID>` is a swap ([below](#swaps)); any other gets F02. Each is handled before routing, so the rules below never see them.
- **Below the node's own address,** only children and downlinks, by whole-segment prefix; a downlink whose peering is ending takes no new Prepare. The node's own address itself is unroutable.
- **Beyond it,** the longest whole-segment prefix in the route table, which the peer protocol's routing fills with a few first hops per node ([Network](network.md#routing)). Its usable options are those that are an endpoint, are not the packet's source (so packets do not bounce between peers), are not draining, and have a [method available](#settlement-method-slots). A route whose only options are the packet's source answers T01: the source routes through this node and this node back through it, so one of them has not recomputed its routes yet, which passes ([route-back-is-temporary](../decisions/route-back-is-temporary.md)). Each packet's next hop, and the method it goes on, is drawn among them ([weighted-next-hops](../decisions/weighted-next-hops.md)), with a number the node draws from its RNG for every Prepare, so a simulation stays deterministic; nothing is sticky per destination or per payment.
  - **Among the options with a method available on the packet's incoming ledger,** while there is one: each weighted by the fees we earned on packets fulfilled through it on that ledger that kept it, and a share of the draws (`explore_ppm`) gives each the same chance. While none has earned, every draw is even. The packet goes on that method.
  - **Otherwise, among all usable options,** each as likely: the packet converts, on the drawn hop's available method with the best rate in the price table, rates on different ledgers compared at spot ([below](#comparing-outputs)), and its fee counts toward no hop.
  - **The weights** are per peer and ledger, on the peer's slots, in memory, and so bounded by the endpoints and their slots; a restart starts them from nothing. A slot's weight takes its fee at Posted, and halves once per `earnings_half_life_ms`, computed lazily at the next draw or fee. Rejects count for nothing, and room is never a weight: a hop short of room is still drawn, answers T04, and loses weight only as its earnings decay.
- **A method is chosen by structure, never by room** ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)): by the packet's incoming ledger and the methods available. A method short of room answers T04, or T05 for its money in flight, and no packet moves to another method or another hop for it. A route whose hops have no method available gets T04 too, as a want of liquidity that passes, not F02.
- **The owner's packets** never convert ([Ledger](ledger.md#the-owners-accounts)), so they draw only among the options with a method on their endpoint's ledger; with none, one that moves money gets F02. For a packet from one of the node's own endpoints, the connector says which ledger its first hop is on, not drawn: the route's first usable option by node ID with a method available on the ledger the owner asks to pay from; with none asked, or no option on it, the first usable option and its first available method; so a quote or a payment picks once the ledger it pays from, and each of its packets then draws among the options with a method on it.

## A periphery node

A periphery node ([Network](network.md#periphery-nodes)) has an address beneath each uplink, `<uplink's address>.<its node ID>`: what that uplink's ILDCP would tell it, which it derives rather than asks for, since an uplink's address and the rule are fixed. Its connector takes them all, as many as a periphery node may be pinned to: `MAX_CHILD_ADDRESSES` equals the peer protocol's `MAX_UPLINKS`, which the node checks at start ([Network](network.md#pinned-uplinks)). A packet's address is matched against each in turn. A core node keeps an address beneath each of its core peers too (`set_child_addresses`), which they route to it whatever its kind: one promoted into the core goes on being paid at those it handed out, and is paid beneath them until it uses its own address ([Network](network.md#the-core)).

- **Beneath any of them,** on any node, lie the node's own endpoints, as below its own address. A packet routed there goes on with its destination written below the node's own address, the only one its endpoints know.
- **It forwards nothing.** A Prepare from a peer that lies beneath none of its addresses gets F02. Its own endpoints' packets go out by the routes, through its uplinks.
- **It is paid beneath an uplink it is connected to:** its STREAM receiver's credentials name the address beneath an uplink confirmed now, the one the owner names if any ([Payments](payments.md#receiving)), so a payment's packets reach it through that uplink. Credentials handed out earlier keep their address, and reach it while that peering lasts.

## Swaps

A node swaps one asset for another with a peer over two settlement methods of their peering ([peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md)): a Prepare to `peer.swap.<scheme ID>`, which the peer converts and sends back on the method that names (`connector/swaps.rs`). A method's scheme ID is its ledger's slug ([Ledger](ledger.md#ledgers-and-amounts)). The swap is never routed, so the routing rules, the bounce rule and keeping the ledger among them, stay as they are; it goes back to the peer it came from because it is under `peer.`, not by a route.

- **At the peer (B).** A peer's Prepare to `peer.swap.<scheme>` on another method of their peering is transit from the slot it came on to the slot of the same endpoint that the scheme names, with the checks of any Prepare, in their order ([below](#handling-a-prepare)): expiry, the largest packet, conversion by the curve with the fee and the spread, F08 with the most that would pass, the money in flight, the books. It goes back with its destination, condition and data unchanged; B never reads the data. Its fee counts toward no hop's weight, as no converting packet's does. B refuses with F02 when it does not offer swaps, or the scheme is not a method of the peering; with T04 when that method is not available. Offering swaps is the owner's setting, on by default (`offer_swaps` among the peer settings, which the engine hands the connector with `set_offer_swaps` at start and whenever it changes); a periphery node never offers them, since it forwards nothing.
- **At the node that swaps (A).** Each ledger the node runs has a swap endpoint, a local endpoint on the owner's `local/swap` sub-account, which no packet reaches by its address. A's swap goes out from the swap endpoint of the ledger it pays on, to the peer it chooses, on that peer's method there (`send_swap`): the owner's own funds, which never convert at A, refused with F02 unless the peering has a method on that ledger and the address names another of its methods. A Prepare from a peer to `peer.swap.<scheme>` that comes on the method its address names is one of A's swaps coming back, whether or not A offers swaps: it goes to the swap endpoint on that ledger, as a packet into the owner's own endpoints, unconverted and uncharged. So a swap that names the method it came on is never forwarded; with no swap endpoint there it gets F02.
- **Matched by the condition alone.** The swap endpoint's data is the node's own, small, fixed and versioned (`engine/core/swaps.rs`): the least the swap takes back, and a nonce. Its fulfillment is an HMAC-SHA-256, under a secret drawn with the node's first swap and held only in memory, over the ledger it is to come back on and the data, and its condition is that fulfillment's SHA-256. A swap coming back is fulfilled only if the condition its data derives is its own (else F05) and it brings at least its minimum (else F04): a peer that changed the data or sent it back on another method gets nothing. So the node keeps no table of swaps in flight; one in flight at a restart is lost, as any packet in flight is.
- **What the swap service gets.** Each swap sent has one outcome, fulfilled or the code it was refused with by this node, the peer, or the swap endpoint on the way back, kept in a bounded queue the service reads, with the amounts an F08 named and what the swap endpoint fulfilled on the way back. A swap the peer took the way back of and then let expire reads as rejected, though it paid the node: so each swap sent is kept until its outcome, at most `MAX_SWAPS_IN_FLIGHT`, for what came back, and one whose swap endpoint went with its scheme ends as timed out.

### The swap service

With whom, how much and when the node swaps (`engine/core/swapping.rs`), as [peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md) has it, from the snapshots its peers push it, which the node takes only verified under the peer's key and newer than the last ([Network](network.md#snapshot-subscriptions)), read with `dassie-feed` ([Rate feed](rate-feed.md#reading-a-snapshot)).

- **Who swaps by itself.** A core node while its owner lets it swap (`swapping`), toward its curve's targets; a periphery node only toward targets its owner sets (`swap_targets` among the peer settings: a share per asset, by the ledger its holding counts toward, none by default). Any node swaps when its owner asks; it reads its peers' snapshots only while `swapping` is on.
- **When it gains.** It swaps `a` for `b` with a peer while what the peer's snapshot promises, valued at the node's own curve, clears one plus its own costs. The curve is the price table's (`PriceTable::value_rate`): the rate a peer's packet from `b` to `a` would get, at the worst state the packets in flight can leave and after the swap's own move, without the spread, which the node charges its peers and not itself; a periphery node's aims for its owner's targets in place of equal ones. The costs are the node's alone (`swap_costs_ppm`): over a hub, the hub's fee on an operation per least settlement, and the XRP Ledger's fee of a deposit from the owner book over the least such a deposit moves, a step of the hub's target, a token hub's converted at spot; nothing on a method over no hub.
- **With whom and how much.** For each ordered pair of a peering's available methods, the peer whose snapshot promises the most at the node's threshold (`Snapshot::swap_at_least`), the first by node ID on a tie. A snapshot's promise is for one swap from the state it was made in, so the service keeps, per peer and pair, how far its swaps went along the snapshot's walk, and starts the next snapshot where its swaps still in flight leave it.
- **Packets.** Each stays within one step of the ladders (`Snapshot::step_end`), the largest packet and the most the pair's last F08 named, carries what the snapshot promises it as its minimum (`Snapshot::packet_out`), and goes only while that minimum, valued at the curve after the node's own move with every swap in flight counted against it, still clears the costs. At most `PACKETS_PER_PEER` are in flight with a peer, two of the largest packets, within the cap on money in flight with it ([below](#money-in-flight-with-a-peer)); no room is set aside for swaps.
- **Refusals.** T04, T05 and every other temporary or relative error back off from the peer, as STREAM does: the first wait, then twice the last, up to the longest; a fulfilled swap starts it again. F08 sizes the pair's packets by the amounts it names. F02 and every other final error stop the swaps with the peer until its snapshot changes, and so does one that came back below its minimum (F04 from the node's own swap endpoint).
- **The owner's swaps** (`SwapWithPeer`, [RPC](rpc.md)) go the same way, on the peer and pair the owner names, up to an amount, at the rate the owner names in place of the curve's, which must be one: a rate of nothing, as one left out reads, would take whatever the peer's own snapshot says, and is refused. A packet goes while what the snapshot promises it, rounded down, is no less than its size at the owner's rate rounded down too, and its minimum is the larger of that promise and its size at the owner's rate rounded up, so the node never takes less than its owner asked. At exactly the rate promised, a packet so asks up to a unit more than the snapshot promises it, which the peer's own price, bounded below by the snapshot, gives on all but the smallest packets. Each runs for at most `OWNER_SWAP_MS` and what it has in flight then; one that came back short waits for the next snapshot. Each ends with what it paid, what came back and why it ended: all of it paid, no more promised at the rate, refused, or out of time. The service swaps by itself with none of the peers an owner's swap runs on.
- **What it did** is counted, never logged, for the owner (`GetSwapStatus`): the peers it backs off from now and those it waits on for their next snapshot, so that its quiet tells from a pause; swaps sent, fulfilled and rejected, those rejected that paid the node all the same, backoffs, refusals, swaps short of their minimum or too large, outcomes dropped unread, and per ledger what the node paid and was paid. Everything is in memory and bounded: the peers it reads (`MAX_SWAP_PEERS`), the swaps in flight, and the owner's swaps at once (`MAX_OWNER_SWAPS`). It looks at its peers and pairs again only when something it decides by changed: a snapshot, an outcome, a backoff that ended, an owner's swap, the node's prices, holdings or settings, or what a step persists, such as a peering or a scheme's state.

## Handling a Prepare

The checks run in the order of `connector/prepare.rs`; the first that fails answers the source at once with a Reject from this node. A swap runs the same checks in the same order, its next hop the slot its address names rather than a route's ([Swaps](#swaps)). A packet that would convert is refused unless it comes from a peer, has fresh rates, and still carries money after conversion; transit out on a ledger the owner holds too little on gets T04 ([below](#inventory-pricing)). The money checks come last: the cap on money in flight with the peer, then a token's spending cap, then the ledger.

**Expiry.** The outgoing packet expires one message window before the incoming one, and at most the longest hold time from now: the window leaves time for the response to travel back, and the hold bounds how long the next hop holds it. A packet that leaves too little time is refused (R02).

## Amounts and conversion

- **Same ledger:** what goes on is what came, less our fee.
- **Across ledgers, from one peer to another:** what came less our fee, at the curve's rate for the packet: spot, moved by the owner's inventory after the packet's own move, less the spread ([below](#inventory-pricing)). A peer's swap is such a packet, from the peer to itself ([Swaps](#swaps)).
- **Across ledgers, from a peer into the owner's own endpoints:** what came, at spot, uncharged; one to one in the same currency, without a rate, but never to or from a ledger that converts nothing now, such as a dollar token's off its peg, or its hub's ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-peg-guard)). Only a packet from a peer converts.

Conversion is exact integer arithmetic, rounding down, never floating point: at spot on rates relative to the dollar (`fx.rs`), on transit at the curve's rate (`pricing.rs`). It uses only rates learned at most an hour before `now` and not after it, so a clock that went back cannot make a rate young; without them the packet gets T00.

## Exchange rates

`rates.rs` holds the decisions without IO, parsing and combining sources' answers; `node/rates.rs` is the fetcher that runs them a minute after the last cycle, and whenever the sources or the currencies wanted change.

- **A rate per currency, relative to the dollar,** kept for the node's ledgers' assets and the currencies an owner may see totals in. Each rate ages on its own.
- **The median of several sources.** A currency's rate is the median of the sources that cover it and answered in this cycle, once more than half of them did; with an even number, the middle two must agree closely, and the rate is their midpoint. A new node's sources cover every currency three times (below). So one wrong source can move a rate only within what the other two say, never beyond, and with one silent, the other two must agree. A currency the owner's sources cover twice gets a rate only while both answer and agree; one they cover once takes what that source says, as the owner chose (the dev environment does). A currency without a rate this cycle keeps its last one until it is stale, and the node warns.
- **Exact decimals.** Answers are read in exact decimal arithmetic; an answer that does not read leaves the source out of the cycle, and it is simply asked again at the next.
- **Across restarts,** each currency's last rate is persisted with when it was learned, so a restarted node converts at once while its rates are fresh.

### Sources

The owner keeps an ordered list of sources of four kinds: Coinbase's format, which covers every currency and which any server may serve (as the dev environment does); Kraken's ticker, the last trade of every currency against the dollar on its market; Bitstamp's ticker, which covers XRP; and the European Central Bank's daily reference rates, which cover the fiat currencies. A new node starts with one of each, so three sources cover every currency, and their data comes from three upstreams: for the fiat currencies Coinbase's data provider, Kraken's market and the ECB, for XRP three exchanges' markets. All four are free and need no key. A node upgraded with the sources it started with gets the ECB; an owner's own list is left alone.

The ECB publishes once a business day, so its rates are up to a day behind the markets; as one of three, a rate of its outside the two live ones leaves the rate at the nearer of them. Its answer is taken only while its newest day is no older than its calendar allows (`MAX_ECB_AGE_DAYS` in `rates.rs`, the gap at Easter), and never one of a day to come: a daily source that stopped reads as silent rather than as a wrong rate.

Sources are asked through the node's outbound client, under its rules for outbound requests ([Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md)).

The exchanges' terms license their data for personal or internal use only; sources whose terms allow a node's use are not built yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#open-rate-sources)).

### Inventory pricing

Transit from one peer to another converts at a rate the owner's inventory moves from spot, so that what flows through a node brings its holdings back toward their targets without the node trading ([passive-rebalancing](../decisions/passive-rebalancing.md)). The curve and the table are in `pricing.rs`.

- **The curve.** Each ledger that converts (an active scheme that converts now, on a hub's ledger only while the peg guard of its owner book lets it, and a fresh rate) is valued at spot, whatever settles it, by its currency, from what the owner's group holds on it. The owner's funds are counted per asset, each asset whole: a settlement hub's ledger counts with the owner book that funds the hub, the node's XRP in the testnet's XRP hub with its XRP on the XRP Ledger, as one holding ([passive-rebalancing](../decisions/passive-rebalancing.md)), since the node moves funds between the two by itself ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)), though a move on its way counts on neither until it lands; every other ledger, an owner book that funds no hub priced beside it among them, is an asset of its own. Each asset's share of the total against an equal target, one per asset, gives it a weight `w = (1 − k) + k · target / share`, which every ledger of it takes, and a pair converts at spot times `w_in / w_out`, less the spread; a pair of ledgers of one asset converts at spot less the spread. At target the rate is spot less the spread. The bonus for bringing in what the node lacks is capped at `1 + k` above spot; the penalty for taking it out is not, and out of a ledger the owner holds too little on, nothing converts (T04), however much of its asset the owner holds on another.
- **An owner book is priced as a ledger,** though it peers with nobody and no packet in transit comes in or goes out on it: its rate is what a peer's packet into the owner's own endpoints on it converts at, and the pairs it is in are the curve's like any other's.
- **Each packet is priced at the worst state the packets in flight can leave, after its own move.** Each ledger of the asset it comes in as counts what is posted and every pending credit (as if every packet bringing that asset in were fulfilled), but none of its pending debits, and the ledger it comes in on the packet's own amount too; each ledger of the asset it goes out as counts what is posted less every pending debit (as if every packet taking that asset out were fulfilled), but none of its pending credits, and the ledger it goes out on less what the packet takes out at spot too; every other ledger counts what is posted. So a packet pays its own impact, as on an AMM; no packet held, rejected or in flight beside it ever improves its price; and a round trip never gains, whatever is in flight. A packet reads its group's posted and pending totals per ledger, each in constant time, and is priced without allocating.
- **`k` is the owner's,** beside the fee and the spread, bounded as they are, and on by default (`DEFAULT_REBALANCE_PPM`).
- **The price table.** The engine builds a table whenever the rates, the owner's funds on a ledger, the peer settings or whether a scheme converts change, and when a rate in it ages out. It holds each ledger's spot rate and what the owner's group held on it, the charges and `k`, and for each ordered pair of ledgers with fresh rates the rate a vanishingly small packet got then: at the worst state the packets in flight could leave, as a rate out per in, with when its older rate was learned. A real packet gets that or less: its own impact, near target about `4k` times its share of the node's value, and whatever moved since. A ledger without a fresh rate is left out, so its pairs get T00, and so is one that converts nothing now, as a dollar token's, or its hub's, while the token's peg guard pauses it ([XRPL](xrpl.md#the-peg-guard)): transit on it alone still passes, and the node's snapshot quotes its method no more; two ledgers in the same currency need its rate too, since the curve values every holding.
- **No rate is T00, no funds T04.** A pair whose rate at target (spot less the spread) rounds to nothing or does not fit, or whose rate or result does not fit, is answered as a missing rate is (T00): the cause is the rate, not liquidity. T04 is only for too little on the way out: nothing once the packets in flight and the packet's own move are counted, or so little that the penalty rounds the rate to nothing.
- **One realm.** A node runs the ledgers of its own realm alone, so a table prices the ledgers of one realm, and its bound is the most ledgers a realm has (`MAX_PRICED_LEDGERS`), hubs' included; a table built from two realms is a bug, and an assertion stops it. A [snapshot](rate-feed.md#bounds) quotes at most one method per ledger the table prices, and the most a table prices fits one, which a constant assertion checks.
- **Exact and bounded.** Shares are known to twelve decimals, rounded down, and every product stays within 128 bits or goes through a 256-bit intermediate: a rate is never better for the sender than the curve, and none overflows.

### Quotes and ladders

A snapshot of the node's prices, for peers that swap with it ([peer-swaps](../decisions/peer-swaps.md)), is a quote per settlement method and a ladder per quote, made from the price table without IO (`quotes.rs`, `PriceTable::quotes`). Its wire format is the [rate feed's](rate-feed.md#wire-format), and so is [how a reader walks it](rate-feed.md#reading-a-snapshot); the engine makes the node's snapshot from them ([Rate feed](rate-feed.md#what-it-publishes)).

- **A bid and an ask per method,** in the node's accounting unit per unit of the method's ledger: what the node pays for a unit coming in, the fee and the spread folded in, and what it charges for a unit going out. A swap from one method to another is priced as the one's bid over the other's ask. A side the node does not convert is zero: a method whose ledger the table leaves out (no fresh rate, or a dollar token's peg guard), an ask on an asset the owner holds nothing of, and a bid with no other method's ask open, which has nothing to swap into.
- **A ladder per side,** on the grid of drops the snapshot carries, exactly as many as its levels (`GRID_LEVELS`), each a whole number of basis points (the default is `DEFAULT_GRID_PPM`): on the bid, the most that can come in while its last unit still gets at least the bid less the drop; on the ask, the most that can go out while its last unit still costs at most the ask over one less the drop, where a swap's position is what has come in valued at the top-of-book price, the amount in times its method's bid over this ask. A ladder never shrinks with the drop. Ladders count each asset whole, a hub's ledger with the owner book that funds it, as the curve does: a swap the hub's own ledger cannot fund is refused for liquidity (T04), as any packet is.
- **The promise.** Within the levels a reader's walk has reached, a unit in gets the bid less its level's drop, times one less the ask's, over the ask; a packet is priced at its last unit, after the swap's packets before it. So each packet of a swap gets at least the rate of the levels its last unit falls in, on all of it, however the swap is split, sent one packet after another or together, and whatever the packets in flight when the quotes were made do: its own impact, the worst state in flight, the cap on a pair's weight ratio and the coupling of every asset through the owner's total value are all counted against the reader. The promise is for one swap between two methods from the state the quotes were made in; other traffic, the reader's other swaps included, can move the price either way.
- **How ours keeps it.** Each asset's bid uses its weight at its most and every other asset at its least, its ask its weight at its least and the others at their most, so that no pair's state in flight is better for the reader. The bid is clamped at `1 + k` times the least ask weight of the other methods quoted, so that no product of a bid and an ask passes the curve's cap; for two assets the clamp is exact, and with more it understates the pairs whose other side is not the most plentiful. A bid ladder holds the owner's total at most `k` times what came in below the bid's state, the most the other side's bonus can pay out more than came in. An ask ladder holds it at most what came in above the ask's state; the curve takes from the asset going out what came in, valued at spot, and an ask ladder bounds that by what came in at the top-of-book price over the least bid of any other method per unit of spot.
- **Rounded against the reader.** A bid rounds down, an ask up and a ladder down; shares round as the curve's can never be worse; and the bid is lowered by a margin that covers the curve's rounding of its rate toward any ask, so that for two assets at a vanishingly small size, the bid over the ask is the table's price less the fee within that rounding. A publisher may round quotes further against the reader by up to `QUOTE_TOLERANCE_PPB`, which the ask ladders allow for.
- **One method per asset.** A bid over an ask cannot say that two methods of one holding convert at spot less the charges, so a snapshot that named two would mislead: asking for it is a bug, and an assertion stops it. The node's snapshot lists neither of two such methods ([Rate feed](rate-feed.md#what-it-publishes)).
- **Checked against the curve.** A property test publishes the quotes as a snapshot, wire rounding and all, at random skews, holdings in flight, numbers of assets, `k`, fee and spread, and has `dassie-feed`'s reader price random pairs as far as the ladders reach: a packet per step of its walk, one packet, or packets cut anywhere, each with the minimum the reader sets on it, sent one after another or together, through the curve. No packet gets less than its minimum.

### Comparing outputs

A packet that could go out on more than one ledger, such as to a next hop it can pay on several, keeps the ledger it came in on where it can, and otherwise converts on the output where it gets the most relative to spot (`PriceTable::best_output` in `pricing.rs`), so that the choice follows the curve: the output whose asset the node holds the most of above its target gives the most.

- **By value, not by number.** A rate is out per in, in the output ledger's own units, so rates on ledgers of different currencies or scales do not compare as numbers. Each candidate `b` is ranked by `rate(a, b) · spot(b) / spot(a)`, with `spot` what a unit is worth: one at spot, more when the curve pays a bonus, less when it charges. Since `a` is common to all, this is the rate per what a unit of `b` is worth, compared exactly by cross-multiplying in 256 bits, with no rounding.
- **The packet's own rate.** Each candidate is priced as [above](#inventory-pricing), with the packet's own amount and what is in flight, so the comparison ranks what the packet would get, not what the table said; with nothing moved, it ranks the table's prices.
- **Staying is never compared.** With the ledger the packet came in on among the candidates, the packet goes out on it, converting nothing, with no rate needed, however much the node holds of another: a method on the incoming ledger is chosen by structure, not by rate ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)). The ranking is only among the outputs that convert, when none keeps the ledger. Each needs a fresh rate on both ledgers and the funds to go out on, or it is passed over.
- **Ties** go to the ledger first in the closed list's order, whatever the order the candidates came in, so the choice is deterministic. No candidate with a rate is no choice: the caller answers as a conversion without a rate or funds would.

### The owner's total

The total is the sum over the active ledgers of the owner's posted balance ([Ledger](ledger.md#the-owners-accounts)), converted into the owner's display currency. It is shown only while every rate it needs is fresh: with one missing or stale, there is no total, rather than a wrong one.

## Booking a packet

A packet that moves money books linked pending transfers, all or none, each referencing its trace ID ([Ledger](ledger.md#transfers)):

```
same ledger:     Dr source          Cr destination      in − fee
across ledgers:  Dr source          Cr Ls:revenue/fx    in − fee
                 Dr Ld:expenses/fx  Cr destination      out
our fee:         Dr source          Cr Ls:revenue/fees  fee          (only if fee > 0)
```

`Ls` is the ledger of the source's slot the packet came in on, and `Ld` of the destination's slot it goes out on; each slot books on its own accounts. A packet without money, or between two endpoints that share an account, books nothing. The ledger is sized for three pending transfers per packet the connector may hold.

A limit refusal becomes T04, a full table T03. So a peer can push packets to us only while it stays within the credit we extend, and the owner's endpoints only while the owner's group has funds.

**A downlink without accounts yet** has slots without an account. A packet from it that carries money is refused as for want of liquidity (T04), as one with no credit is. A packet that moves money to it opens its accounts on the slot's ledger first, through the node (`OpensAccounts`), and books on them; if the books then refuse the packet, they are taken back, so it leaves nothing; with no room for them, the packet is refused with T04 ([lazy-downlink-accounts](../decisions/lazy-downlink-accounts.md)).

## Responses and expiry

- **A Fulfill that matches the condition** posts the transfers, and its bytes go upstream unchanged once the batch that posted them is on disk ([Node](node.md#the-engine)).
- **A peer's Fulfill that does not match** is taken for a stale resend of an earlier packet's with the same request ID: it is dropped, and the packet waits for its own response or its expiry ([connector-timing](../decisions/connector-timing.md)). Any other wrong Fulfill gets F05 at once.
- **A response after the outgoing expiry** counts as a timeout, whatever it says.
- **A Reject, or a response that does not decode,** voids the transfers.

**Failing early.** The connector fails a packet before its expiry only when it surely did not reach its peer (the peer protocol says the connection was refused, or the peer, or its transport before the peer saw the packet, answered with a status that means it did not take it, [Peer protocol](peer-protocol.md#statuses)), or when its next hop is removed. A peer that refused it for its rate gets T05, so that the sender backs off; every other such packet T01. A Prepare that may have reached its peer is left to expire: a peer that fulfilled a packet we already failed would bear the loss.

**Packets to a peer over a hub wait for the hub.** A peer may still win a claim on a settlement hub for a packet that went on a method over the hub after its outgoing expiry, so when such a packet times out, the connector does not reject it upstream: it waits, until the incoming packet's expiry and at most one message window past its own, for the node to learn from the hub's final block stamped at or after the commitment's expiry whether the peer won a claim (`settle_hub`). With the fulfillment the claim revealed, the packet is fulfilled upstream and the books follow the hub; without one, it is rejected. A Fulfill from the peer is on time until then, even when it comes before the wait began. At the end of the wait it is rejected whatever the hub says, and what the peer then wins on the hub is a loss the node books ([settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#4-peering-over-a-hub)). Over a hub of version 2, a Reject from the peer without its release of the packet's commitment, and a definite failure the peer answered with a status, never reach the connector: the packet times out and waits as one that expired, since the peer could still claim it ([signed-rejects](../decisions/signed-rejects.md)). This is the one exception to the timeout of [connector-timing](../decisions/connector-timing.md).

**Fulfills are final.** A packet is posted when it is fulfilled, and stays posted whatever its upstream does with the Fulfill ([Ledger](ledger.md#fulfills-booked-as-final)). The peer protocol sends a response to a peer again until it arrives or a bounded time has passed; a Reject that never arrives has the effect of expiry upstream.

**Draining.** While a peering ends, its endpoint is draining: no new Prepare goes to it or is taken from it, but packets in flight finish. The peering ends once nothing is in flight to or from it ([Network](network.md#termination)). Before that, while the node collects from a peer it decided to end and has not told, the peer's endpoint is refusing: its new Prepares get T04 "insufficient liquidity" before any other check, so a peer that probes with a packet another check would answer can tell, and ours still go to it ([ending-a-peering](../decisions/ending-a-peering.md)).

**Unreached peers.** A peer our messages surely do not reach is refused the same way, on its own: once an ILP packet of ours to it surely did not arrive, its new Prepares get T04 before any other check, until it answers a request of ours, while ours still go to it. Across a link cut one way, a Fulfill of ours would never arrive and count only in our books, so each try of the sender's would drift the books apart by a packet; refused, they drift by at most what was in flight ([unreachable-peers](../decisions/unreachable-peers.md)).

## Money in flight with a peer

The connector caps, for each peer's method and each way, what the packets in flight carry, at two and a half of the [largest packets](#the-largest-packet) (`IN_FLIGHT_PACKETS` and `Config::max_in_flight`). A Prepare that would pass it gets T05, from which STREAM backs off as from any temporary error, and never moves to another method.

- **Why it exists.** A node that crashes with Prepares in flight never learns that its downstream fulfilled them, so each one drifts the two peers' books apart by its amount. Without a cap, a crash with many STREAM connections over one peer drifted the books far enough that settlement between them stalled for good. The cap is the most one crash, or one link cut one way ([unreachable-peers](../decisions/unreachable-peers.md)), costs a link, and what a link survives is counted in it ([Settlement](settlement.md#credit)).
- **Two and a half.** A payment's STREAM keeps two of the largest packets in flight; the half packet left takes smaller ones, such as a quote's probes while a payment fills the link. Without it those probes are refused and the quote fails.
- **Per method,** since each method's books are on a ledger of their own, and a crash drifts each by what was in flight on it: what a peer can cost is the sum over its methods ([settlement-methods](../decisions/settlement-methods.md)).
- **Amounts, not packets,** so packets from parts of the network with a smaller largest packet just fit more of them.
- **Each way, with our own number.** A crash of either side raises what our books say the peer owes us, so each node bounds both directions with its own cap, knowing nothing of the peer's.
- Packets without money always pass, and a packet passes alone, with nothing else in flight that way.

## The largest packet

A Prepare above the largest packet `P` gets F08, with the amounts. `P` is how much the owner is prepared to lose on a link, so every threshold of a link is counted in it: the cap above, and the settlement trigger, the least settlement and the least default credit ([Settlement](settlement.md#positions-and-thresholds)). It is one value for the node, as is the default credit; per-ledger values are not built ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#not-built-yet)).

A Prepare that a conversion would forward above `P` gets F08 too, naming the most that would pass in its own units at the rate it got; with one `P` for every ledger, a packet of `P` in a dear asset converts to more than `P` of a cheap one. Forwarded, it could find too little room toward the next hop while the hop, its trigger counted in `P`, sees too much to ask for a settlement; refused for liquidity (T04), its sender would send it again as it is, for good.

## Fees and spread

Only what passes from one peer to another is charged: the owner's fee, and on a conversion the owner's spread, both in parts per million (`charges.rs`), and priced by the [curve](#inventory-pricing). Packets to or from the node's own endpoints are never charged.

- **The fee rounds down,** so a packet too small to carry one passes free. Rounded up, it would take a payment's last packets whole, and strand the payment short.
- **The spread and the curve are not booked per packet:** their gain is the net of the FX accounts, and negative when the node pays above spot for what it lacks.
- **The fee is what a peer earns us,** and what weighs it as a next hop ([Routing](#routing)), on a packet that keeps its ledger; a converting packet's, a swap's among them, counts toward no hop. Fees default to 0.
- New charges apply from the engine's next step.

## Spending caps

A BTP token may carry a cap: at most an amount `A` per window `W`, refilling continuously (`cap.rs`).

- **What counts** is a packet that debits the token's account and credits an account outside the owner's group: a peer. A payment to the node's own receiver, or to another token's client, moves no money out of the owner's funds and does not count.
- **The bound.** The cap is a bucket of used amount·time that drains at `A`, with what packets in flight hold counted as if fulfilled. A Prepare that would overfill it is refused (T04; F08 if it alone exceeds `A`). So what a token spends in any period `t` is at most `A + A·t/W`.
- **Per account, in the connector,** so all of a token's connections share one cap, and it is checked before the ledger books the packet.
- **Checkpointed with the journal rows that change it.** The journal alone cannot rebuild a refilling window: two histories with the same recent rows can have used different amounts. A replay of the token's journal since the cap was set is the reference the tests check the checkpoint against.

## The BTP server

ILP clients such as `ilp-plugin-btp` connect with `btp+wss://:<token>@<host>/btp` (BTP 2.0, RFC 23); the codec is in [ILP](ilp.md).

- **The token is the authentication.** Clients are not browsers, so there is no Origin check. Failed logins are rate-limited per client address; only failed ones count, so honest clients may reconnect as often as they need.
- **Money moves only in ILP packets:** a BTP Transfer is refused.
- **A client that goes away** has the packets in flight to it failed upstream; packets it sent keep running, and their responses are dropped. Revoking a token closes its connections, and from that moment they take no new Prepares, so none passes without the token's cap while they close.

### BTP tokens

- A token's secret is shown once at creation; the database keeps its SHA-256 ([secrets-at-rest](../decisions/secrets-at-rest.md)).
- A token is bound to one ledger that an active scheme serves, and gets a sub-account under the owner's pool on it, by the token's number. Numbers are never reused, and a revoked token keeps its account, since the journal refers to it.
- **A client connects only while a scheme serves the token's ledger.** A stored scheme that does not run, as one disabled or of the other realm, keeps its tokens, for the owner to see and revoke, but they connect no client until it runs again: two clients on its ledger could otherwise pay each other and move the books of a ledger the node does not run ([Settlement](settlement.md#schemes-and-ledgers)). Such a refusal is not a failed login.

## Local endpoints

The STREAM receivers, payers and swap endpoints are in-process endpoints that the engine adds when a scheme activates and removes when it goes ([Payments](payments.md), [Swaps](#swaps)). A packet forwarded to one is delivered in the same step. A payment from the node to its own receiver goes through the connector like any other, through a bounded queue drained at the end of the step, so nothing recurses.

## The packet trace

The connector keeps a bounded record of recent packets, keyed by trace ID, which the journal's packet references point into, and streams its changes to live subscribers. Events nobody polled are dropped and counted.

- **A record joins its packet's hops.** Each node keeps its own trace, so a record says what ties it to the same packet's records on other nodes: the Prepare's execution condition, which is a hash already and the same on every hop, so a client that reads several nodes' traces joins one packet's hops by it. A packet refused before it decoded has none. The record also keeps the ledger the packet came in on and the one it went out on, so each hop says what it was worth on either side.
- **Records are fixed in size and inline,** the destination address cut to a bound, so tracing a packet allocates nothing on the packet path.
- **The connector knows a peer only by the node's number for the peering.** The node adds the peer's node ID to each record it hands out, as far as it still has that peering, so a record names its hops by node rather than by a number that means nothing elsewhere.
- **An owner may keep the records.** The node's opt-in [event log](node.md#the-event-log) writes each record the trace hands out to a file, so a packet's hops can be joined after the fact from several nodes' files.

## ILP-over-HTTP

ILP-over-HTTP (RFC 35) is not built: the connector has no HTTP endpoint ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#not-built-yet)).
