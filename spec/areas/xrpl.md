# XRPL

The node's account on the XRP Ledger testnet and the settlement schemes on it, `xrpl-testnet` in its XRP and the token scheme in a dollar token on a trust line, as `xrpl-testnet-rlusd` in RLUSD and `xrpl-testnet-dsusd` in DSUSD: the wallet, derived from the owner's passphrase; the account as an external asset, which connects, signs and follows the ledger for the schemes on it; the Payments the schemes sign to settle; how every transaction that touches the account is booked once; how they survive restarts; and the gates that keep real money out. Beside them, the ledger's AMM pools: a pool's price, read with `amm_info`, and the Payment that swaps through one, for a client that converts on the ledger.

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-xrpl/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/fake_ledger.rs`, `testdata/xrpl/`
**Read with:** [Settlement](settlement.md), [Ledger](ledger.md), [Identity](identity.md)

The crate does no IO and reads no clock. The account says what to send its ledger and the node feeds it what comes back, over the one connection it keeps for the account; the account tells the schemes on it what the ledger said, and they book it, through the node ([Settlement](settlement.md#external-assets)). Its constants are in `lib.rs`, `account.rs`, `scheme.rs`, `token.rs`, `rlusd.rs`, `dsusd.rs` and `amm.rs`.

The account is an external asset (`account.rs`). `xrpl-testnet` is a scheme on it that settles in its XRP (`scheme.rs`). The token scheme settles in a dollar token on its trust line to the token's issuer, with its XRP as gas (`token.rs`, [The token scheme](#the-token-scheme)): `xrpl-testnet-rlusd` in RLUSD ([RLUSD](#rlusd)) and `xrpl-testnet-dsusd` in DSUSD ([DSUSD](#dsusd)). Each runs alone, or any of them together on the one account.

## Real money

Real money is refused at every level until the conditions of [real-money](../decisions/real-money.md) hold, which the [roadmap](../roadmap.md#real-money) lists. The scheme meets the XRP Ledger's part of them (the reserve is never booked, an account's creation is a deposit, it settles whole drops, books fees and checks results and senders); mainnet also needs a memo type of its own. Until then:

- The realm `live` is refused ([Node](node.md)), a scheme on a ledger outside the test realm does not activate ([Settlement](settlement.md#schemes-and-ledgers)), and there is no mainnet scheme.
- **The testnet account refuses other networks.** Before it reads the account, it checks that its server reports the testnet's network ID. A server that reports another, or none, stops the account as failed, and every scheme on it with it, before anything is booked, written off or signed. An owner who points it at a mainnet server gets a failed scheme, not mainnet XRP booked or signed for with a testnet wallet.
- A server that reports more XRP for the account than the books can hold stops the account as failed.
- **Only the testnet resets** ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)). Taking a ledger below the newest seen for a new chain, and writing off an account gone with it, are gated on the network ID by a check of their own (`resets` in `account.rs`), not only by the one that keeps the account on the testnet. On any other network, a lower ledger stops the account, and so does the absence of an account that a scheme on it held anything of, with nothing written off. Letting the account run on another network can never make a lower ledger acceptable.

## The wallet

The wallet is the original's, byte for byte, so the same passphrase finds the same account and its funds. It is derived (`keys.rs`) from the node's settlement entropy at `node/settlement-module/xrpl-testnet/seed-testnet` ([Identity](identity.md)) as an Ed25519 family seed. The 64 wallets of the identity chain's golden vectors pin the derivation. That path belongs to the node's first XRPL testnet account (`FIRST` in `account.rs`), whichever schemes settle through it; further accounts get paths of their own when they are built, and no path gets a new meaning. The node's deposit address is this account's.

## The account

The account owns what the schemes that settle through it would otherwise race for ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)):

- **The connection:** the network check, the newest ledger seen, testnet resets and the faucet ([below](#following-the-account)). The server and the faucet are the account's config, which the scheme that activates it names; a scheme that names others while the account is active is refused.
- **What it signs:** the next sequence and the transactions in flight: Payments of XRP or of a token, and `TrustSet`s. A scheme asks the account to sign; the account hands out sequences in order, so no two schemes on one account take the same one, and keeps each transaction with the scheme that signed it. It signs nothing unless it is synced and knows its sequence, has room for another transaction in flight, the amount is above zero, the network fee is at most its ceiling (while it is higher, it waits), and what it pays in XRP, its fee and any reserve it takes fit above the reserve after what is in flight; a Payment of a token, only while the line holds it after what the scheme has in flight, as the ledger last said.
- **What it spends as gas:** the XRP a scheme that does not settle in it spends, a token Payment's fee or a `TrustSet`'s fee and its line's reserve, for each token scheme on it alike. While `xrpl-testnet` settles from the account, that XRP is on its books, so the account signs for another scheme only while the owner's funds on `xrpl-testnet+xrp`, which the node tells it before each call that may sign, cover the gas, with the gas already in flight ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)). While the books stopped `xrpl-testnet`, its books still hold the XRP, and the account spends none of it as gas until the scheme runs again after a restart. Otherwise (no `xrpl-testnet`, or one the owner removed) the XRP is off the books, and only the reserve bounds it.
- **What it holds,** as the ledger last said: its XRP and the reserve, and the balance of each trust line a scheme on it follows, with what the line's issuer froze: the line's own freeze and deep freeze, and a freeze of every line from the issuer's account as last read for a scheme, on the chain the account follows: a write-off forgets the issuers' accounts with the lines. The node shows it from the account, never from the books ([Settlement](settlement.md#external-assets)), and the wallet shows it to the owner ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)).
- **What it learnt,** which it tells every scheme on it, in order: each validated transaction that touches the account, with the scheme that signed it if it was in flight; each line its schemes follow, and that it is synced; that a transaction can never apply; that the account is gone; each ledger that closes; each transaction a scheme looked up, under the hash the scheme asked for; and an issuer's account a scheme asked for. A transaction that names more XRP than the books can hold for the account, from the stream, the history or a lookup, stops the account before any scheme books it.
- **What a scheme not on it misses of its own.** While a scheme is stopped or removed, a transaction of its that can never apply is remembered as never sent, and one the ledger validates is kept with the scheme that signed it, both written with the account's state. So is what the account told a scheme of its own in the input whose bookings the books refused, which stopped it: the node lets the scheme go before the account forgets what it told, the scheme's checkpoint stays from before that input, and the account's state is written on while another scheme on it runs. Back on the account, the scheme cancels the first when it reconciles its settlements, and books the second as its own when it reads it again from its cursor, never as a withdrawal. What it does not read again (a history that does not reach back), the account tells it with the sync, which made its books match the balance, or the line, through suspense; it looks that up by its hash, and books it as its own once found, as one read behind its cursor is, so what suspense booked comes back out. It books it by what the books hold pending for it, which is what it signed, never by what a server says it delivered. It keeps that it never read it with its state, since a restart before the lookup would otherwise take it for one booked already. Until then it signs nothing more, nor while the account tells it of its own (as a token scheme may sign a `TrustSet` on what it is told), so it never has more of its own in flight and kept than it can have in flight, even once a refused input's are kept. The account tells the second again for that scheme alone: the schemes on the account were told it when the ledger validated it, and `xrpl-testnet` booked its fee then, so it books no fee twice.
- **The write-offs a scheme missed.** The account counts the times it was written off ([below](#following-the-account)), kept with its state, and each scheme keeps the count it last saw with its own. A scheme not on the account through a write-off (stopped or removed during a testnet reset) sees another count when it is back, after a restart or added again: before it follows the account, it writes off what its books held through suspense, drops its cursor, which is on a chain gone, and syncs again on the new chain, a token scheme making its line again. So no cursor of an old chain holds the account, or the issuer's account it reads, behind a ledger the new chain has not reached. What the account kept for it went with the chain: a settlement that validated on the old chain is cancelled, as one in flight is.

**Sharing has one cost.** Until a stopped scheme's transaction that was never sent is dropped, the other schemes' transactions with later sequences cannot apply: they are looked up and cancelled past their last ledger, and those schemes back off settling, as after any cancelled settlement ([Settlement](settlement.md#outgoing-settlements)). It costs a window of ledgers and a backoff, and nothing is paid twice. One address for the owner to fund, and one supply of XRP for every scheme, are worth it ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)).

**Each scheme keeps its own cursor** into the account's transactions, and tells the account where it is. On each connection the account reads its history from the earliest cursor of its schemes, and each scheme books only what is past its own, so a scheme that stopped reads again what it missed, and the others are not held back. A scheme that joins the account once it has read its ledger is read for on a new connection, which reads the lines it follows too ([Settlement](settlement.md#external-assets)).

## The Payment it signs

A settlement is a direct `Payment` to the address the peer gave when peering, with one memo of the scheme's memo type naming our node ID: of XRP for `xrpl-testnet`, of its token for a token scheme ([The token scheme](#the-token-scheme)), which also signs a `TrustSet` for its line (`codec.rs`).

- **Byte for byte as xrpl.js, which the original used, signs it.** Golden vectors (`crates/dassie-xrpl/tests/data/vectors.json`, made by `testdata/xrpl/generate-vectors.mjs`) pin the codec.
- **The settlement's ID is the transaction hash.** The message for the peer is the hash, so it can always be made again from the ID.
- **The memo type and what a `settlement` message means are a contract between peers.** Both sides of a peering must agree, so they change only with the peer protocol version. Each scheme has its own memo type, a constant of the scheme, so a Payment is never taken for a settlement of another.

## Tokens

The codec and the API's parser also cover tokens on trust lines, such as RLUSD and DSUSD (`amount.rs`, `codec.rs`, `api.rs`), for the [token scheme](#the-token-scheme).

- **Amounts are exact.** A token's value is decimal, a mantissa of sixteen digits and an exponent, held in the ledger's one canonical form and never as a float. A value the ledger cannot express exactly, in text or in bytes, is refused rather than rounded, so what the node reads is what the ledger holds.
- **Whole units convert exactly.** A value becomes the whole units of a ledger rounded down, toward minus infinity, so the books never hold more than the line. Units become a value only when the ledger can express it exactly (sixteen significant digits), so the node never signs an amount other than the one it booked.
- **The transactions** are a `Payment` without paths, of XRP or of a token, and a `TrustSet`, which sets a line's limit and flags. A Payment is never partial: the encoder asserts it and the decoder refuses it, so a payment delivers its whole amount or fails. It may name a `SendMax`, the most it spends: of the other asset, which the ledger converts through its default paths, such as an [AMM pool](#amm-pools), or of the same token, to pay an issuer's transfer fee; never XRP for XRP, which the ledger refuses. The schemes' Payments name none. Golden vectors from xrpl.js pin both kinds, a `SendMax` either way between XRP and a token, and token amounts at the edges (the least and the largest value, sixteen digits, zero), byte for byte.
- **A line reads the same from either side.** The ledger keeps a line's balance from the side of the account whose ID is the lower number, and its flags in a low and a high half. The parser gives the balance, the limits and the flags (NoRipple, freeze and deep freeze, as each side set them) from the side asked for, so no caller depends on which number is lower; a line whose limits name its accounts out of order is refused.
- **What a transaction did to a line,** from its metadata: the line before and after, created from zero or deleted to zero. A settlement, a deposit, a withdrawal and an issuer's clawback, which is no Payment, all show as a change of the line's balance. A Payment's delivered amount is read when it is a token.
- **Reading the ledger:** the line itself (`ledger_entry`), as of the ledger the account was read at, and the issuer's account (`account_info`): its global freeze and its transfer rate. Each answer names the ledger it is as of, so a scheme can take only what is [newer](#following-the-account).

## AMM pools

The ledger's AMM holds two assets in a pool and swaps one for the other at a price that keeps the product of what it holds from falling, with a trading fee that stays in the pool. The crate prices a pool, reads one and signs the Payment that swaps through one (`amm.rs`, `api.rs`, `codec.rs`), for a client that converts on the ledger, such as a market maker. No scheme swaps: a settlement delivers the asset its ledger books, and the node trades nothing on the ledger.

- **Prices are exact, and round to the pool.** What taking an amount costs is rounded up, and what paying one yields rounded down, to what the ledger can express: whole drops, or a token's sixteen digits, as the ledger rounds. So a swap at the price never lowers the pool's product, its fee included, and a unit less buys less than asked. Nothing passes through a float, and amounts too far apart in size for exact arithmetic are refused, never rounded.
- **`amm_info` is read exactly,** as everything the account reads: two amounts of two different assets, not below zero, an LP token that the pool's account issues, a trading fee the ledger takes, and the ledger it is as of; anything else is no answer. The LP token's currency is derived as the ledger derives it.
- **A swap is a Payment with `SendMax`** of the asset paid in (`sign_send_max`), which delivers all of its amount or fails: `SendMax` is the payer's bound on the price, and a pool short of the amount fails the Payment rather than paying part of it. It may pay the payer's own account. Whoever signs keeps the sequence. To a scheme that follows the account, a swap it did not sign is a withdrawal of what it spent and a deposit of what it got, as any such transaction is ([below](#following-the-account)).

## The token scheme

A token scheme settles in a dollar token on the account's trust line to the token's issuer, and books on a dollar ledger of its own (`token.rs`). One code serves every token: what tells one token scheme from another is its token (`Token`), the scheme's ID, its memo type, the issuer, the currency code and the ledger, all constants of the scheme. Two run on it, [RLUSD](#rlusd) and [DSUSD](#dsusd), each beside the other and beside `xrpl-testnet` on the one account. The ledger's asset is `USD`, at par, so it converts one to one with every other dollar ledger ([issued-assets](../decisions/issued-assets.md)); its unit is a millionth of a dollar, which a token's sixteen digits hold exactly for any balance a node could hold. It pays its fees and its line's reserve in the account's XRP, as gas ([above](#the-account)).

**The line comes first.** The scheme counts as synced, and the node peers on it, only once the ledger shows the account's line to the issuer with rippling disabled on our side and the largest limit the ledger can express, so no one moves our token through our account and no settlement fails for reaching the limit ([synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md)). A line missing, or made outside the node otherwise, is set so with a `TrustSet`, signed once the account holds the XRP for its fee and its reserve and the issuer is known to exist, to which a `TrustSet` would only fail. It books nothing on the scheme's ledger, so the account submits it at once. One that fails waits for the next twice as long as the last, as each costs a fee. The first sync with the line as it needs it books what the line holds as the owner's deposit, and sets the cursor. Each scheme follows its own token's line alone: a node that runs both token schemes holds two lines, and pays a reserve for each.

**Settling.** A settlement is a Payment of the token of its issuer, without paths and never partial, with one memo of the scheme's own memo type naming our node ID; its ID and message are the hash, as for XRP. It settles whole millionths. `prepare_settlement` is also `Unavailable` while the issuer froze our line, while the issuer froze its token for every holder, while it charges a transfer fee (which a Payment without `SendMax` cannot pay; not provided for yet, [roadmap](../roadmap.md#not-built-yet)), until the issuer's account was read, and while the gas cannot pay the fee.

**Booking a validated transaction** by what it did to the line, read from its metadata:

- **One of ours:** posted when it succeeded, voided when it failed. Its fee is gas: on `xrpl-testnet+xrp` if that scheme runs on the account, otherwise on no books.
- **A settlement from a peer:** a successful Payment to us that delivered the token of our issuer, whose memo has the scheme's memo type and names a peer, from the address that peer gave when peering. It is booked by what it delivered, in whole units, never by what it stated.
- **Anything else that raises the line** is a deposit; **anything that lowers it** a withdrawal, an issuer's clawback included, which lowers the line without a Payment. Each moves the books by what the transaction did to the line, its balance before and after as the metadata says, never by what the scheme last saw of the line, which after a restart is nothing.
- **A Payment that names us as the issuer of what it pays,** meaning any issuer we trust, may say it delivered a token of our own account rather than of the token's issuer. It is no settlement: what it did to the line is booked as a deposit. That credits the peer less than arrived, never more.
- **Other currencies, other issuers and XRP** move nothing on this ledger: the other token scheme's settlements included, and a Payment of our token with another scheme's memo, which is a deposit.

After each, the books hold the line's balance in whole units; a remainder below a unit, which anyone can send, stays on the line and is booked once it adds up to one.

**The issuer's acts,** read from the ledger, each shown in what the account holds:

- **Our line's flags** at each sync, with the line itself, so a freeze from before the node first saw the line is known; then with every transaction that touches the line, the issuer's freezes included, which the account's stream carries. A line set otherwise since, such as rippling enabled outside the node, is set again.
- **The issuer's account,** for a global freeze and a transfer fee, at every settlement check, taking only an answer as of a ledger at or past the newest the account has seen ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)). Both change only the issuer's account, which our account's stream never carries.
- **A frozen line** settles nothing, and still receives. **A line that cannot receive** stops what peers pay us too: one deep-frozen or under a global freeze, and one whose issuer charges a transfer fee, which no node of the scheme pays, as each is `Unavailable` while the fee holds. The scheme says so (`can_receive`), and the node asks no peer on it to settle, counts a peer's time short toward withdrawing it but never toward ending it, and leaves the scheme out of its link state ([Settlement](settlement.md#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)). The scheme stays synced throughout: it still sees its ledger.

**A testnet reset** takes the line and what it held, which is written off through suspense with the account, and the issuer's account with them. The scheme makes the line again once the account and the issuer are back. An issuer that does not come back at its address leaves the scheme unsynced until a release names the new one.

**Whom it peers with.** A peering is on one scheme ([Network](network.md#peering)), so two nodes that share several XRPL schemes settle in one of them. A token scheme's node peers on it only with nodes that run the same scheme: RLUSD and DSUSD are two ledgers, never one.

## RLUSD

`xrpl-testnet-rlusd` is the [token scheme](#the-token-scheme) in RLUSD, Ripple's dollar token, on the account's trust line to RLUSD's testnet issuer, and books on `xrpl-testnet-rlusd+usd` (`rlusd.rs`). Its issuer, its currency code and its memo type are constants of the scheme.

**Funding.** The owner sends the account XRP for gas, from the faucet as for `xrpl-testnet`, and RLUSD, which Ripple hands out only by hand; the wallet's Receive names both ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)). Dev (`rlusd-settlement`, on the fake ledger only), the end-to-end tests and the simulations use the fake ledger's issuer ([below](#tokens-in-the-fake-ledger), [Dev](dev.md#scenarios)).

**Whom it peers with.** The testnet's bootstrap nodes run both `xrpl-testnet` and RLUSD ([bootstrap-network](../decisions/bootstrap-network.md)), so a node that runs only RLUSD peers with them on it, and one that runs both settles with them in XRP.

## DSUSD

`xrpl-testnet-dsusd` is the [token scheme](#the-token-scheme) in DSUSD, a testnet dollar token of Dassie's own, on the account's trust line to DSUSD's issuer, an account of Dassie's on the testnet, and books on `xrpl-testnet-dsusd+usd` (`dsusd.rs`). Its issuer, its currency code and its memo type are constants of the scheme; the memo type is a contract between peers, as every XRPL scheme's is ([above](#the-payment-it-signs)). It runs beside RLUSD, not in its place: test RLUSD trades far from a dollar against XRP on the testnet's AMM, while DSUSD's XRP/DSUSD pool there is kept near an exchange's XRP/USD price, so a dollar booked at par is worth about a dollar in XRP ([dsusd](../decisions/dsusd.md)).

**Funding.** DSUSD has no faucet. The owner sends the account XRP for gas, from the faucet as for `xrpl-testnet`, and gets DSUSD only by paying XRP for it on the testnet's XRP/DSUSD AMM pool: from any XRPL wallet, a Payment to the account whose amount is DSUSD and which spends XRP crosses the pool, and the scheme books what arrives as a deposit, as anything else that raises the line. The issuer mints DSUSD only into the pool, never to a person, so no one takes the pool's XRP with DSUSD they did not pay for. The node buys none itself. The wallet's Receive says so ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)). Dev (`dsusd-settlement`, on the fake ledger only), the end-to-end tests and the simulations use the fake ledger's DSUSD issuer ([below](#tokens-in-the-fake-ledger), [Dev](dev.md#scenarios)).

## Following the account

The scheme books **every validated transaction that touches its account once, in ledger order**, from its cursor `(ledger, index)`, kept in its state.

**Only what is newer** ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)). The account acts only on what a server says as of a validated ledger at or past the newest it has seen, by the ledger stream, a server's state or what a scheme on it booked. A public address spreads its connections over several servers, and one may answer from a ledger minutes old: its answers, an absent account included, are not acted on, and are asked for again as ledgers close, until it has caught up. Validated ledgers are final, so a server behind can only delay the account. The newest ledger seen, with its close time, is kept with the account's state.

**The server is trusted.** The account takes its server's word on which ledgers are validated and what they hold: it checks no validator's signature. Only what is newer protects against a server that lags, not one that lies, which can have a scheme book anything, or claim a testnet reset. Checking the validators against a UNL of the node's own is [not built](../roadmap.md#checking-xrp-ledger-validators).

**Connecting.** On each connection: check the network, read the server's fees and reserves, and tell from its validated ledger whether it is behind or on a chain started again; subscribe to the account and the ledger stream; only then read the account at the validated ledger, so nothing falls between what it says and what the stream says next, and each line a scheme follows as of that same ledger, so what the line says and what the transactions after it say add up; catch up from the earliest cursor with the account's history, holding the stream's transactions meanwhile; then the schemes make their books match the balance and the lines and book what was held, and the account submits what waits. A server whose history does not reach back leaves the balance and the lines to say what changed, through suspense, without who it was.

**Booking a validated transaction** by what it did to the account's XRP, for `xrpl-testnet`:

- **One of ours:** posted when it succeeded, voided when it failed, and its fee booked either way.
- **One another scheme on the account signed,** such as a token settlement or a `TrustSet`: its fee is a ledger fee here, since this is the ledger whose scheme settles in the account's XRP, and a line's reserve is reconciled as any reserve change is ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)). Whatever else it did to the XRP is a deposit or a withdrawal.
- **A settlement from a peer:** a successful Payment of XRP to us, whose memo has our memo type and names a peer, from the address that peer gave when peering. It is booked by `delivered_amount`, with its hash as the journal reference.
- **Anything else** that adds XRP, the account's creation included, is a deposit; anything that takes XRP, a withdrawal.

**Peers' addresses** are the scheme's. They are learnt only once a peering is made, never from an attempt that may still be refused, and kept after it ends, like the [settlement channel](settlement.md#settlement-channels) the peer's settlements come over. The scheme keeps at least as many as the node has channels (asserted at start), so no stranger's attempt displaces a peer's address and turns its settlement into the owner's deposit.

After each, the books must hold the balance less the reserve; any difference, such as a reserve change or a balance below the reserve, is reconciled through `equity/suspense`. The part a change of the reserve explains (the base, the increment per object, or how many objects the account owns, as when a token scheme opens its line) is reconciled as the reserve's, down to the books holding nothing: expected, so the node logs it as information; the rest as a warning. **The reserve is never booked, so never spent.** A transaction of ours is settled whenever the ledger shows it, even behind the cursor: what the balance booked through suspense meanwhile comes back out.

**The first sync** books what the account holds above the reserve as the owner's deposit. Before it, a scheme books none of the history the account reads for the others, only its own transactions. Only then does the node peer on the scheme, so no peer's settlement is taken for that deposit ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-scheme-interface), [synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md)). The cursor it sets is the record of that sync, kept with the scheme's state.

**The account gone** (a testnet reset). A reset starts the chain again from a first ledger far below the newest seen, which the rule above alone would never act on. A server whose validated ledger is below the newest seen but closed after it is on a new chain: close times only grow along a chain, so a server behind cannot report that. On the testnet only, the account then follows the new chain. Then, or when the account is absent as of a ledger it has not seen past: each scheme books what its books hold as a loss through suspense and drops its cursor, everything in flight is cancelled (an account made again starts its sequence anew), and the faucet is asked until the account exists again. A scheme not on the account then does the same once back ([above](#the-account)). The node takes no new peering on the scheme until it has synced again.

## Settling

`prepare_settlement` is `Unavailable` ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-scheme-interface)) unless the account signs the Payment now ([above](#the-account)). `xrpl-testnet` settles whole drops, a token scheme whole millionths ([The token scheme](#the-token-scheme)), and the transaction signed is kept in flight, not yet executed.

- **Nothing is sent before it is booked.** A transaction is submitted only once the node executed its settlement, its pending transfer on disk. One never executed is never sent: past its last ledger it is dropped, the sequence read again, and the account remembers it as never sent until its scheme has reconciled it ([below](#state-and-restarts)). Its sequence goes to the next transaction, whichever scheme asked.
- **Cancelled only when it can never apply.** A malformed transaction (`tem…` on submit) is cancelled at once. Past its last ledger, a transaction is looked for over the ledgers it could have applied in; only when the server has searched all of them and it is not there is it cancelled. Anything less certain waits, since cancelling a transaction that applied would pay the peer twice.

## Settlement messages

The `settlement` message carries the transaction's hash, and books nothing: the ledger says what arrived, once. A Payment without the memo, from an address no peer gave, or that failed, is not a settlement.

## Deposits and withdrawals

The owner funds the scheme by sending XRP to its account. The node cannot deposit for the owner, and has no way to withdraw: a withdrawal is made outside the node, with the same keys, and booked when the ledger shows it.

The account takes payments with or without a destination tag: it is the node's alone, so tags tell nothing apart and are ignored.

**The owner may ask the faucet** of a test ledger for more test XRP (`AskFaucet`), as the account asks while it does not exist. It is the same request, at the same pace: at most one at a time and one a minute, so the owner adds no load the faucet would not see anyway. It is asked only on a connection, since its answer is taken on one. Nothing is booked at the asking: what the faucet sends is a deposit once the ledger shows it, by the rule above. A ledger without a faucet (the config's `faucet` is `null`, as on the live ledger) refuses.

## State and restarts

Each keeps its own state, written with its checkpoint in the batch it explains ([Settlement](settlement.md#checkpoints-and-activation)): the account its next sequence, its transactions in flight (each with the scheme that signed it), those it remembers as never sent, those it keeps for a scheme not on it, the count of its write-offs and the newest ledger seen; each scheme its cursor, its peers' addresses, the settlements it looks up, with those of them it never read, and the count of write-offs it last saw. What the ledger says of the lines and the issuer is read again on each connection, never kept. A state either could not have written is refused as it loads, rather than stopping the node later: an account state that names a scheme that cannot settle through the account, or a scheme state that looks up a transaction the account has in flight or remembers as never sent. So is a scheme state that still holds the account's: the database migrates one written before the account kept its own into the two (`store/schema.rs`), and a node that restarts on it books nothing anew.

After a restart, executed transactions in flight are submitted again once synced. Each settlement the node still holds pending is handed to its scheme:

- **In flight:** submitted again.
- **Never sent:** cancelled at once. It was signed and booked but never executed, and dropped past its last ledger, or it was found never to apply, or the account was written off, while its scheme was not on the account: either way no ledger will ever hold it, and a scheme that stopped and starts again cancels it rather than leave it pending for good.
- **Neither:** looked up by its hash and posted or voided by what the ledger says, unless the scheme reads it again first as one kept for it ([above](#the-account)), and posts or voids it then, with its fee. One the scheme read before the restart it booked then, with its fee and what it did to the balance or the line; one it never read ([above](#the-account)) it books once found, with its fee, and what the balance or the line booked through suspense comes back out. It is looked for in all the server's history, without a range of ledgers: when it was submitted is not kept, and a server refuses to search more than a short range at once. A transaction of ours that no history holds any more cannot be known to have applied, and its settlement stays pending, for the owner; it is asked for again less often the longer it is not found, so a lookup that never succeeds costs little.

## The fake ledger

The feature `fake` models the ledger for simulations and tests (`fake.rs`): accounts, reserves, fees, the checks the ledger makes on a Payment with their result codes, close times, streams, history that can be cut short, network IDs, testnet resets that start the chain again from a lower ledger, a faucet, trust lines with issuers of test RLUSD and DSUSD ([below](#tokens-in-the-fake-ledger)), and AMM pools of XRP and a token ([below](#pools-in-the-fake-ledger)). A copy of it is a server behind the others, stopped where the ledger was. In-process its close times are fixed steps, so a simulation stays deterministic; served by `fake_ledger`, its ledgers close on the wall clock, so a new server is a new chain that closes later, and nodes that knew an earlier one see a testnet reset. It answers the subset of the rippled WebSocket API the account speaks, and checks what it is sent with the node's own codec. The node simulation settles on it in-process; `fake_ledger` serves it over WebSocket to the node's tests, the end-to-end tests and `node --run dev -- --xrpl fake`.

### Tokens in the fake ledger

The fake ledger is the only source of test RLUSD and DSUSD on demand for dev, the end-to-end tests and the simulations: Ripple hands out testnet RLUSD only by hand, and DSUSD comes only from a pool. So it models what a token scheme meets on the testnet, and what it cannot get from the real one on demand: freezes and clawbacks (`fake/tokens.rs`).

- **Its issuers are RLUSD's and DSUSD's,** at their testnet addresses, with RLUSD's settings: lines ripple through them, they require no authorisation, and they keep the right to freeze and to claw back. A scheme with either token's constants runs on the fake unchanged. An issuer outlives a reset, as one that came back at its address; its lines and what they held do not. A test can add issuers, so a line is seen from both sides, whichever account's ID is the lower number.
- **Trust lines** are kept as the ledger keeps them ([Tokens](#tokens)): the balance from the low side, each side's limit and flags, and an owner reserve on each side not in its default state, normally the holder's. A `TrustSet` sets the sender's limit and flags, with the ledger's refusals, such as a deep freeze on a line not frozen, or a line the account lacks the reserve for (its first two are free). A line back in its default state on both sides is deleted, and its reserve freed.
- **A token Payment** goes over the line between the two when either issues the token, else through the issuer, and fails as the ledger fails it. Nothing to pay with, no line, a payer frozen, a payee deep-frozen, or a global freeze between holders: `tecPATH_DRY`. Less than all of it, above the payee's limit, a `SendMax` of the token below the amount, or an issuer's transfer fee, which the fake takes from no Payment, with `SendMax` or without: `tecPATH_PARTIAL`. No payee: `tecNO_DST`. A frozen line still receives, and still pays its issuer. A failure costs its fee and moves no token.
- **The issuer acts on request,** without a key: it mints (a Payment to a holder), freezes and deep-freezes a line (a `TrustSet` of its own), freezes every line or sets a transfer fee (`AccountSet`), and claws back (`Clawback`, which lowers the line with no Payment). Each applies in the next ledger as any transaction does, with the metadata, delivered amount, history and streams the ledger gives: both accounts of a line hear what changes it, and an `AccountSet` reaches only the issuer's own stream. `ledger_entry` reads a line, and `account_info` the issuer's flags and transfer rate. `fake_ledger` takes these requests over HTTPS next to the faucet, under `/tokens/`, for dev and the end-to-end tests, of RLUSD's issuer unless a request names DSUSD.
- **Its arithmetic is exact.** Where the ledger would round a balance to sixteen digits, the fake refuses the transaction (`tecPRECISION_LOSS`) instead of guessing how the ledger rounds; a scheme books by what the line holds, which covers the ledger's rounding too. As the XRP that exists never changes, every token's holders hold exactly what its issuer minted, less what came back to it, was clawed back or wiped, after every ledger.

### Pools in the fake ledger

The fake keeps a constant-product pool of XRP and a token, one per token, which the token's issuer makes or sets on request (`fake/pools.rs`): no liquidity provider deposits or withdraws; the issuer sets what the pool holds and its trading fee with a transaction of the fake's own, minting or taking back the token and moving the XRP from or to genesis. `fake_ledger` takes it under `/tokens/`, for dev and the end-to-end tests: RLUSD's pool unless the request names DSUSD or another of the fake's tokens.

- **A pool's assets are its own account's,** as on the ledger: its XRP in its account root, its token on its line to the issuer. So the XRP and every token still add up after every ledger, a swap's metadata shows what it did to each, and a reset wipes the pools with the other accounts. The fake derives the account from the token, so a pool made again after a reset has the same one. It takes no Payment (`tecNO_PERMISSION`), as the ledger's pool accounts take none.
- **A Payment with `SendMax` crosses the pool,** XRP for its token or its token for XRP, at the price of [AMM pools](#amm-pools), with the metadata, delivered amount, history and streams of any Payment, and fails as the ledger fails it. No pool, another token, or a token for another token: `tecPATH_DRY`. A price above `SendMax`, all the pool holds or more, or more XRP than the payer holds above its reserve: `tecPATH_PARTIAL`. The token's own failures are a token Payment's: no line, a freeze, a limit.
- **Its arithmetic is exact,** as for tokens. Where the ledger would round the payer's balance or the pool's, the fake instead rounds a price in the token up, a digit at a time, until both hold their balances exactly (`FakeLedger::quote`), so a payer pays less than a unit of their last shared digit above the pool's price; a balance it still cannot hold fails the Payment (`tecPRECISION_LOSS`). A swap that lowered the pool's product would be a bug, and is asserted against.
- **`amm_info`** reads a pool by its two assets, in either order and answered in that order, or by its account, with the LP tokens a pool made at its balances would have issued.

## Decisions

- [external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md): the account apart from the schemes that settle through it, and gas on or off the books.
- [issued-assets](../decisions/issued-assets.md): a token's issuer as the owner's counterparty, and a dollar token at par.
- [dsusd](../decisions/dsusd.md): a testnet dollar of Dassie's own beside RLUSD, bought only with XRP through a pool kept at the market.
- [real-money](../decisions/real-money.md): the gates before real money, and the testnet-only network check.
- [tigerstyle](../decisions/tigerstyle.md): sans-IO, so the fake ledger can stand in for the network.
- [synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md): no peering on the scheme before its first sync.
- [ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md): only what is newer than the newest ledger seen; a reset is a lower ledger that closed later, and only on the testnet.
