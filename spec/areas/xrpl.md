# XRPL

The node's accounts on the XRP Ledger testnet and mainnet and the settlement schemes on them: in each network's XRP, and in its dollar token on a trust line, the token scheme; on the testnet `xrpl-testnet` and `xrpl-testnet-dsusd` in DSUSD, on mainnet `xrpl` and `xrpl-rlusd` in RLUSD, real money, which run only in the live realm. Test RLUSD's scheme, `xrpl-testnet-rlusd`, is disabled. The wallet, derived from the owner's passphrase; the account as an external asset, which connects, signs and follows the ledger for the schemes on it; the Payments the schemes sign to settle; how every transaction that touches the account is booked once; how they survive restarts; and the gates that keep real money out; and the peg guard that pauses a dollar token's scheme while its AMM pool prices it off a dollar. Beside them, the ledger's AMM pools: a pool's price, read with `amm_info`, and the Payment that swaps through one, for a client that converts on the ledger. And what an account several keys control is set up and paid out with, for a [settlement hub](../designs/settlement-hubs.md)'s escrow: secp256k1 keys, account settings, signer lists and multi-signing. And [checking the validators](../designs/xrpl-validation.md): the ledger's records read in their one form, ledger headers, validations and manifests, branches in the ledger's hash trees, the skip lists, the ledger objects and transactions the account reads, and the UNL from its publishers' signed lists, with the quorum that validates a ledger ([below](#the-validators-records)); the account's anchors on them: its lists fetched from their publishers' sites, the ledgers its UNL validated, which alone move what it takes as validated, and testnet resets told by ancestry ([below](#anchors)); and all the account reads proven at them: what it holds, its lines, their issuers and the pools by state proofs, each transaction by its own proof, found by the history the proven state names, and an absence only when proven ([below](#following-the-account)).

**Paths:** `crates/dassie-xrpl/`, `crates/dassie-node/src/fake_ledger.rs`, `testdata/xrpl/`
**Read with:** [Settlement](settlement.md), [Ledger](ledger.md), [Identity](identity.md)

The crate does no IO and reads no clock. The account says what to send its ledger and the node feeds it what comes back, over the one connection it keeps for the account; the account tells the schemes on it what the ledger said, and they book it, through the node ([Settlement](settlement.md#external-assets)). Its constants are in `lib.rs`, `network.rs`, `account.rs`, `scheme.rs`, `token.rs`, `rlusd.rs`, `dsusd.rs`, `peg.rs`, `amm.rs`, `binary.rs`, `header.rs`, `validation.rs`, `manifest.rs`, `shamap.rs`, `skip_list.rs`, `validator_list.rs`, `unl.rs`, `anchor.rs`, `records.rs`, `account/anchors.rs` and `account/history.rs`.

The account is an external asset (`account.rs`), on one network of the ledger (`network.rs`): its kind names the network, which gives it the network ID its server must report, its seed path, its default server (the project's [proof server](proof-servers.md) for the network), its faucet if the network has one, its realm, and the scheme that settles in its XRP. Each network's XRP scheme is the same code (`scheme.rs`), with a memo type and a ledger of its own: `xrpl-testnet` on the testnet's account, `xrpl` on mainnet's, which books on `xrpl+xrp`, a ledger of the live realm, so only a node of that realm runs it ([mainnet-schemes](../decisions/mainnet-schemes.md)). The token scheme settles in a dollar token on its trust line to the token's issuer, with its XRP as gas (`token.rs`, [The token scheme](#the-token-scheme)): `xrpl-rlusd` in RLUSD on mainnet's account ([RLUSD](#rlusd)), on `xrpl-rlusd+usd`, of the live realm too, and `xrpl-testnet-dsusd` in DSUSD on the testnet's ([DSUSD](#dsusd)). Each runs alone, or together with its network's XRP scheme on the one account.

## Real money

Real money is refused at every level until the conditions of [real-money](../decisions/real-money.md) hold, which the [roadmap](../roadmap.md#real-money) lists. The scheme meets the XRP Ledger's part of them (the reserve is never booked, an account's creation is a deposit, it settles whole drops, books fees and checks results and senders); mainnet also needs [schemes of its own](../roadmap.md#mainnet-schemes), the [validators checked](../roadmap.md#checking-xrp-ledger-validators) and [proof servers](../roadmap.md#proof-servers). Until then:

- The realm `live` is refused ([Node](node.md)), and a scheme activates only on a ledger of its node's realm ([Settlement](settlement.md#schemes-and-ledgers)), so `xrpl` and `xrpl-rlusd` run only by the dev switch, which no release build honours. Mainnet's dollar scheme pauses while its pool prices it off its peg ([The peg guard](#the-peg-guard)).
- **An account refuses other networks.** Before it reads the account, it checks that its server reports its own network's ID. A server that reports another, or none, stops the account as failed, and every scheme on it with it, before anything is booked, written off or signed. An owner who points the testnet's account at a mainnet server gets a failed scheme, not mainnet XRP booked or signed for with a testnet wallet; a scheme settles only through an account of its own network.
- A server that reports more XRP for the account than the books can hold stops the account as failed.
- **Only the testnet resets** ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)). Taking a validated ledger off the anchor's chain for a new chain is gated on the account's network, a constant of the account (`Network::resets`), and writing off an account gone on the network ID its server reports (`resets` in `account.rs`), each a check of its own, not only the one that keeps the account on its network. On any other network, a validated ledger off the anchor's chain stops the account, and so does the absence of an account that a scheme on it held anything of, with nothing written off. Letting the account run on another network can never make another chain acceptable.

## The wallet

The wallet is the original's, byte for byte, so the same passphrase finds the same account and its funds. It is derived (`keys.rs`) from the node's settlement entropy at `node/settlement-module/xrpl-testnet/seed-testnet` ([Identity](identity.md)) as an Ed25519 family seed. The 64 wallets of the identity chain's golden vectors pin the derivation. That path belongs to the node's first XRPL testnet account (`FIRST` in `account.rs`), whichever schemes settle through it; further accounts get paths of their own when they are built, and no path gets a new meaning. The node's deposit address is this account's. The mainnet account's wallet, which `xrpl` settles from, is the original's too, derived the same way at `node/settlement-module/xrpl/seed-mainnet`, so the same passphrase finds the address the original derived, and the same 64 golden vectors pin it; the two networks' accounts have different keys, so a Payment signed for one never applies on the other.

## The account

The account owns what the schemes that settle through it would otherwise race for ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)):

- **The connection:** the network check, its validator lists and its anchor, testnet resets and the faucet ([below](#following-the-account)). The server, the faucet and the list publishers are the account's config, which the scheme that activates it names; a scheme that names others while the account is active is refused.
- **What it signs:** the next sequence and the transactions in flight: Payments of XRP or of a token, and `TrustSet`s. A scheme asks the account to sign; the account hands out sequences in order, so no two schemes on one account take the same one, and keeps each transaction with the scheme that signed it. It signs nothing unless it is synced and knows its sequence, has room for another transaction in flight, the amount is above zero, the network fee is at most its ceiling (while it is higher, it waits), and what it pays in XRP, its fee and any reserve it takes fit above the reserve after what is in flight; a Payment of a token, only while the line holds it after what the scheme has in flight, as the ledger last said.
- **What it spends as gas:** the XRP a scheme that does not settle in it spends, a token Payment's fee or a `TrustSet`'s fee and its line's reserve, for each token scheme on it alike. While its network's XRP scheme (`xrpl-testnet` on the testnet's account, `xrpl` on mainnet's) settles from the account, that XRP is on its books, so the account signs for another scheme only while the owner's funds on that scheme's ledger, such as `xrpl-testnet+xrp`, which the node tells it before each call that may sign, cover the gas, with the gas already in flight ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)). While the books stopped the XRP scheme, its books still hold the XRP, and the account spends none of it as gas until the scheme runs again after a restart. Otherwise (no XRP scheme, or one the owner removed) the XRP is off the books, and only the reserve bounds it.
- **What it holds,** as its anchor last proved it: its XRP and the reserve, and the balance of each trust line a scheme on it follows, with what the line's issuer froze: the line's own freeze and deep freeze, and a freeze of every line from the issuer's account as last proven, on the chain the account follows: a write-off forgets the issuers' accounts with the lines. The node shows it from the account, never from the books ([Settlement](settlement.md#external-assets)), and the wallet shows it to the owner ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)).
- **What it learnt,** which it tells every scheme on it, in order: each validated transaction that touches the account, with the scheme that signed it if it was in flight; each line its schemes follow, and that it is synced; that a transaction can never apply; that the account is gone; each ledger that closes; each transaction a scheme looked up, under the hash the scheme asked for; and an issuer's account a scheme asked for. A proven transaction that names more XRP than the books can hold for the account, from the history or a lookup, stops the account before any scheme books it.
- **What a scheme not on it misses of its own.** While a scheme is stopped or removed, a transaction of its that can never apply is remembered as never sent, and one the ledger validates is kept with the scheme that signed it, both written with the account's state. So is what the account told a scheme of its own in the input whose bookings the books refused, which stopped it: the node lets the scheme go before the account forgets what it told, the scheme's checkpoint stays from before that input, and the account's state is written on while another scheme on it runs. Back on the account, the scheme cancels the first when it reconciles its settlements, and books the second as its own when it reads it again from its cursor, never as a withdrawal. What it does not read again (a history with a gap the server no longer has, [below](#following-the-account)), the account tells it with the sync, which made its books match the balance, or the line, through suspense; it looks that up by its hash, and books it as its own once found, as one read behind its cursor is, so what suspense booked comes back out. It books it by what the books hold pending for it, which is what it signed, never by what a server says it delivered. It keeps that it never read it with its state, since a restart before the lookup would otherwise take it for one booked already. Until then it signs nothing more, nor while the account tells it of its own (as a token scheme may sign a `TrustSet` on what it is told), so it never has more of its own in flight and kept than it can have in flight, even once a refused input's are kept. The account tells the second again for that scheme alone: the schemes on the account were told it when the ledger validated it, and `xrpl-testnet` booked its fee then, so it books no fee twice.
- **The write-offs a scheme missed.** The account counts the times it was written off ([below](#following-the-account)), kept with its state, and each scheme keeps the count it last saw with its own. A scheme not on the account through a write-off (stopped or removed during a testnet reset) sees another count when it is back, after a restart or added again: before it follows the account, it writes off what its books held through suspense, drops its cursor, which is on a chain gone, and syncs again on the new chain, a token scheme making its line again. So no cursor of an old chain holds the account, or the issuer's account it reads, behind a ledger the new chain has not reached. What the account kept for it went with the chain: a settlement that validated on the old chain is cancelled, as one in flight is.

**Sharing has one cost.** Until a stopped scheme's transaction that was never sent is dropped, the other schemes' transactions with later sequences cannot apply: they are looked up and cancelled past their last ledger, and those schemes back off settling, as after any cancelled settlement ([Settlement](settlement.md#outgoing-settlements)). It costs a window of ledgers and a backoff, and nothing is paid twice. One address for the owner to fund, and one supply of XRP for every scheme, are worth it ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)).

**Each scheme keeps its own cursor** into the account's transactions, and tells the account where it is. On each connection the account walks its history back to the earliest cursor of its schemes, and each scheme books only what is past its own, so a scheme that stopped reads again what it missed, and the others are not held back. A scheme that joins the account once it has read its ledger is read for on a new connection, which reads the lines it follows too ([Settlement](settlement.md#external-assets)).

## The Payment it signs

A settlement is a direct `Payment` to the address the peer gave when peering, with one memo of the scheme's memo type naming our node ID: of XRP for `xrpl-testnet` and `xrpl`, of its token for a token scheme ([The token scheme](#the-token-scheme)), which also signs a `TrustSet` for its line (`codec.rs`).

- **Byte for byte as xrpl.js, which the original used, signs it.** Golden vectors (`crates/dassie-xrpl/tests/data/vectors.json`, made by `testdata/xrpl/generate-vectors.mjs`) pin the codec.
- **The settlement's ID is the transaction hash.** The message for the peer is the hash, so it can always be made again from the ID.
- **The memo type and what a `settlement` message means are a contract between peers.** Both sides of a peering must agree, so they change only with the peer protocol version. Each scheme has its own memo type, a constant of the scheme, so a Payment is never taken for a settlement of another: mainnet's is not the testnet's, which the original wrote on both. A test holds every XRPL scheme's memo type and ledger distinct, and no two settling in one asset on one network.

## Tokens

The codec and the API's parser also cover tokens on trust lines, such as RLUSD and DSUSD (`amount.rs`, `codec.rs`, `api.rs`), for the [token scheme](#the-token-scheme).

- **Amounts are exact.** A token's value is decimal, a mantissa of sixteen digits and an exponent, held in the ledger's one canonical form and never as a float. A value the ledger cannot express exactly, in text or in bytes, is refused rather than rounded, so what the node reads is what the ledger holds.
- **Whole units convert exactly.** A value becomes the whole units of a ledger rounded down, toward minus infinity, so the books never hold more than the line. Units become a value only when the ledger can express it exactly (sixteen significant digits), so the node never signs an amount other than the one it booked.
- **The transactions** are a `Payment` without paths, of XRP or of a token, and a `TrustSet`, which sets a line's limit and flags. A Payment is never partial: the encoder asserts it and the decoder refuses it, so a payment delivers its whole amount or fails. It may name a `SendMax`, the most it spends: of the other asset, which the ledger converts through its default paths, such as an [AMM pool](#amm-pools), or of the same token, to pay an issuer's transfer fee; never XRP for XRP, which the ledger refuses. The schemes' Payments name none. Golden vectors from xrpl.js pin both kinds, a `SendMax` either way between XRP and a token, and token amounts at the edges (the least and the largest value, sixteen digits, zero), byte for byte.
- **A line reads the same from either side.** The ledger keeps a line's balance from the side of the account whose ID is the lower number, and its flags in a low and a high half. The parser gives the balance, the limits and the flags (NoRipple, freeze and deep freeze, as each side set them) from the side asked for, so no caller depends on which number is lower; a line whose limits name its accounts out of order is refused.
- **What a transaction did to a line,** from its metadata: the line before and after, created from zero or deleted to zero. A settlement, a deposit, a withdrawal and an issuer's clawback, which is no Payment, all show as a change of the line's balance. A Payment's delivered amount is read when it is a token.
- **Reading the ledger:** the line itself, and the issuer's account, for its global freeze and its transfer rate, each proven at the account's anchor ([Following the account](#following-the-account)). The API's parser reads them from `ledger_entry` and `account_info` too, for a client that takes its server's word.

## AMM pools

The ledger's AMM holds two assets in a pool and swaps one for the other at a price that keeps the product of what it holds from falling, with a trading fee that stays in the pool. The crate prices a pool, reads one and signs the Payment that swaps through one (`amm.rs`, `api.rs`, `codec.rs`), for a client that converts on the ledger, such as a market maker; the account proves one for a dollar token's [peg guard](#the-peg-guard). No scheme swaps: a settlement delivers the asset its ledger books, and the node trades nothing on the ledger.

- **Prices are exact, and round to the pool.** What taking an amount costs is rounded up, and what paying one yields rounded down, to what the ledger can express: whole drops, or a token's sixteen digits, as the ledger rounds. So a swap at the price never lowers the pool's product, its fee included, and a unit less buys less than asked. Nothing passes through a float, and amounts too far apart in size for exact arithmetic are refused, never rounded.
- **`amm_info` is read exactly,** for a client that takes its server's word: two amounts of two different assets, not below zero, an LP token that the pool's account issues, a trading fee the ledger takes, and the ledger it is as of; anything else is no answer. The LP token's currency is derived as the ledger derives it.
- **A swap is a Payment with `SendMax`** of the asset paid in (`sign_send_max`), which delivers all of its amount or fails: `SendMax` is the payer's bound on the price, and a pool short of the amount fails the Payment rather than paying part of it. It may pay the payer's own account. Whoever signs keeps the sequence. To a scheme that follows the account, a swap it did not sign is a withdrawal of what it spent and a deposit of what it got, as any such transaction is ([below](#following-the-account)).

## The transactions

Beside the schemes' Payments and `TrustSet`s, the codec reads and writes what a [settlement hub](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#2-founding-a-hub)'s escrow is set up and paid out with (`codec.rs`, `codec/wire.rs`): an `AccountSet` that requires a destination tag or disables the master key, a `SetRegularKey`, a `SignerListSet`, and Payments that name a destination tag. No scheme sends them yet.

- **One form only.** Fields go in the ledger's canonical order, by type code and then field code. The decoder takes only the fields it knows, each once and in that order, each header in its shortest form, and arrays neither empty nor longer than the ledger allows (32 signers, or signer entries). So bytes decode only if they encode to themselves, which a property test holds for changed bytes of every kind: a transaction never has a second form, with a second hash, that the node would read as the same.
- **The last ledger is optional.** A transaction that names none stays valid until its sequence is used, as a hub's payout set must, since its signatures are gathered over days. The account still names one on everything it signs, so that it can tell a lost transaction from one still to come ([below](#following-the-account)).
- **One key signs, or several, never both.** One key signs `STX\0` and the fields. Otherwise each signer of the sender's signer list signs alone: `SMT\0`, the fields with the key left empty, and its own account. So signatures are gathered in any order, from signers that are never online together, and put together afterwards, sorted by account as the ledger requires. Putting them together checks each signature and refuses a signer twice or the sender itself. A multi-signed transaction pays the base fee once more for each signer.
- **Whether a key may sign for an account is the ledger's to say,** as of when the transaction applies; the codec checks signatures, not authority. `account_info` and `account_objects` read an account's settings, its regular key and its signer list, so that a hub's validators can check an escrow from outside before they trust it.
- **Golden vectors** from xrpl.js 3.1.0 (`crates/dassie-xrpl/tests/data/escrow-vectors.json`, made by `testdata/xrpl/generate-escrow-vectors.mjs`) pin each kind, single-signed by either kind of key and multi-signed, byte for byte.

## Keys and signatures

The ledger knows two kinds of key, told apart by a public key's first byte (`keys.rs`): Ed25519, which the node's wallet is, and secp256k1, which a hub's payout keys are, since anyone derives them from a public parent ([settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#9-shutdown)). Either signs a transaction alone or as a signer, and an account's ID is the same hash of either.

- **A secp256k1 signature is fully canonical, both ways.** It signs the SHA-512Half of the message, deterministically (RFC 6979), with a low S, in strict DER. Verifying refuses a high S, and DER that does not write back to the same bytes, as rippled does: either would let anyone turn one signed transaction into another with another hash. The vectors pin ripple-keypairs' signatures byte for byte.
- **How a secp256k1 key is derived is its owner's.** The crate takes the secret scalar, so a hub derives its payout keys as its design says, outside the ledger's code.

## The validators' records

What the [validators' design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md) proves from is read by code of its own, beside the codec for what the node signs (`binary.rs`, `header.rs`, `validation.rs`, `manifest.rs`, `shamap.rs`), and judged against the UNL (`validator_list.rs`, `unl.rs`). The account anchors on it ([below](#anchors)), and proves at its anchors what it reads ([below](#following-the-account)).

- **Any serialization, in one form only.** A walker reads the ledger's binary format knowing only its types, never which fields a record has, which is each reader's to check: within each object, fields strictly ascending; headers and length prefixes in their shortest form; each value in the form rippled writes it, except a `Number`, whose form depends on rippled's amendments and which a reader of one checks; objects and arrays nested no deeper than rippled reads, an array holding only objects. So bytes are read only if they write back to themselves, which a property test holds for changed bytes of real records of every kind. It never recurses, and bytes from outside get an error, never a panic.
- **A ledger's header** is its 118 bytes, and the ledger's hash, which validators sign, is the SHA-512Half of `LWR\0` and them.
- **A validation** is read with rippled's validation fields and no others, and a secp256k1 key, so it writes back to the bytes its validator signed. It counts only when it is full and says its signature is fully canonical, and the signature, with a low S in strict DER, is its key's over `VAL\0` and its fields but the signature. Whose key it is, whether that validator is trusted and whether its signature is fresh are the quorum's to judge.
- **A manifest** hands its master key's signing to another key, or revokes the master key, and then names none. Both keys sign `MAN\0` and its fields but the two signatures, and either may be Ed25519 or secp256k1. Its domain, if it names one, is well-formed as rippled has it.
- **Validators' keys** are written as node public keys (`n…`).
- **A branch** proves a leaf in one of a ledger's two hash trees, its transactions' or its state's, or a key's absence from it, against the tree's root in the ledger's header. One check serves both trees and absences, which differ only in the leaf: a transaction under its own hash, with its metadata; an object under its key; or, for an absence, a branch that ends at the empty slot where the key would hang or at the leaf of another key that hangs there. A leaf's hash commits to its key, and a leaf hangs at the shallowest depth at which its key is unique, so an inner node would stand there had the key been in the tree. The branch is the nodes on the key's path, deepest first, each as rippled hashes it, prefix included, as a proof server gives them ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#3-the-proof-api)); each node is read by its prefix, so as one kind only, and a shape rippled never builds, such as a leaf below an inner node with no other child, proves nothing, so a branch holds only as a path of a tree rippled built. An empty tree's branch is empty, and proves every key absent. The check walks down from the root, never recursing, through at most one inner node per nibble of the key. A builder of trees and their branches, behind the fake ledger's feature, serves the tests.
- **A validator list** counts when its publisher is one the account trusts, the publisher's manifest verifies under its master key, the list's blob, as its site served it, under the publisher's current signing key, its sequence is not below the highest seen of that publisher, and it has not expired; one that takes effect later waits as its publisher's pending list until then. A site answers in either of rippled's versions, and bounded. A publisher that revokes its key has no list for good.
- **The publishers built in:** on mainnet, Ripple's and the XRP Ledger Foundation's, as rippled's default configuration names them; on the testnet, its own; each with the site that serves its list and the network's floor on its UNL. The owner's publishers, each with a key and its sites, named in the account's config (`publishers`, with perhaps a `floor`), replace them entirely, with the network's floor unless they name one.
- **Manifests** of validators come from the lists and from the server's `manifest` method alike, and count only for a master a kept list names, when that master signed them; the highest sequence wins, a revocation leaves its master no key, and a signing key serves one master only, never a listed master's own key. A master with no manifest signs with its own key, as in rippled.
- **The UNL** is the validators every publisher's list in effect names, and there is none while a publisher has no list in effect or while fewer remain than the floor, never the validators last seen. Quorum is four fifths of it, rounded up, with no negative UNL.
- **A tally** counts the UNL's validators that signed a ledger's hash and index with a full, fully canonical validation, signed by the master's current signing key at a time fresh by the node's clock, as long as rippled holds a validation current. A master counts once, and not at all if it also signed another ledger of the index. The cheapest checks come first, so an answer costs at most two signature checks per master; the signatures are checked behind an interface a simulation substitutes. It names the UNL's validators that did not count, whose newer manifests the account may ask for.
- **A skip list** is read from its proven `LedgerHashes` object (`skip_list.rs`): of the 256 ledgers before a ledger, or of the flag ledgers, every 256th, of 65,536. A hash it holds is a proven ledger's; any ledger of a chain is at most two state proofs from a later one, through the flag ledger after it.
- **A state proof** proves a ledger's header and, for each key asked, its object or its absence, each by its branch to the header's state root (`anchor.rs`); every key asked has one entry, and nothing else. The header is a proven ledger's only once its hash is.
- **A transaction proof** proves a transaction and its metadata, under the hash its own bytes hash to, by its branch to the transaction root of its ledger's header (`anchor.rs`). Its ledger is a proven one's when it is the anchor, or a skip list proven from the anchor holds its hash: at most two lists away.
- **What the account reads from proven bytes** (`records.rs`): the keys of an account's root, a trust line, a pool's `AMM` object and the fee settings, as rippled derives them; an account's root, a line, the fee settings in either of rippled's forms, and an `AMM` object's account and assets, each with the transaction that last changed it; and a transaction with its metadata, as the record the schemes read, the same the JSON of the stream and `tx` gives: what it delivered is the metadata's `DeliveredAmount`, else a successful Payment's `Amount` when it is not partial. Beside the record, what the history is walked by: for each object the account follows that it changed, the transaction that changed it before; and the sequence numbers of its sender it used up. Mainnet pins them: its fee settings and its XRP/RLUSD pool, every transaction of one of its ledgers, and the transaction that last changed the pool's root and line, which its metadata leaves as the ledger holds them; and every transaction the fake writes decodes as its JSON reads.
- **An anchor** is a ledger a quorum validated, never one older than the anchor kept, a lower index that closed earlier, which is refused before any signature is checked. One that diverged from the kept anchor, another ledger of its index or one whose index and close time disagree with it, is told apart, and left to the chain's rules ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#7-resets-under-a-unl)).
- **What is kept:** each publisher's newest manifest, highest list sequence, and current and pending lists as they came, and each listed validator's newest manifest, also one a list carries, since that list may not come back at a restart while another with an older one does, which would hand the validator's signing back to a key it rotated away from. At load all of it verifies again; what no longer verifies, because the publishers changed, or what expired, is dropped, while a state the account never writes, such as a publisher twice, two manifests of one master, or a list above its publisher's highest sequence, is refused.
- **Golden data** captured from mainnet and the testnet (`crates/dassie-xrpl/tests/data/`) pins them: headers that hash to their ledgers, the publishers' lists as their sites served them, which verify under the keys built in, every validation of one ledger on each network, with its UNL reaching quorum, by the margin the lists' manifests leave and in full with the validators' live manifests, the publishers' and the validators' manifests, transactions, metadata and ledger objects as rippled wrote them, and a mainnet ledger's whole transaction set, whose tree the builder rebuilds to the root its header names, with branches that verify, each node as rippled hashes it.

## Anchors

The account anchors on a ledger its own UNL validated ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#6-the-account-on-proofs)), in `account/anchors.rs`, and only an anchor moves what it takes as validated.

- **Its lists,** from each publisher's sites in its config: its network's built-in publishers, or the owner's, which replace them entirely ([config](#the-validators-records)). The node fetches a list by an HTTPS GET it asks for (`AssetEffect::Fetch`), following no redirect, bounded, and hands back the body or nothing ([Settlement](settlement.md#assets-that-follow-a-ledger)). An answer is every publisher's that asked that site, and fetched only for the publisher whose list it is, so two publishers may share a site. A site that fails is followed by the publisher's next at once, and the first again a while later; a list is fetched again before it expires, more often near its expiry, and at once when there is no UNL or quorum is lost. Lists and manifests are kept with the account's state, so a restart needs no site; they verify again as they load.
- **A round** is a state proof at the server's validated ledger of its list of recent ledgers and of all the account follows ([below](#following-the-account)), then that ledger's validations, counted against the UNL. Validators that did not count while validations came under keys no listed manifest names are asked about by their masters, a bounded number a round, for newer manifests, and the validations counted again. The account starts a round as it connects, and on each closed ledger's hint while none is in flight and no history is being walked, so the schemes hear of ledgers at the ledger's pace.
- **An older anchor is never current.** A ledger below the kept anchor that closed earlier is refused before any signature is checked, and the account waits for a newer one; one of an older chain, a higher index that closed no later, is ignored on the testnet, and on a network that never resets is a fork, which stops the account as failed. A server that serves old ledgers with their old validations can stall the account, never move it back.
- **Ancestry.** A later anchor is on the kept one's chain when its skip lists hold it: the recent ledgers' list within 256 ledgers; past them, the flag ledgers' list holds the kept anchor's flag ledger, kept with it, or, for one below its chain's first flag ledger, the flag ledger after it, whose own list then holds it. One off the chain, or below it but closed later, is a testnet reset, which writes the account off ([below](#following-the-account)); on any other network it stops the account as failed. A state written before anchors kept the newest ledger without a hash: the first anchor replaces it by index and close time alone, and on mainnet replaces it where it would have stopped the account, since only the old hint can have been wrong.
- **Stalls, with their reason.** No list in effect, a UNL below its floor, no quorum, a server that cut its validations short of one (`truncated`), which is no lie the account can tell, a server that serves no proofs or refuses one, one that does not answer, one whose history is too short, an older ledger than the anchor, or answers that do not prove: the account waits, and says why (`ExternalAsset::waiting`), which the node shows beside its status ([RPC](rpc.md)). It never falls back to the server's word. An answer that does not prove is counted by kind (`Doubt`); after it, or after a refusal, the account connects anew a while later, perhaps to another server behind the same address, so a server that keeps failing costs a connection every so often. It counts what it proved too, by kind (`Proofs`: anchors, transactions and absences, and what it could not prove), never kept, so that a simulation shows that a run went through the verifier.
- **Freshness** is by the node's clock: validations count only while rippled would hold them current, so a clock far ahead sees none fresh, and waits. A ledger that a quorum signed but not freshly, each validator counted once and only once its signature checks, is old, as from a server behind: the account waits for a newer one on the same server, as for a ledger older than its anchor, and does not take it for a lie.

## The token scheme

A token scheme settles in a dollar token on the account's trust line to the token's issuer, and books on a dollar ledger of its own (`token.rs`). One code serves every token: what tells one token scheme from another is its token (`Token`), the scheme's ID, its memo type, the issuer, the currency code, the ledger and the network, all constants of the scheme. Two run on it, [RLUSD](#rlusd) on mainnet and [DSUSD](#dsusd) on the testnet, each beside its network's XRP scheme on the one account; a scheme refuses an account of another network than its token's. The ledger's asset is `USD`, at par, so it converts one to one with every other dollar ledger ([issued-assets](../decisions/issued-assets.md)); its unit is a millionth of a dollar, which a token's sixteen digits hold exactly for any balance a node could hold. It pays its fees and its line's reserve in the account's XRP, as gas ([above](#the-account)).

**The line comes first.** The scheme counts as synced, and the node peers on it, only once the ledger shows the account's line to the issuer with rippling disabled on our side and the largest limit the ledger can express, so no one moves our token through our account and no settlement fails for reaching the limit ([synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md)). A line missing, or made outside the node otherwise, is set so with a `TrustSet`, signed once the account holds the XRP for its fee and its reserve and the issuer is known to exist, to which a `TrustSet` would only fail. It books nothing on the scheme's ledger, so the account submits it at once. One that fails waits for the next twice as long as the last, as each costs a fee. The first sync with the line as it needs it books what the line holds as the owner's deposit, and sets the cursor. Each scheme follows its own token's line alone, and pays a reserve for it.

**Settling.** A settlement is a Payment of the token of its issuer, without paths and never partial, with one memo of the scheme's own memo type naming our node ID; its ID and message are the hash, as for XRP. It settles whole millionths. `prepare_settlement` is also `Unavailable` while the issuer froze our line, while the issuer froze its token for every holder, while it charges a transfer fee (which a Payment without `SendMax` cannot pay; not provided for yet, [roadmap](../roadmap.md#not-built-yet)), until the issuer's account was read, while the gas cannot pay the fee, and while the peg guard pauses the scheme ([The peg guard](#the-peg-guard)).

**Booking a validated transaction** by what it did to the line, read from its metadata:

- **One of ours:** posted when it succeeded, voided when it failed. Its fee is gas: on its network's XRP ledger, such as `xrpl-testnet+xrp`, if that network's XRP scheme runs on the account, otherwise on no books.
- **A settlement from a peer:** a successful Payment to us that delivered the token of our issuer, whose memo has the scheme's memo type and names a peer, from the address that peer gave when peering. It is booked by what it delivered, in whole units, never by what it stated.
- **Anything else that raises the line** is a deposit; **anything that lowers it** a withdrawal, an issuer's clawback included, which lowers the line without a Payment. Each moves the books by what the transaction did to the line, its balance before and after as the metadata says, never by what the scheme last saw of the line, which after a restart is nothing.
- **A Payment that names us as the issuer of what it pays,** meaning any issuer we trust, may say it delivered a token of our own account rather than of the token's issuer. It is no settlement: what it did to the line is booked as a deposit. That credits the peer less than arrived, never more.
- **Other currencies, other issuers and XRP** move nothing on this ledger: the other token scheme's settlements included, and a Payment of our token with another scheme's memo, which is a deposit.

After each, the books hold the line's balance in whole units; a remainder below a unit, which anyone can send, stays on the line and is booked once it adds up to one.

**The issuer's acts,** read from the ledger, each shown in what the account holds:

- **Our line's flags** at each sync, with the line itself, so a freeze from before the node first saw the line is known; then with every transaction that touches the line, the issuer's freezes included, which the account's stream carries. A line set otherwise since, such as rippling enabled outside the node, is set again.
- **The issuer's account,** for a global freeze and a transfer fee, proven at the account's anchor: as the scheme syncs, and at every settlement check. Both change only the issuer's account, which our account's history never names.
- **A frozen line** settles nothing, and still receives. **A line that cannot receive** stops what peers pay us too: one deep-frozen or under a global freeze, and one whose issuer charges a transfer fee, which no node of the scheme pays, as each is `Unavailable` while the fee holds. The scheme says so (`can_receive`), and the node asks no peer on it to settle, counts a peer's time short toward withdrawing it but never toward ending it, and leaves the scheme out of its link state ([Settlement](settlement.md#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)). The scheme stays synced throughout: it still sees its ledger.

**A testnet reset** takes the line and what it held, which is written off through suspense with the account, and the issuer's account with them. The scheme makes the line again once the account and the issuer are back. An issuer that does not come back at its address leaves the scheme unsynced until a release names the new one.

**Whom it peers with.** A peering is on one scheme ([Network](network.md#peering)), so two nodes that share several XRPL schemes settle in one of them. A token scheme's node peers on it only with nodes that run the same scheme: RLUSD and DSUSD are two ledgers, never one, and so are RLUSD on mainnet and test RLUSD.

## RLUSD

`xrpl-rlusd` is the [token scheme](#the-token-scheme) in RLUSD, Ripple's dollar token, on the trust line of mainnet's account to RLUSD's mainnet issuer, and books on `xrpl-rlusd+usd`, a ledger of the live realm: real money, run only where `xrpl` may run ([Real money](#real-money)). Its issuer, its currency code and its memo type are constants of the scheme (`rlusd.rs`); the memo type is its own, never test RLUSD's, so a Payment of RLUSD that carries test RLUSD's memo is no settlement on mainnet. The issuer is the owner's counterparty there with real money ([issued-assets](../decisions/issued-assets.md)).

**Funding.** Mainnet has no faucet: the owner sends the account XRP for gas and RLUSD. The in-process tests and the `node` simulation run it in the live realm on the fake ledger's mainnet, with its RLUSD issuer ([below](#tokens-in-the-fake-ledger)); so do dev (`rlusd-live-settlement`) and the end-to-end tests, through the node's dev switch ([Dev](dev.md#scenarios)).

**Test RLUSD is disabled** ([issued-assets](../decisions/issued-assets.md)): it has no dollar price on the testnet, so no peg guard could run on it, and DSUSD stands in for it there ([DSUSD](#dsusd)). Its scheme, `xrpl-testnet-rlusd`, is no longer offered and cannot be added; nothing settles in it any more. A node that ran it starts without it and says so: the scheme stays stored, with its checkpoint and its books on `xrpl-testnet-rlusd+usd`, and its line on the ledger keeps what it holds, the owner's; nothing is written off. Its peerings end, collecting nothing, so the pair can peer on a scheme both run ([Settlement](settlement.md#schemes-and-ledgers)). Its ID, memo type and ledger stay constants (`rlusd.rs`), so the testnet's account still knows what it kept for the scheme, as for any scheme not on it ([The account](#the-account)), and no other scheme ever takes them.

## DSUSD

`xrpl-testnet-dsusd` is the [token scheme](#the-token-scheme) in DSUSD, a testnet dollar token of Dassie's own, on the account's trust line to DSUSD's issuer, an account of Dassie's on the testnet, and books on `xrpl-testnet-dsusd+usd` (`dsusd.rs`). Its issuer, its currency code and its memo type are constants of the scheme; the memo type is a contract between peers, as every XRPL scheme's is ([above](#the-payment-it-signs)). It is the testnet's dollar, in test RLUSD's place: test RLUSD trades far from a dollar against XRP on the testnet's AMM, while DSUSD's XRP/DSUSD pool there is kept near an exchange's XRP/USD price, so a dollar booked at par is worth about a dollar in XRP ([dsusd](../decisions/dsusd.md)). The testnet's bootstrap nodes run it beside `xrpl-testnet` ([Network](network.md#bootstrap-nodes)), so a node that runs only DSUSD peers with them on it, and one that runs both settles with them in XRP.

**Funding.** DSUSD has no faucet. The owner sends the account XRP for gas, from the faucet as for `xrpl-testnet`, and gets DSUSD only by paying XRP for it on the testnet's XRP/DSUSD AMM pool: from any XRPL wallet, a Payment to the account whose amount is DSUSD and which spends XRP crosses the pool, and the scheme books what arrives as a deposit, as anything else that raises the line. The issuer mints DSUSD only into the pool, never to a person, so no one takes the pool's XRP with DSUSD they did not pay for. The node buys none itself. The wallet's Receive says so ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)). Dev (`dsusd-settlement`, on the fake ledger only), the end-to-end tests and the simulations use the fake ledger's DSUSD issuer ([below](#tokens-in-the-fake-ledger), [Dev](dev.md#scenarios)).

## The peg guard

A dollar token is booked at par, so a node that converts at par while the token is off its peg can be drained ([issued-assets](../decisions/issued-assets.md)). The token scheme of a token that a pool prices has a guard (`peg.rs`): mainnet RLUSD by mainnet's RLUSD/XRP pool, testnet DSUSD by the testnet's DSUSD/XRP pool. Disabled test RLUSD has none.

- **The price.** The account reads the pool for the scheme once a minute, both its balances proven at its anchor in one state proof ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#4-what-is-proven-and-how)): the pool's `AMM` object, found by its asset pair, names its account, whose root holds the pool's XRP and whose line to the issuer its token. A pool read for the first time, or whose object names another account, is read again at once. The node hands the scheme its XRP/USD rate before each settlement check, and fetches XRP's rate for it even on a node with no XRP ledger. The price is the pool's ratio times that rate, in millionths of a dollar, exact and rounded down.
- **What counts.** A reading counts only if the pool holds at least the token's floor of XRP and the rate is fresh, by a bound shorter than the connector's, since XRP moves the price within that.
- **Hysteresis.** Pegged, the scheme pauses once every reading that counted has been outside a band around a dollar for a hold; off its peg, it runs again once every reading has been inside a narrower band for a longer hold; a gap in the readings longer than the staleness bound starts either hold again. Off upward or downward is the same pause.
- **Fails closed.** With no reading that counted for as long as a de-peg must last to trip it, a pegged guard pauses its scheme, unread. The next reading inside the band ends that; readings outside it trip the guard as from pegged. A guard new to a scheme starts pegged, as if read in the band when it started.
- **A pause, both ways.** The scheme settles nothing (`Unavailable`), cannot receive (`can_receive`), so the node asks no peer on it to settle, withdraws but never ends one short of room and leaves it out of the link state ([Settlement](settlement.md#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)), and converts nothing (`converts`): the price table leaves its ledger out, and nothing converts to or from it at spot either, not even another dollar into the owner's own endpoints ([Connector](connector.md#amounts-and-conversion)). A scheme the books stopped feeds its guard nothing more, so its ledger converts nothing until it runs again. What the line shows is still booked, a peer's settlement, a deposit, a withdrawal or the issuer's act alike. The scheme stays synced.
- **Kept and shown.** The guard's standing, its hold and its last reading are kept with the scheme's state, so a restart neither skips a recovery nor trips again. The node logs each pause and each resumption, and the ledger's view carries the guard's last price and the ledger it is as of; the owner's RPC and the wallet show it, with why the scheme is paused ([Wallet](wallet.md#pages)).
- **On dev timers** (`DASSIE_DEV_TIMINGS`, which dev and the end-to-end tests run), the guard runs the same rule a reading a second: each hold and the staleness bound are as many readings long as in production, so a token pauses in seconds and recovers in a minute. The rate's bound stays production's, since the node fetches rates as often on dev timers (`Timings` in `peg.rs`).

## Following the account

The scheme books **every validated transaction that touches its account once, in ledger order**, from its cursor `(ledger, index)`, kept in its state.

**Only what is proven** ([verified-ledgers](../decisions/verified-ledgers.md), [the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#4-what-is-proven-and-how)). Only an anchor, a ledger a quorum of the account's own UNL signed, moves what it takes as validated: the newest ledger, expiry, and the schemes' news of closed ledgers ([below](#anchors)). So no server can claim a testnet reset, or move the account's view back: a public address spreads its connections over several servers, one may serve a ledger minutes old, and an older anchor than the one kept is never current, so a server behind can only delay the account ([ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md)). What the account acts on, it proves (`account/history.rs`):

- **State, at the anchor.** Each anchor's state proof proves the account's root, so what it holds and its sequence, or that it does not exist; each line its schemes follow; those lines' issuers' roots; and the fee settings, so the reserve. An issuer's account or a pool a scheme asks for is proven at the anchor at once, in a state proof of its own.
- **Transactions, by their proofs.** A transaction is told only once `tx_proof` proves it, in the anchor or a ledger the anchor's skip lists hold, and is read from its bytes. Its bytes and metadata come from `tx` in binary once the proof came: the account builds the transaction's leaf from them and checks the proof's branch, which starts above the leaf, from there, never from a leaf the server sent. One in another ledger than the proven state names is a lie.
- **The history, from the state.** Each object the account follows names, as proven at the anchor, the transaction that last changed it, and each proven transaction's metadata the one before. From each anchor the account walks these chains, of its root and of each line its schemes follow, back to what it told, or on a connection to the earliest cursor of its schemes, and tells what it found in ledger order. The lines are walked as well as the root, since a transaction that changes only a line, such as a token's settlement or a clawback, leaves the root as it was. So a transaction a server leaves out of its history or its stream is found by its hash; the hints only name hashes sooner. The newest transaction that changed an object must leave it as the anchor proves it: a history the state proves wrong is a lie. A walk finds at most a bound of transactions (`MAX_WALKED`); what it does not reach is a gap, as below, and no new anchor is taken until it is done. A walk that left a gap tells the schemes the proven balance as when they sync, also while synced, so that they match their books to it at once.
- **The history bound.** Every proof server keeps at least a bound of ledgers of history (`HISTORY_BOUND`). A transaction the proven state names in a ledger further back than that from the anchor, which the server says it no longer has, is history it does not have: the walk leaves a gap there, which the schemes match through suspense, without who it was, and the gap is counted. Whether a ledger is beyond the bound is judged only on an index the account proved, never on one a hint names. Within the bound, a server that says it no longer has a ledger keeps too short a history: the account waits, says so, and connects anew a while later.
- **Hints.** The server's state and fee, the ledger stream, `submit`'s answers, `account_tx` and the account's stream steer what the account asks for, and never reach the books. The fee offered is bounded by its ceiling ([above](#the-account)), and the fee booked is the proven transaction's.

**Connecting.** On each connection: check the network, and take the server's fee as a hint; subscribe to the account and the ledger stream, as hints; anchor, the account's lists fetched first if it has none in effect, which proves the account and each line a scheme follows as of that one ledger; walk the history back to the earliest cursor of its schemes, with a page of `account_tx` as a hint; then the schemes make their books match the balance and the lines as proven, and the account submits what waits.

**Booking a validated transaction** by what it did to the account's XRP, for `xrpl-testnet`:

- **One of ours:** posted when it succeeded, voided when it failed, and its fee booked either way.
- **One another scheme on the account signed,** such as a token settlement or a `TrustSet`: its fee is a ledger fee here, since this is the ledger whose scheme settles in the account's XRP, and a line's reserve is reconciled as any reserve change is ([external-assets](../decisions/external-assets.md)). Whatever else it did to the XRP is a deposit or a withdrawal.
- **A settlement from a peer:** a successful Payment of XRP to us, whose memo has our memo type and names a peer, from the address that peer gave when peering. It is booked by `delivered_amount`, with its hash as the journal reference.
- **Anything else** that adds XRP, the account's creation included, is a deposit; anything that takes XRP, a withdrawal.

**Peers' addresses** are the scheme's. They are learnt only once a peering is made, never from an attempt that may still be refused, and kept after it ends, like the [settlement channel](settlement.md#settlement-channels) the peer's settlements come over, until that channel closes: then the address goes, and a Payment from it is the owner's deposit ([closing-channels](../decisions/closing-channels.md)). The scheme keeps at least as many as the node has open channels (asserted at start), so no stranger's attempt displaces a peer's address and turns its settlement into the owner's deposit.

After each, the books must hold the balance less the reserve; any difference, such as a reserve change or a balance below the reserve, is reconciled through `equity/suspense`. The part a change of the reserve explains (the base, the increment per object, or how many objects the account owns, as when a token scheme opens its line) is reconciled as the reserve's, down to the books holding nothing: expected, so the node logs it as information; the rest as a warning. **The reserve is never booked, so never spent.** A transaction of ours is settled whenever the ledger shows it, even behind the cursor: what the balance booked through suspense meanwhile comes back out.

**The first sync** books what the account holds above the reserve as the owner's deposit. Before it, a scheme books none of the history the account reads for the others, only its own transactions. Only then does the node peer on the scheme, so no peer's settlement is taken for that deposit ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-scheme-interface), [synced-before-peering](../decisions/synced-before-peering.md)). The cursor it sets is the record of that sync, kept with the scheme's state.

**The account gone** (a testnet reset). A reset starts the chain again from a first ledger far below the anchor, which the rule above alone would never act on. An anchor off the kept anchor's chain is on a new chain ([below](#anchors)): one below the kept anchor that closed after it, or one past it whose skip lists do not hold it. On the testnet only, the account then follows the new chain. Then, or when an anchor proves the account's root absent: each scheme books what its books hold as a loss through suspense and drops its cursor, everything in flight is cancelled (an account made again starts its sequence anew), and the faucet is asked until the account exists again. A scheme not on the account then does the same once back ([above](#the-account)). The node takes no new peering on the scheme until it has synced again.

## Settling

`prepare_settlement` is `Unavailable` ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-scheme-interface)) unless the account signs the Payment now ([above](#the-account)). `xrpl-testnet` settles whole drops, a token scheme whole millionths ([The token scheme](#the-token-scheme)), and the transaction signed is kept in flight, not yet executed.

- **Nothing is sent before it is booked.** A transaction is submitted only once the node executed its settlement, its pending transfer on disk. One never executed is never sent: past its last ledger it is dropped, the sequence read again, and the account remembers it as never sent until its scheme has reconciled it ([below](#state-and-restarts)). Its sequence goes to the next transaction, whichever scheme asked.
- **Cancelled only when it can never apply,** as proven ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#4-what-is-proven-and-how)): past its last ledger, by an anchor at which the account's proven sequence is not past its own, so nothing took its number in time; or once the history tells another transaction of the account that took its number. `submit`'s answers, a malformed transaction's (`tem…`) included, and a server that says it found nothing cancel nothing. Where the sequence moved past it and the history names nothing that took the number, as for an account deleted and made again, its own proof is asked for: found, it is booked; not found, it stays pending for the owner, and is counted. Anything less certain waits, since cancelling a transaction that applied would pay the peer twice.

## Settlement messages

The `settlement` message carries the transaction's hash, and books nothing: the ledger says what arrived, once. A Payment without the memo, from an address no peer gave, or that failed, is not a settlement.

## Deposits and withdrawals

The owner funds the scheme by sending XRP to its account. Otherwise a withdrawal is made outside the node, with the same keys, and booked when the ledger shows it.

**The owner's payments.** The node pays out of the account only to move the owner's funds into its settlement hub ([Settlement hubs](hubs.md#in-the-node)): `pay_owner` signs a Payment of whole drops of XRP, never partial, with a destination tag and one memo of its own memo type (`OWNER_MEMO_TYPE`), never a settlement's, and executes it at once, so its submission leaves with the batch that keeps it. One at a time; its ID is its hash. The scheme keeps it with its state, and what became of the last one, so a restart books it once and still answers for it; one it never read the outcome of (it validated while the scheme was stopped, and the history no longer reaches it) is unknown, never failed. One the account keeps for the scheme, validated while it was away ([above](#the-account)), is still on its way until the scheme reads it again, as its owner's payment; and the scheme reads its last payment again as the owner's, never as a settlement, booking it then only if it had given it up as unknown. The node holds it against the owner's funds while it is on its way ([Settlement](settlement.md#the-scheme-interface)): once validated, the hold is posted, as the owner's withdrawal, with its fee, or voided when it failed, and only its fee booked; one that can never apply voids it and books nothing. A withdrawal from the hub reaches the account from the hub's escrow, which no peer gave as its address, so it is the owner's deposit by the rule below.

The account takes payments with or without a destination tag: it is the node's alone, so tags tell nothing apart and are ignored.

**The owner may ask the faucet** of a test ledger for more test XRP (`AskFaucet`), as the account asks while it does not exist. It is the same request, at the same pace: at most one at a time and one a minute, so the owner adds no load the faucet would not see anyway. It is asked only on a connection, since its answer is taken on one. Nothing is booked at the asking: what the faucet sends is a deposit once the ledger shows it, by the rule above. A ledger without a faucet (the config's `faucet` is `null`, or mainnet, which has none) refuses; mainnet's account waits for the owner's deposit.

## State and restarts

Each keeps its own state, written with its checkpoint in the batch it explains ([Settlement](settlement.md#checkpoints-and-activation)): the account its next sequence, its transactions in flight (each with the scheme that signed it), those it remembers as never sent, those it keeps for a scheme not on it, the count of its write-offs, its anchor, and its validator lists and validators' manifests; each scheme its cursor, its peers' addresses, the settlements it looks up, with those of them it never read, the count of write-offs it last saw, the owner's payment on its way and what became of the last, and a token scheme its peg guard. What the ledger says of the lines and the issuer is read again on each connection, never kept. A state either could not have written is refused as it loads, rather than stopping the node later: an account state that names a scheme that cannot settle through the account, or a scheme state that looks up a transaction the account has in flight or remembers as never sent. So is a scheme state that still holds the account's: the database migrates one written before the account kept its own into the two (`store/schema.rs`), and a node that restarts on it books nothing anew.

After a restart, executed transactions in flight are submitted again once synced. Each settlement the node still holds pending is handed to its scheme:

- **In flight:** submitted again.
- **Never sent:** cancelled at once. It was signed and booked but never executed, and dropped past its last ledger, or it was found never to apply, or the account was written off, while its scheme was not on the account: either way no ledger will ever hold it, and a scheme that stopped and starts again cancels it rather than leave it pending for good.
- **Neither:** looked up by its hash and posted or voided by what the ledger says, unless the scheme reads it again first as one kept for it ([above](#the-account)), and posts or voids it then, with its fee. One the scheme read before the restart it booked then, with its fee and what it did to the balance or the line; one it never read ([above](#the-account)) it books once found, with its fee, and what the balance or the line booked through suspense comes back out. It is looked up by its proof wherever the server finds it, and taken once its ledger is proven: when it was submitted is not kept, and needs not be. A transaction of ours that no history holds any more cannot be known to have applied, and its settlement stays pending, for the owner; it is asked for again less often the longer it is not found, so a lookup that never succeeds costs little.

## The fake ledger

The feature `fake` models the ledger for simulations and tests (`fake.rs`): accounts, reserves, fees, the checks the ledger makes on a Payment with their result codes, close times, streams, history that can be cut short, network IDs, testnet resets that start the chain again from a lower ledger, a faucet, trust lines with the issuers of DSUSD on its testnet and RLUSD on its mainnet ([below](#tokens-in-the-fake-ledger)), AMM pools of XRP and a token ([below](#pools-in-the-fake-ledger)), who may sign for an account ([below](#keys-in-the-fake-ledger)), and the proofs of a patched rippled, with validators of its own and a server that lies ([below](#proofs-in-the-fake-ledger)). A copy of it is a server behind the others, stopped where the ledger was. In-process its close times are fixed steps, so a simulation stays deterministic; served by `fake_ledger`, its ledgers close on the wall clock, so a new server is a new chain that closes later, and nodes that knew an earlier one see a testnet reset. It answers the subset of the rippled WebSocket API the account speaks, and checks what it is sent with the node's own codec. It is the testnet, or **mainnet** (`FakeLedger::mainnet`): mainnet's network ID, no faucet, so owners deposit, never a reset, RLUSD's mainnet issuer, and none of the testnet's test tokens. The node simulation settles on it in-process, in the live realm on its mainnet; `fake_ledger` serves it over WebSocket to the node's tests, the end-to-end tests and `node --run dev -- --xrpl fake`, as the testnet or as mainnet (`--network mainnet`), whose server has no faucet. Beside the faucet, at `/deposit`, XRP arrives from outside, as an owner funds an account, on either network.

### Tokens in the fake ledger

The fake ledger is the only source of dollar tokens on demand for dev, the end-to-end tests and the simulations: DSUSD comes only from a pool, and RLUSD on mainnet is real money. So it models what a token scheme meets on either network, and what it cannot get from the real one on demand: freezes and clawbacks (`fake/tokens.rs`).

- **Its issuers are DSUSD's on the testnet and RLUSD's on mainnet,** each at its address on its network (test RLUSD, whose scheme is disabled, is on neither), with RLUSD's settings: lines ripple through them, they require no authorisation, and they keep the right to freeze and to claw back. A scheme with either token's constants runs on the fake unchanged. An issuer outlives a reset, as one that came back at its address; its lines and what they held do not. A test can add issuers, so a line is seen from both sides, whichever account's ID is the lower number.
- **Trust lines** are kept as the ledger keeps them ([Tokens](#tokens)): the balance from the low side, each side's limit and flags, and an owner reserve on each side not in its default state, normally the holder's. A `TrustSet` sets the sender's limit and flags, with the ledger's refusals, such as a deep freeze on a line not frozen, or a line the account lacks the reserve for (its first two are free). A line back in its default state on both sides is deleted, and its reserve freed.
- **A token Payment** goes over the line between the two when either issues the token, else through the issuer, and fails as the ledger fails it. Nothing to pay with, no line, a payer frozen, a payee deep-frozen, or a global freeze between holders: `tecPATH_DRY`. Less than all of it, above the payee's limit, a `SendMax` of the token below the amount, or an issuer's transfer fee, which the fake takes from no Payment, with `SendMax` or without: `tecPATH_PARTIAL`. No payee: `tecNO_DST`. A frozen line still receives, and still pays its issuer. A failure costs its fee and moves no token.
- **The issuer acts on request,** without a key: it mints (a Payment to a holder), freezes and deep-freezes a line (a `TrustSet` of its own), freezes every line or sets a transfer fee (`AccountSet`), and claws back (`Clawback`, which lowers the line with no Payment). Each applies in the next ledger as any transaction does, with the metadata, delivered amount, history and streams the ledger gives: both accounts of a line hear what changes it, and an `AccountSet` reaches only the issuer's own stream. `ledger_entry` reads a line, and `account_info` the issuer's flags and transfer rate. `fake_ledger` takes these requests over HTTPS next to the faucet, under `/tokens/`, for dev and the end-to-end tests, of the issuer of its network's dollar unless a request names another of the fake's tokens.
- **Its arithmetic is exact.** Where the ledger would round a balance to sixteen digits, the fake refuses the transaction (`tecPRECISION_LOSS`) instead of guessing how the ledger rounds; a scheme books by what the line holds, which covers the ledger's rounding too. As the XRP that exists never changes, every token's holders hold exactly what its issuer minted, less what came back to it, was clawed back or wiped, after every ledger.

### Pools in the fake ledger

The fake keeps a constant-product pool of XRP and a token, one per token, which the token's issuer makes or sets on request (`fake/pools.rs`): no liquidity provider deposits or withdraws; the issuer sets what the pool holds and its trading fee with a transaction of the fake's own, minting or taking back the token and moving the XRP from or to genesis. `fake_ledger` takes it under `/tokens/`, for dev and the end-to-end tests: the pool of its network's dollar unless the request names another of the fake's tokens.

- **A pool's assets are its own account's,** as on the ledger: its XRP in its account root, its token on its line to the issuer. So the XRP and every token still add up after every ledger, a swap's metadata shows what it did to each, and a reset wipes the pools with the other accounts. The fake derives the account from the token, so a pool made again after a reset has the same one. It takes no Payment (`tecNO_PERMISSION`), as the ledger's pool accounts take none.
- **A Payment with `SendMax` crosses the pool,** XRP for its token or its token for XRP, at the price of [AMM pools](#amm-pools), with the metadata, delivered amount, history and streams of any Payment, and fails as the ledger fails it. No pool, another token, a token for another token, or no XRP above the payer's reserve: `tecPATH_DRY`. A price above `SendMax`, all the pool holds or more, or more XRP than the payer holds above its reserve: `tecPATH_PARTIAL`. The token's own failures are a token Payment's: no line, a freeze, a limit.
- **Its arithmetic is exact,** as for tokens. Where the ledger would round the payer's balance or the pool's, the fake instead rounds a price in the token up, a digit at a time, until both hold their balances exactly (`FakeLedger::quote`), so a payer pays less than a unit of their last shared digit above the pool's price; a balance it still cannot hold fails the Payment (`tecPRECISION_LOSS`). A swap that lowered the pool's product would be a bug, and is asserted against.
- **`amm_info`** reads a pool by its two assets, in either order and answered in that order, or by its account, with the LP tokens a pool made at its balances would have issued.

### Keys in the fake ledger

The fake checks who signs as the ledger does (`fake/signers.rs`), so that a hub's escrow, its setup and its payout can be tested against it.

- **An account signs** with its master key unless it is disabled, with its regular key, or with signers of its signer list whose weights reach its quorum, each with its own account's master key (an account that does not exist has only that) or regular key. Disabling the master key takes the master key's own signature and another way to sign; removing the regular key or the signer list when it is the only other way is refused (`tecNO_ALTERNATIVE_KEY`), so an account is never locked.
- **Checked twice.** Signatures are checked when a transaction is submitted (`temBAD_SIGNATURE`), since they cannot change. Whether its keys may sign is checked then, and again when it applies, against the account as it is by then. A transaction whose keys may no longer sign applies nothing, costs nothing and uses no sequence (`tef`). So once one payout set's signer list replaced the escrow's signers, no transaction signed for another list applies, and two payout sets never mix ([settlement hubs](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#9-shutdown)).
- **A signer list** is one object toward its owner's reserve, however many entries it has, as the ledger counts every list made since its `MultiSignReserve` amendment; it is kept sorted by account, and the ledger's refusals of a list are the codec's (`check_signer_list`). `account_objects` lists it with the account's trust lines, so a validator can check that an escrow owns nothing else.
- **A destination tag,** once an account requires one: a Payment to it without one fails (`tecDST_TAG_NEEDED`) and costs its fee, the fake's own deposits too. The tag is in the transaction as the streams, `account_tx` and `tx` give it, which is how a hub tells whose deposit it is.
- **What the fake does not model, it refuses** rather than ignores: an `AccountSet` of any setting but those two (`temINVALID_FLAG`). The first `SetRegularKey` costs its fee, where the ledger lets the master key send it free.

### Proofs in the fake ledger

The fake proves what it holds as a proof server would ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#11-the-fake-ledger-the-lying-server-and-the-simulation)), so the verifier is tested against a ledger, not against itself (`fake/chain.rs`, `fake/objects.rs`, `fake/meta.rs`, `fake/validators.rs`, `fake/proofs.rs`, `fake/lies.rs`).

- **Each closed ledger is real.** Its header binds its parent's hash, the root of a tree of the transactions it applied and the root of a tree of its state, built as rippled builds them. Every transaction is there in binary with its binary metadata, the fake's own included (deposits from genesis, the issuers' acts, a pool set), and the JSON it answers with is rendered from those bytes, so what it says and what it proves are one. The state holds every account root, trust line, signer list and pool in rippled's binary form under its key, the skip lists of ledger hashes and the fee settings as rippled keeps them, and each object names the transaction that last changed it, as the metadata does. Objects captured from mainnet pin the layouts and keys, and a test reads every kind of transaction, metadata and object the fake writes field by field as its JSON.
- **What a test sets by hand** outside any transaction, such as an account's sequence, shows in the next ledger's state naming no transaction; what it does at the fake's start names none either.
- **Validators and a publisher.** Validators enough that one can be withheld and the rest still make a quorum, with master and signing keys and manifests, sign each ledger as it closes; a publisher signs their list. Every key comes from the fake's seed, so a fake of another seed shares no key with it; the fake mainnet's seed is another than the testnet's, so their validators share none, as live. A test rotates a validator's key, withholds it, revokes it, and publishes a new list, which may expire; a reset keeps the validators, and new ones can be keyed with a new list.
- **Validations are signed for real,** or in a simulation with a keyed hash under a secret that only the fake and the check a simulation substitutes hold (`KeyedSignatures`), as the design asks. Either way they are signed only when a server is asked for them.
- **The methods** `validations`, `tx_proof`, `state_proof` and `manifest` answer as the design's shapes say, and as the live proof servers do, whose captured answers the same parsers read: no bytes beside a branch, a state object only in the leaf its branch starts at, and a transaction's branch above its leaf, which the node builds from `tx` or `account_tx` in binary. They answer at any ledger the fake keeps: headers for days of ledgers, and the state of the recent ones and of every flag ledger, so any ledger kept is two state proofs from the newest. Like rippled, an honest fake gives the validations of the ledgers of the last ten minutes only, and the skip list reaches the rest. A ledger beyond them is `lgrNotFound`. `fake_ledger` serves the list over HTTPS beside its faucet, and prints what a config names to trust its validators, and where it takes requests (`control`) to rotate, withhold or revoke a validator's key, publish a list, or make every connection lie; in-process, tests answer the account's fetches of the list's site (`LIST_SITE`), with configs that name the fake's publisher (`with_publishers`). A ledger closes a second after its parent at least, as rippled's do, on the clock of the test or simulation that closes it, so the nodes read its validations as fresh.
- **A lying server.** A connection, or every connection, can be made to tell one of the lies the design lists (`Lie`), each made from the honest answer, so it is as close to the truth as a lie can be; a stock server that serves no proofs is one of them. A lie holds no key and no secret, so it withholds and replays validations, and makes up everything else. Each lie told is counted. A test catches every one with the verifier: by a quorum short, an anchor older than the one kept, a branch that does not hold, a header no anchor proves, or, for history left out or a hint of an old ledger, by the history the proven state names, of the account's root and of its lines.
- **Against the node.** The `node` simulation draws a lying server as a fault, and a scripted run tells every kind in turn, while a validator rotates its key, a quorum is withheld and a list lapses; each node's books must hold to the fake's truth throughout, and every account counts what it proved, so a run shows that it went through the verifier ([Testing](testing.md#the-simulations)). Two daemons over `fake_ledger` and the end-to-end tests meet a lying server, one without proofs and a withheld quorum: each waits with its reason, books nothing meanwhile, not even a deposit that arrived, and books it once and settles as before when the server is honest again ([Testing](testing.md#end-to-end)).

- [ledger-resets](../decisions/ledger-resets.md): only what is newer than the anchor; a reset is an anchor off the kept one's chain, and only on the testnet.
