# A node funds a largest packet per peer in each hub, and takes no more peers than its pool covers once it cannot, ending downlinks first

**Areas:** [Network](../areas/network.md#slots-by-liquidity), [Settlement hubs](../areas/hubs.md#in-the-node), [Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#credit).

## Facts

- Over a hub, the credit a node extends a peer is its hold toward it, funded when the peer asks ([Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#credit)). What the node can have on hold toward its peers over a hub, its pool, is its balance there and its holds toward them. A hold below one largest packet carries no packet.
- The slot caps count peerings, not money: a core node keeps up to `max_peers` core peers and `max_downlinks` downlinks, whatever its hub holds. A node whose pool is far below a packet for each takes newcomers all the same, and every peer it adds spreads the pool thinner, for everyone routing through it.
- The node keeps a target in its hub, a generous default or one its owner sets, and deposits back up to it from the owner's account on the ledger while that account has funds ([generous-hub-target](generous-hub-target.md)). The target counts the account's reserve and a reserve per hold of ours, beside the pool, so a hub at its target can hold far less than a packet per peering, with funds on the owner's own account. A deposit takes a funding check to start and up to `CREDIT_WAIT_MS` to be credited before it is late.
- A core node's core peers carry the routes that make it a core node; its downlinks only use it as their uplink. A node outside the core, by the core it computes ([promotion-to-core](promotion-to-core.md)), reaches the network only through its uplinks, and its owner may pin them; one that others still route through keeps its downlinks meanwhile.
- An attacker can fake any least amount of activity, and makes downlink keys for nothing ([free-receivers-half](free-receivers-half.md)). The peering's age, which side asked, its kind and whether the peer ever paid a largest packet are what it cannot fake.
- The pool moves a settlement at a time: funding a hold moves money within it, a peer taking from our hold toward it lowers it, and a deposit, or what the node takes from a peer's hold toward it, raises it. One request to settle funds a hold by at most what `policy::hub_spendable` gives for one peering.
- A peering may settle over several hubs, one method per hub ([settlement-methods](settlement-methods.md)).

## Decision

- **Funds first.** The node's funding keeps each hub's pool at a packet for each peering over it and one more, with the margin below (`Standing::least_pool`): it deposits what the pool lacks from the owner's account, past the hub's ceiling if need be, as much as the account can pay less the gas it keeps, and a withdrawal never takes the pool below it. A packet per peer is a bounded exception to the target, never the default credit per peer: it funds the reserve of a largest packet each that the allotment sets aside for every other peer ([hold-allotment](hold-allotment.md)), counting the same peerings. So slots are cut only for want of funds.
- **A packet each.** At each funding check, for each hub it is in, the node compares its pool with one largest packet for each peering with a method over the hub, those being ended or winding that method down aside. A peering over two hubs counts toward each; each hub is judged on its own pool.
- **Newcomers first, core before downlinks.** A new downlink over the hub, peering or method, is taken only while the pool covers every peering there and it too; a new core peer while the pool covers the core peers there and it too. A newcomer refused is refused for capacity, as past the caps, and the node asks none over the hub (`PeeringHooks::takes_newcomer`): it asks a new peer over the first hub they share that takes one, and a method over this hub once it does. A node with no core peer over the hub takes one all the same.
- **Refusals lift past a margin.** Once refused, newcomers are taken again only when the pool covers their need and one request to settle's funding more (`policy::hub_spendable` for one peering at the hub's target, which caps it at half a step: smaller under a small target the owner set, as every move then is), so one settlement either way does not flip the node's caps. Nothing is re-added by force: the caps come back, and newcomers fill them as before.
- **Ends only once the node cannot fund the pool, and after a grace.** The pool is judged short only while no deposit of the node's own can come: its owner's account cannot pay, its moves are off, paused or given up, its deposits back off after a failure, or the hub is unavailable; while a deposit is due, or on its way and not late, no grace runs. Once the pool has stayed short of its peerings so for a funding check and a deposit's credit time (`liquidity::grace_ms`), in which a deposit the owner makes, or the node makes once its backoff ends, can land, the node ends as many as bring the pool back to a packet each, and waits a grace again before the next round. The grace starts again whenever the pool covers every peering, so a dip hours later gets a fresh one.
- **Who goes, by what nobody can fake:** downlinks over the hub first, those that never paid us before those that did, the newest first; core peers only once no downlink is left over the hub, those that asked us before those we asked, the newest first, never the last, and only by a node that is core in its own view.
- **A peering over another hub loses only this method,** wound down, collecting first; one with no other is ended, collecting first, for the node's liquidity (`ShortOfLiquidity`). The message says no reason: the peer did nothing, so no cooldown follows, and while the node refuses newcomers, it refuses the peer's request to come back.
- **A node outside the core refuses more uplinks and ends none of them.** Its uplinks are all the network it has, and pinned ones are its owner's choice. The downlinks it still keeps while others route through it go as a core node's do.
- **The owner sees it** with the hub: the pool, the peerings it covers, which newcomers are refused, since when the pool is short and from when the node ends peerings (`GetHubStatus`, `dassie hub`, the wallet's Hubs page).
- **This amends [free-receivers-half](free-receivers-half.md),** whose rule only refuses: a downlink, free or paying, may be ended for the node's own liquidity, as any peering may be ended for its silence or its room.

## Accepted

- **An honest downlink is ended for its uplink's shortage,** not for anything it did. A pool short of a packet for each peering could not carry them all at once; ending the newest few keeps the rest whole, and the downlink peers with another uplink.
- **Idle peers count in full.** A hold is funded on request, so an idle peer costs the pool nothing until it asks. Counting what peers hold now would let a burst of requests find the pool empty; a packet each is what one payment through every peer needs.
- **A node may keep more than its target** in a hub with many peerings or a small target the owner set: a packet per peering and one more, and the margin. That is bounded by the peerings' bounds, a packet each, far below the default credit per peer.
- **Newcomers wait for a deposit.** A newcomer that arrives while the pool covers no more is refused until the deposit it calls for lands, a few seconds; the funding asks for it a peering and the margin ahead, so this is rare while the owner's account can pay.
- **The state is in memory.** A restart judges anew and starts the grace again, so an end may come a grace later; it never comes sooner.
- **A refusal is up to a funding check late.** A newcomer that arrives between a peering that filled the pool and the next check is taken, and may be ended after the grace if the pool stays short.
- **A newcomer is refused for capacity and backs off** as from a full node; it does not learn why.

## Rejected

- *Ending by activity or by earned fees:* an attacker fakes any least activity, and earns fees by paying them; honest nodes are idle most of the time ([free-receivers-half](free-receivers-half.md)).
- *Ending the oldest first:* the oldest are those that stayed; the newest are most likely keys made to fill the places.
- *Ending core peers alongside downlinks:* core peers carry the node's routes; a core node short of its downlinks keeps its core peers whole first.
- *Judging short on the pool alone,* with the funding following only the target: the target counts the reserves, so with the default founding terms (a largest packet of 0.1 XRP, an account reserve of 20 packets, a hold reserve of 2 per hold, up to 64 holds) a node at the floor of the default target, 900 packets, holds a pool of 752 to 880, and would refuse and end downlinks from about 750 peerings, below `max_downlinks`; at an owner's target of 50 packets with 10 peerings holding, the pool is 5 to 10 packets. All with funds on the owner's own account.
- *A least pool without the newcomer and the margin:* the node would refuse a newcomer at every peering until the deposit for it landed, while its account could pay.
- *Ending at once, without a grace:* a deposit the owner makes, or the node makes once its backoff ends, would land a few minutes later, and the peers ended meanwhile would be gone for nothing ([withdraw readily, end reluctantly](../areas/network.md#lifecycle)).
- *A grace of a day, as for a peer short of room:* the node would carry every peer short of a packet for a day; a deposit that has not landed within its credit time is late, and the owner is told.
- *Lowering the caps for every hub when one is short:* a peering over a hub that covers it costs the short one nothing.
- *Ending the whole peering over two hubs:* the hub that covers it would lose a peer for another hub's shortage.
- *Leaving the hub out of the link state while it takes no core peer,* as for a hub peers cannot pay: nobody would ask in vain, but the node's snapshot would stop quoting the hub to the peers it keeps, and each refusal, and each lift, would flood a new link state through the network.
- *A new termination reason on the wire:* `dassie/2` keeps the reasons it has, and the peer has nothing to do differently; the node keeps the reason to itself.
- *A cooldown after such an end:* the peer earned none, and the refusal keeps it out while the shortage lasts.
- *Refusing a node its first core peer over a hub:* a core node without one routes nothing over the hub, and a node outside the core reaches nothing; nor does the node ever end its last.
