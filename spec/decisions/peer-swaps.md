# Nodes swap assets with their peers, priced by snapshots of ladders per asset

**Areas:** [Connector](../areas/connector.md#routing), [Rate feed](../areas/rate-feed.md), [Peer protocol](../areas/peer-protocol.md), [Network](../areas/network.md#settlement-methods), [Market maker VM](../areas/market-maker-vm.md). Built. Where [passive-rebalancing](passive-rebalancing.md) has market makers bring a node back toward balance, this decides how they trade with it, and that the node trades with its peers itself.

## Facts

- A core node that converts drifts, and its curve pays whoever brings back what it lacks ([passive-rebalancing](passive-rebalancing.md)). Without swaps, only market makers act on that, and only by a payment through the node: from a node of theirs paid in one asset to another of theirs paid in the other, since the connector never sends a packet back to the peer it came from, and an owner's own endpoints never convert ([owner-ledger](owner-ledger.md)).
- A STREAM payment's packets are cut by its sender's own rules, and its rate is fixed when it is quoted. A snapshot's promise is per step of its ladders, so a payer can hold a payment to it only by quoting first, and then pays at the quote.
- A peering has a settlement method on every hub both nodes are in, each on a ledger of its own ([settlement-methods](settlement-methods.md)). Two peers in hubs of two assets can pay each other in either within one peering.
- The connector never sends a packet back to the peer it came from, and keeps a packet's ledger where it can ([Connector](../areas/connector.md#routing)). A packet from A to itself through B neither comes back nor converts.
- ILP reserves addresses under `peer.` for the link they are sent on: they are never routed. Dassie uses one, `peer.config`, at which the connector answers ILDCP from every endpoint, peers included, before routing ([Connector](../areas/connector.md)).
- A Prepare holds the room it travels in, on every method it crosses, until it resolves. Learning a price by sending a packet costs both peers that room.
- A pair's price at a node is spot times the curve's ratio of the two assets' weights, so the price of every pair follows from what the node quotes per asset. The curve caps that ratio, so the bonus is bounded on the pair, not per asset. Another curve could bound or couple assets otherwise.
- Rates per pair grow with the square of the methods a node names: a datagram holds a rate for every pair of eight methods of the longest IDs, and sixteen would take twice the datagram for the rates alone. Each says only what a vanishingly small packet gets, not how fast the price falls with size. A feed's subscribers, proven by a cookie, share one bounded table with anyone on the internet, which refuses newcomers when full.
- The price table prices every ledger the node runs, owner books included, since the curve counts each asset whole ([Connector](../areas/connector.md#inventory-pricing)); a price into or out of an owner book is one no packet from a peer can get. Each scheme serves one ledger, `<scheme>+<asset>` ([hub-scheme-names](hub-scheme-names.md)), and a peer or a market maker knows a node's methods by their schemes' IDs.
- In a hub, the payer signs a settlement and pays its gas.
- A version of the peer protocol may change its layouts until a release speaks it ([Peer protocol](../areas/peer-protocol.md#versions)); a feed version's layout is frozen once it exists ([Rate feed](../areas/rate-feed.md#wire-format)). Version 2 of each was on `main`, and no release carried either yet.

## Decision

- **A swap is a Prepare to `peer.swap.<scheme ID>`.** A sends it to B on one method of their peering. B takes any destination under `peer.` itself, before routing: `peer.config` is ILDCP, answered as now, `peer.swap.<scheme>` names the method to send it back on, and any other gets F02. B converts it as transit, by its curve, fee and spread, and sends it back to A on the named method with its destination, condition and data unchanged and its expiry one message window earlier. A takes a `peer.swap.<scheme>` that comes on the method it names as one of its own swaps coming back, so a node never forwards a swap that names the method it came on, and fulfills it only if the condition is its own and the amount meets its minimum. A swap is never routed, so the routing rules stay as they are, and its fee counts toward no hop's weight, as a converting packet's does not ([weighted-next-hops](weighted-next-hops.md)): with the curve's move, a swap that brings B what it lacks can pay B less than spot.
- **B never reads the data.** It is A's: A may derive the fulfillment from it, as STREAM does, so it keeps no table of swaps in flight; if B changed it, A could not fulfill. B forwards a swap whenever its own checks pass, and A rejects what it does not want.
- **Two owner settings, both on by default: offering swaps and swapping.** Only a core node offers swaps, to every peer, downlinks included: a periphery node forwards nothing. A core node swaps by itself toward its curve's targets. A periphery node has no drift, and what its owner holds is the owner's choice, so it swaps by itself only toward targets its owner sets, none by default. Any node swaps when its owner asks, as a market maker does.
- **A swaps when it gains.** A swaps with B while B's quotes, valued at A's own curve, clear one plus A's costs of the swap: the gas of its settlements on the method it pays on, per least settlement, and its moves between the hub and the owner book that funds it ([Settlement hubs](../areas/hubs.md#in-the-node)). A computes them alone; no message carries them. B's costs are in B's price.
- **No room is set aside for swaps.** A swap refused for want of room (T04, T05) waits for it as any packet does, never longer than the room takes to come back ([swaps-wait-for-room](swaps-wait-for-room.md)). The caps on money in flight are per peer, method and direction, so swaps fill only the link between A and B.
- **A node publishes snapshots, not a feed.** A snapshot is the node's quotes at one moment, signed by its node key, made at most once per change and cached.
  - **Peers subscribe over the peer protocol.** A subscriber gets every snapshot the node makes, on the feed's schedule: at most once per interval while quotes change, a heartbeat otherwise. A node that offers no swaps refuses. A subscription ends with the peering.
  - **Anyone else asks once over UDP.** A request padded to at least the largest snapshot gets the cached snapshot, and nothing else: no cookie, no subscriber table, nothing kept per address, and a budget on answers per second. A market maker uses it to choose whom to peer with. A node that offers no swaps, a periphery node among them, answers no request.
- **Quotes per method; a pair's price is a product.** Per method the node offers its peers, never an owner book or any ledger no peer pays the node on: a tag of the scheme's ID (a truncated hash; a reader can use only methods whose schemes it runs, so it knows their IDs), and a bid and an ask in the node's virtual accounting unit per unit of the method's ledger, fee and spread folded in. A reader prices a swap from one method to another as the one's bid over the other's ask. **The promise is only that this never says more than the node gives,** at any size the ladders cover. How the node keeps it is its own: ours clamps its bid at the cap its curve puts on any pair. The format carries no cap, no spot and no parameter of any curve.
- **Each quote has a ladder.** For each of the drops of the grid in the snapshot's header, the most that can come in (bid) or go out (ask) on the method with its last unit still getting at least the quote less the drop. The first amount is a 16-bit float, each later one an 8-bit step up from the one before, every amount rounded down. A side the node does not convert has a quote of zero and an all-zero ladder; a method whose ledger the node does not price now, for want of a fresh rate or a dollar token off its peg, is listed with both sides so, so a reader tells a method offered but not converting from one not offered.
- **Ladders count each asset whole,** the hub's ledger with the owner book that funds it, as the curve does: they say how much is worth swapping, not how much the hub can pay out now. A swap the hub cannot fund gets T04, as any packet does, while the node refills its hub.
- **Errors at B, as for transit:** F02 when it offers no swaps or the scheme is not a method of the peering; F08 above its largest packet, with the most that passes; T04 when the method is unavailable or short of funds; T05 at the cap on money in flight; T00 without a fresh rate. Which code A rejects with is A's.
- **Versions 2, while no release carries them; new versions otherwise.** The peer protocol's version 2 took the subscription and the snapshot before a release spoke it, as its rule allows. The feed's version 2 was replaced by the snapshot, signed under its OID, before a release carried it, as a narrow exception to its frozen layout for a version nobody ran. Releases carry both now, so a changed snapshot is version 3 of the feed under an OID of its own, and changed messages go in `dassie/3`.
- **A market maker is one node,** a periphery node with an uplink to each target over two methods. `dassie market-maker` reads the snapshots its node is sent and swaps through a target when its market and the quotes differ, asking its node for the swap at no less than the rate its market makes worth it. Its node offers no swaps and swaps by itself toward no target of its owner's, so it swaps only when asked.

## Accepted

- **A node trades on its own,** where [passive-rebalancing](passive-rebalancing.md) left trading to its peers. It does so only through its peerings, at a price its peer's curve sets, with no new keys or venues.
- **Swaps spread drift; they do not remove it.** Where the network's flow runs one way, every node drifts alike, and new inventory still comes only from market makers and owners. Swaps let one market maker reach every node through the nodes it trades with.
- **Readers swap less than they could.** The promise lets a node understate: our clamp is exact for two assets and understates pairs whose other side is not the node's most plentiful asset; an 8-bit step is up to one step low. A model of the curve against many skews and sizes showed 8-bit steps within one point of 16-bit floats after three rounds, the rest taken at the next snapshot.
- **The grid's finest step bounds the smallest gain a reader sees.** A swap whose margin is below it shows nothing worth taking. The grid is in the header, so it changes without a new version.
- **A swap below A's minimum holds B's room on the method back for a round trip,** since B does not read A's minimum.
- **Swaps fill a link that payments use.** A peer's swaps can make its own payments over that link wait (T05), and while in flight make B's prices for other packets worse, never better, as any packet in flight does ([passive-rebalancing](passive-rebalancing.md#accepted)).
- **A snapshot shows a node's skew and roughly its scale** to its peers and to anyone who asks.
- **Tags can collide.** A node never offers two methods whose IDs share a tag: a test checks every scheme's, and a snapshot lists neither of two that did.
- **A spoofed request reflects one to one:** the node sends the victim no more than the spoofer sent it.
- **Two methods of one holding,** once a node may be in several hubs of an asset, must price at spot less the charges, which a bid over an ask cannot say. That needs which holding each method is in: a new version then. Until it, a snapshot lists neither of two methods the node prices as one asset.

## Rejected

- *Learning prices by sending swaps:* every probe holds room on both peers for a round trip.
- *Peers subscribing to the UDP feed:* they share a table with strangers who can fill it, and prove their address by a cookie the peering already proves.
- *A swap message of its own:* a standard Prepare under `peer.` carries it, and a swap is an ordinary packet to everything that counts packets.
- *An address beneath A routed back to it* (`<A>.swap.<scheme>`): routing would need an exception for packets addressed back to their source, and every node would parse a convention in addresses.
- *B reading A's minimum from the data:* it would standardize and freeze a format that is A's to choose.
- *Rates per pair:* they grow with the square of the methods, and say nothing of size.
- *Ledgers by ID:* owner books would be quoted, and prices into and out of them, which no peer can use, and a reader would have to know that a hub's ledger is its method's.
- *Cutting the methods at the datagram's bound:* a snapshot would leave out what a node converts at without saying so. A node lists at most one method per ledger of its realm, which fits by construction.
- *Leaving out a method the node does not convert on now:* a reader could not tell a method offered but paused, for want of a fresh rate or a peg, from one not offered.
- *Publishing the curve's inputs* (its parameter, the holdings and their targets): exact, but it makes the curve part of the format, and shows every holding.
- *A polynomial for the price:* valid only over the range it was fitted on, not monotone, and unable to state hard limits.
- *A cap and a spot per method in the format:* the cap on a pair is our curve's, not every curve's.
- *Ladders capped at what the hub holds:* a few largest packets of depth, for a node that holds far more and refills its hub as it drains.
- *Room set aside for swaps:* a packet in flight cannot be taken back for a payment, and swaps only fill their own link.
- *A fixed grid in the format:* tuning it would take a new version.
- *Every node swapping toward the curve's default targets:* it would move a periphery owner's holdings unasked.
- *Settling one method's debt in another's asset:* rejected in [settlement-methods](settlement-methods.md#rejected); a swap converts at the curve, per packet.
- *Market makers of two nodes, one per asset, paying each other through a target:* each trade is a STREAM payment, quoted before it is paid since its packets are not the snapshot's steps; the funds of each asset sit on a node of their own, with a phrase of its own; and a core node short of an asset waits for a market maker, since it cannot swap through its own peerings.
- *Market makers asking targets for snapshots over UDP:* a market maker peered with its targets is pushed their snapshots over the peering it swaps over, verified there.
