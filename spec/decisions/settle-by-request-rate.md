# A payer over a hub sizes its fundings by how often it is asked

**Areas:** [Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#settling-on-request), [Settlement hubs](../areas/hubs.md#in-the-node)

## Facts

- Only the creditor can tell when a peer should settle, so it asks, and the debtor decides how much ([own-fulfills](own-fulfills.md)). A payer over a hub funds its hold toward the payee as if it extended the default credit, eleven of the largest packets, with its hold counted as paid in advance: half the way from there down to its trigger, about four and three quarters of the largest packet for a payee at its trigger ([Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#credit)). The node keeps that much spendable in its hub per peering over it, its floor.
- A payee asks as soon as its room falls to the trigger, one and a half packets ([settle-at-the-trigger](settle-at-the-trigger.md)), and a payer that cannot fund a request funds it once it can ([settle-when-funds-arrive](settle-when-funds-arrive.md)). Swapping over the fake ledger's hubs, each funding then took about 0.75 s from the request to the payer's books, each cycle moved five to seven and a half packets, and a link at its peak drained four to six packets a second: the packet and a half below the trigger ran out before the funding landed, and the payee's Prepares were refused (T04) for the rest of the wait. Senders back off on a refusal, the swap service from a tenth of a second, doubling with each refusal in a row, up to thirty, so a link carried about one packet a second on average.
- A model of swaps over hubs found that a payer that doubles its step when asked again soon after its last funding landed, and halves it when idle, moved two to three times as much on large nodes and slow hubs; a larger fixed default credit locked more money and moved less.
- The payer knows when it is asked, what it funded and when that landed, and nothing of the payee's room. The time between a funding landing and the next request is how long the payee took to spend down to its trigger what that funding brought.
- In the same runs, the payer was asked again a median of 3.1 to 3.4 s after a funding landed, and within one landing time of it in 2 of 178 cycles, within four in 79: the swap service's backoffs, not the step, spaced the requests. What one request funded already grew with what the payer owed the payee from its hold and had not yet been taken: 6.1 to 6.6 packets on average, against the 4.75 of a payer owing nothing.

## Decision

- **A pace per method of a peering over a hub.** The payer counts a credit for each, the default to begin with, and funds a request as if it extended that credit, its hold paid in advance, as before with the default. So a step grows with the credit counted, and what the hold toward the peer keeps beyond what the payer already owes it stays below that credit less the trigger, however the peer asks.
- **It doubles when asked again soon.** A request that comes within four of the last funding's landing times after that funding landed, its landing time counted from the request it answered, doubles the credit counted: the payee spent a step in less than four times what a step takes to arrive, so it waits for fundings a fifth of its time or more. Each landing counts once, and a request that finds a settlement on its way, which is the answer, counts nothing.
- **It halves when idle,** once for each sixteen landing times without a request, a span of at most the request interval. Sixteen is four times the window it doubles in, so one doubling never undoes itself, and a link spending a step in between keeps its pace. A pace back at the default, with no landing left to double by, is forgotten at the next check.
- **Bounded:** two doublings at most, and never past a quarter of the hub's ceiling, 25 packets, which binds at the default policies: 11, 22, then 25. A step with nothing held is then at most 11.75 packets, two and a half times the default's, and what is set aside toward one peer stays below 23.5.
- **The floor follows the step:** per peering over the hub, what one request has the node fund with nothing held at the credit it counts for that peer, at least the least settlement and, as before, at most half the ceiling in all. So a hub that funds a peer often keeps a step of it ready, which the deposit's landing time no longer stands between, and lets it go once the peer is idle.
- **In memory,** as the requests kept for late answers are: a restart counts the default credit again, and relearns the pace within three fundings.
- **Every bound stays:** the payee asks as before, the payer settles one settlement at a time per method and nothing below the least settlement, never less than the rule at the default credit asks, and the least default credit and the thresholds in packets are as they were ([thresholds-in-packets](thresholds-in-packets.md)). Pull settlement stays: the payee asks, and the payer decides how much from its own books and its own record of when it was asked.

## Accepted

- **Money set aside.** What a payer sets aside toward a busy peer, beyond what it already owes it from the hold, reaches nearly the credit counted less the trigger, up to 23.5 packets where it reached 9.5, and the floor up to 11.75 per peering where it was 4.75. That is the owner's own money: held toward one peer it earns nothing, funds no other peering and cannot be withdrawn until the peer spends it, since the node reduces no hold yet ([Settlement hubs](../areas/hubs.md#in-the-node)), and it is lost with the rest if the hub fails. The ceiling bounds all of it, as before: a quarter of it per peer leaves room for four such holds, and the floor's half for four busy peerings at their largest step.
- **What a peer can cost.** Over a hub the credit is the hold, so the bound a peer is held to, the credit we extend plus what it has in flight, grows with it ([own-fulfills](own-fulfills.md)). The peer takes from the hold only what our claims and commitments signed, for packets we forwarded and learn the fulfillment of; the rest stays ours, set aside. A peer that asks right after every landing can make us set aside a quarter of the ceiling toward it, against 9.5 packets before, and no more.
- **What a link survives** grows with the credit the payer counts: drift up to the credit counted plus the asker's hold, less two triggers and twice the least settlement ([crash-drift](crash-drift.md)). The least, at the default credit, is what it was, so the least default credit stands; the simulation judges a link by the credit its payer counts.
- **A modest gain while senders back off.** Two fundings pass before a step reaches its bound, and while the swap service backs off for seconds after a refusal, requests come too far apart to double often. Measured as above, three runs each way: at the production check, a quarter to two fifths of the requests found the credit doubled, at the dev timings a tenth to a quarter; the fundings averaged 7.4 packets against 6.1, the time to the first fifty packets fell from 48 to 53 s to 24, 24 and 56 s, refusals for want of room (T04) from 57 to 44 a run, and the time a payee's room sat at its trigger from 32 to 23 s a run; at the dev timings' one-second check, whose deposits land sooner anyway, the first fifty packets took 41 s either way. The largest hold toward one peer grew from 12.5 packets to 18, and the floor to 11.75 a peering. The pace pays off once senders retry as soon as room is back.
- **State on the payer:** one pace per method of a peering, its invariants checked with the rest.

## Rejected

- *A step sized to the measured request rate,* the rate a payee spends times a target time a step should last: the rate is an estimate from what was funded and when it was asked again, noisy from one sample and smoothed only with more state; one burst sets a large step, and it still needs a rule to shrink and a target to choose. Doubling reads the same signal with a count and two times, and its bound is a number of doublings.
- *Doubling only within one landing time,* as modeled: in the runs above, the payer was asked again within one landing time of a funding twice in 178 cycles. A payee then already waits for fundings half its time.
- *A larger default credit:* it sets more aside toward every peer, idle ones included, raises every peering's floor, and in the model moved less.
- *A higher trigger on a busy link:* it takes room the credit keeps for a crash's drift ([thresholds-in-packets](thresholds-in-packets.md)).
- *A payee naming the amount, or a payer funding as its hold drains:* the creditor does not know what the debtor's books say, and the debtor does not know the creditor's room ([own-fulfills](own-fulfills.md), [settle-at-the-trigger](settle-at-the-trigger.md)).
- *A pace per hub:* one busy peering would raise the steps of every peering over the hub, and the money set aside toward idle peers.
- *A bound of half the ceiling:* a peer asking right after every landing could have half the hub set aside toward it, and two busy peerings take the floor's whole bound.
- *Persisting the pace:* a restart relearns it within three fundings, and a stored one would size the first request after a restart for a burst that may be over.
