# A newcomer pays to enter: a payment on the ledger before it asks, held for it until it peers

**Areas:** [Network](../areas/network.md#downlinks), [XRPL](../areas/xrpl.md#following-the-account), [Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#the-scheme-interface), [Peer protocol](../areas/peer-protocol.md#versions), [Periphery](../designs/periphery.md#8-free-receivers).

## Facts

- Free receivers, downlinks that never paid us, hold at most half of an uplink's downlink places, and a downlink is free until the uplink's books take a settlement of at least one largest packet from it ([free-receivers-half](free-receivers-half.md)). A node can settle only once peered. So while the free half is full, which anyone with keys made for nothing can arrange, every node new to the uplink was refused, whether it would pay or not; only nodes that had paid this uplink before got in.
- An XRPL scheme follows every validated transaction that touches its account, proven at ledgers its own UNL validated ([verified-ledgers](verified-ledgers.md)). It takes a Payment for a peer's settlement by its memo, of the scheme's memo type naming the peer, and its sender, the address the peer gave when peering; any other Payment to it is the owner's deposit ([XRPL](../areas/xrpl.md#following-the-account)).
- A peering request on an XRPL scheme already carries, as the scheme's data, the address the requester settles from, and the request is authenticated by the requester's node key ([Peer protocol](../areas/peer-protocol.md#authentication)).
- The engine is sans-IO and answers a peering request from what it holds, in the step it arrives in; the answer goes out once the peering is on disk.
- Money that moved must be booked exactly once: as the payer's, or as the owner's, never both and never neither.
- A node reads a refusal reason it does not know as unspecified, and backs off from it as from any refusal (`RefusalReason::from_code`).

## Decision

- **A newcomer may pay first, on the ledger.** An entry payment is the Payment a settlement of the newcomer's would be: to the uplink's address on the scheme, which its peering info gives; from the address the newcomer's request will name; with one memo of the scheme's memo type naming the newcomer's node ID; of at least one largest packet, the least a node settles ([thresholds-in-packets](thresholds-in-packets.md)).
- **The proof travels in nothing new.** The uplink's scheme sees the payment arrive as it follows its account, only once its ledger is validated and the payment proven. The request names the address already, and the authenticated sender is the node. No field, message or scheme version is added: the uplink matches what it follows against what the request names.
- **Bound to its payer.** It counts only for the node its memo names, and only with a request whose data names the address it came from. Nobody can use another's payment: a request for another node is authenticated by that node's key, and a payment from another address names nothing the request can claim.
- **Held off the books, for a while.** Until its node peers, the scheme holds the payment off its books (its books hold the account less the reserve, less what it holds), in a table kept with its state: at most `MAX_ENTRIES` at once, each for `ENTRY_HOLD_LEDGERS` from when the scheme read it (`crates/dassie-xrpl/src/entry.rs`). A payment of less than one largest packet, one that finds the table full, and one whose hold is over without its node peering are the owner's deposit, as a payment from an address no peer gave is.
- **Booked once, as a settlement.** As the node peers from that address, the scheme books what it held from it as its settlement, under the transaction's hash, in the batch that keeps the peering, through the path every settlement takes, which marks the node's channel as paid for good. The scheme's cursor reads each transaction once, its table keeps each hash once and the journal books each settlement ID once, so a restart before or after books nothing twice.
- **Admission skips the free half alone.** A downlink whose request's scheme holds an entry payment from it is taken past a full free half, as a downlink that paid us before is. Every other bound still holds: bans and the wait after an owner's end, the downlinks' bound, the settlement channels and the peers' accounts. Nothing is ever ended or displaced. A payment held for a request refused for another bound stays held for a later one, until its hold is over.
- **The uplink says when paying would get the newcomer in.** Past a full free half, with room left among the downlinks, a newcomer that has not paid is refused with `PayToEnter` on a scheme that takes entry payments, and for capacity as before on one that takes none. A newcomer pays only when told so, so it never pays an uplink that is full, nor one that would not hold its payment: a node that does not take entry payments, older ones included, never says `PayToEnter`. An older requester reads the new reason as unspecified, and backs off as from capacity.
- **Which schemes take them.** The XRP schemes and the token schemes, RLUSD's and DSUSD's, which follow their account and attribute a settlement alike: the same table, by what a payment did to the account or to the line. The stub scheme has no ledger to pay on, and takes none. A settlement hub takes none: what a payer settles over a hub funds a hold toward the payee, which stays the payer's until the payee claims from it under the key the payer gave at peering ([Settlement hubs](../areas/hubs.md#the-members-side)), so before a peering there is neither a payment to us nor a key to bind it to. A newcomer on a hub alone waits for a free place, as every newcomer did before.

## Accepted

- **What it costs an attacker:** one largest packet per key, 0.1 XRP at the default, which is not lost. It is the key's balance with the uplink once peered, which the key may spend back out through the uplink, so the same money can mark key after key, each for the ledger's fee, the uplink's fees and the time a payment and a peering take. On the testnet, whose XRP is free, that time is all it costs; on mainnet it takes a largest packet of capital at a time. A fee would make the cost final, and is not part of this ([registration fees](../roadmap.md#registration-fees)). What paying buys is the half of the places that free receivers cannot take; the paying half was always open to a key that paid.
- **A payment its node does not claim is the uplink owner's.** A newcomer that does not peer within the hold, or that finds the last place taken between its refusal and its request, or the table full, loses its payment, one largest packet, to the uplink's owner. The uplink cannot tell a newcomer that changed its mind from an attacker that paid to fill the table, and a refund would be a payment the uplink signs and pays a fee for, at an attacker's choosing.
- **The table can be filled.** An attacker who fills it pays one largest packet for each place, and loses each to the uplink's owner once its hold is over: on mainnet, at the defaults, some 25 XRP an hour. Meanwhile newcomers' payments are the owner's deposits, and newcomers wait for a free place, as they did before this decision. On the testnet that costs nothing. A larger table or a longer hold costs more to fill and to write with the scheme's state; a shorter hold gives an honest newcomer less time after it paid. A newcomer asks right after it pays, so the hold covers a restart or two on either side.
- **A former peer's late payment is held first.** The memo is the settlement memo, so a payment of at least a largest packet from a former peer whose channel closed, which was the owner's deposit at once ([closing-channels](closing-channels.md)), is held for the hold first, and is its settlement if it peers again from that address meanwhile.
- **The amount is the uplink's largest packet.** The refusal does not say it. Every node takes it from the same default, so a newcomer pays what its uplink asks unless an owner changed it; a payment below the uplink's is not held, and is the owner's deposit.
- **Held money is in the account, not the books.** Until it is claimed or its hold is over, what the scheme holds is spendable by nothing the books drive. The account's holding shows it, the owner's funds do not. Fees, a higher reserve, an issuer's clawback or the owner spending outside the node still take from the account, and once the books hold nothing they take from what is held. So a payment is booked whole, as the node's settlement or the owner's deposit, and the books are then matched to what the account still holds, the shortfall through suspense as any unexplained difference is. The node is credited what it paid; the loss is the uplink's, which spent it. Booking only what is left would instead credit the node less than it paid, for costs that were the uplink's.

## Rejected

- *A probation pool,* places that take any newcomer for a while and end it if it does not pay: keys made for nothing would fill the probation places as they filled the free half, and a pool that ends peerings to make room is what [free-receivers-half](free-receivers-half.md) refuses, since an attacker's keys would push out honest nodes as fast as it made them.
- *A final fee to enter:* it makes the attacker's cost final, but makes peering cost money of its own, where a newcomer's payment here stays its own to spend. Fees for using the network belong with registration fees.
- *A field in `peeringRequest` naming the transaction, or a scheme data that adds its hash:* the uplink follows its account anyway, so a hash names nothing it cannot find. A field changes the request's layout, a new protocol version; the XRPL schemes' data is read strictly, its length first, so older uplinks would refuse the request, a new scheme version. And a hash invites a lookup.
- *Looking the transaction up by its hash as the request arrives:* the engine answers from what it holds, so the request would wait on the ledger, or be refused and asked again anyway, and a node could make the uplink look up hashes for nothing. Following the account costs the uplink no request of its own.
- *A memo type of its own for entry payments:* another contract between peers, per scheme. As a settlement's Payment, an entry payment is what the newcomer would sign anyway, and the uplink takes it by the rule it takes settlements by.
- *Holding it on the books,* as the owner's deposit moved to the peer later, or in an account of its own per newcomer: the first books money as the owner's that is not, and the second opens accounts for strangers, which are bounded and stay for good.
- *Opening the newcomer's accounts when its payment arrives,* and booking it as its settlement at once: anyone could open accounts with payments, and keep them open, with no peering.
