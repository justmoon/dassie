# Kopje's rocks are granite: gray blocks with hard edges, stacked, bigger than the dassies, and the brand page says why (not drawn yet)

**Areas:** [Website](../areas/website.md). Not drawn yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#kopje-across-dassie)): on `main`, the scenes' rocks are Terracotta, as [visual-identity](visual-identity.md) has them.

## Facts

- In Kopje the rocks are the ledgers, and they carry the identity's idea: solid rocks, nimble dassies ([visual-identity](visual-identity.md)). They are in the footer of every page of dassie.land, in the brand page's scenes, its kopje line and its big sun, and in the far horizon at the foot of the home page.
- People who see those rocks read them as droppings. A shape reads so when it has four things together: a dark, warm brown; a soft, rounded outline; one lump with nothing inside it to say what it is made of; and the size of the animal beside it. Every rock in the scenes has all four. They are filled in dark red-browns, one flat fill each; their corners are curves; each is one mound; and a dassie lies on top of a rock hardly longer than itself, which is the outline of the emoji.
- The mark had the same trouble, and its rock became ink ([visual-identity](visual-identity.md#accepted)). That changed one drawing and left the rule: the palette still gives Terracotta to the kopjes in every scene, so each new drawing brings the brown back. The brand page already asks the mark's rock for "flat faces and tight corners: rock, not dough", and nothing holds the scenes to it.
- Real kopjes are granite. The rock splits along joints into blocks and slabs, which weather at their edges and lie stacked on each other, gray to pinkish gray; a block is many times the length of a dassie. Seen from far away, through the haze, rock turns paler and cooler.

## Decision

**A rock in Kopje is granite: gray, faceted, stacked, and large beside a dassie. The brand page states these rules and why, and every rock is drawn to them, by hand but for the node circles'.**

- **Gray, not brown.** A rock's faces are grays: their chroma stays below a bound that the brand page sets, so they read as stone whatever their hue leans to. The face turned away from the sun is a step darker than the face toward the viewer, and far kopjes are paler and cooler than near ones.
- **Terracotta is earth, never a rock.** It keeps its place for grounds, bands and cards, such as the balance card and the veld grass, and for nothing that stands on the horizon as a closed shape.
- **Flat faces, hard corners, joints.** A rock's outline is straight lines whose corners are cut short, never rounded; tops are flat or tilted slabs, never domes. Each rock shows its joints: a crack across a face, or the line of shade where one block rests on another.
- **Lit from behind, as a low sun lights rock.** A rim of Sunrise runs along every edge that has sky behind it, the sides as well as the top, down to the ground, so the backlit look and the line of light between rock and dassie stay. An edge with another rock behind it has no rim. Where a block rests on a wider one, the lower block's top beside it lies in its shade: the rim there comes in a little way from the outer corner and fades out before the block above, and the joint between them is a line of shade.
- **Stacked.** A kopje is two or more blocks: a broad base and smaller blocks on it. Only a far kopje, too small for its parts to show, is a single slab, and then a low and wide one.
- **Bigger than the dassies.** A block on which a dassie sits or lies is at least three times the dassie's length, and a dassie lying down lies on a ledge or toward one end, never on the crown of a small rock.
- **A phone gets a composition of its own.** A scene drawn across a wide screen has a narrow one for a phone: fewer kopjes, the main one under a sun that fills the width, and fewer dassies, drawn at a size where they still read. The wide scene shrunk to a phone's width is never shown.
- **Rules on the brand page, drawn by hand.** The brand page states these rules where it shows the rocks, in its palette and its graphic language, with the four things that make a shape read as droppings, so whoever draws a rock knows what to stay clear of. Every drawing with rocks is drawn again to them; the mark keeps its own rules. The node circles, whose rock is drawn from each node's ID, follow the same rules in their code once they are built.

## Accepted

- The scenes are less warm: gray rocks under an apricot sun are cooler than brown ones. The warmth comes from the sun, the sandstone ground and the Terracotta earth instead.
- Every drawing with rocks is drawn again: the footer, both far horizons, the brand page's scenes, kopje line and big sun; and a scene shown on phones is drawn twice, wide and narrow.
- Nothing but review holds a new drawing to the rules. What made the brown come back was the palette itself, which named Terracotta for the kopjes; once it names granite, and the brand page says what to avoid and why, a drawing that follows the page is a gray, faceted one.

## Rejected

- *Keep Terracotta, and reshape the rocks:* faceted, stacked brown rocks read better, but brown is what the mark's trouble came down to, and every softer drawing later on would bring the old reading back.
- *Make the rocks gray, and keep their shapes:* smooth gray mounds read as dough, pebbles or potatoes rather than as kopjes; the form has to say rock as much as the color.
- *A lighter Terracotta, the clay orange:* a lighter brown is still brown, a mound of it reads as clay, and orange rock against the apricot sun loses the edge that makes the scene.
- *A rim along the top edges only, the same on every block:* it is the rim the Terracotta rocks have, and it lights the joints between stacked blocks as brightly as the outline, where a low sun behind the kopje leaves them in shade, and leaves the sides of the outline dark, where the sun would catch them. The light then says nothing of the stack's shape.
- *Shrink the wide scene for a phone:* at a third of its size the dassies are a few pixels high and the kopjes pebbles, which is the misreading this decision is about.
- *Ink rocks, as the mark's:* the mark has one dassie and one rock, parted by a rim of light. A scene's many ink dassies on ink rocks melt into one dark band, and the scene no longer shows ledgers apart from the nodes on them.
- *Red sandstone mesas, flat-topped and layered:* strata read clearly as rock, but they are red, and a mesa is tall where Kopje lies low; kopjes are granite.
- *One seeded generator in `packages/ui` draws every rock, and a test holds it to the rules:* it would keep a later drawing from bringing the brown or the mound back, but it is code to keep, and it binds every scene to what the generator can draw. The drawings with rocks are few, and the rules on the page are enough to draw them by.
