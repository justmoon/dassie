# A peering settles by every scheme both nodes run, and a packet keeps its ledger where it can

**Areas:** [Network](../areas/network.md#peering), [Settlement](../areas/settlement.md), [Connector](../areas/connector.md#routing), [Peer protocol](../areas/peer-protocol.md). Not built yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#settlement-methods)).

## Facts

- A peering is on one scheme. The node that asks takes the first scheme in the other's link state that it also runs, or the hub both are in; the link state lists schemes in the order of their IDs, so the pick is alphabetical.
- Nothing moves a peering to another scheme. A scheme both come to run, or a hub both join, leaves it where it is; one that can no longer receive keeps it withdrawn, never ended ([Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#when-peers-cannot-pay-us)); and the owner cannot remove a scheme while peerings use it. Moving takes ending the peering and peering again.
- A peer's accounts, credit and position are per ledger. A pair that peers again on another scheme leaves what is owed on the old ledger to no peering, though [ending-a-peering](ending-a-peering.md) carries it into a later one; starts its credit afresh; and an end for room keeps the lower credit only on the old ledger ([ended-for-room](ended-for-room.md)).
- A packet converts at a node whenever it leaves on another ledger than it came on. A conversion costs the spread, the inventory curve's move, drift that market makers are paid up to `k` to undo, the oracle's error, and refusals while a rate is stale or a dollar token's peg guard holds ([passive-rebalancing](passive-rebalancing.md), [issued-assets](issued-assets.md)). With one scheme per pair, a payment in the asset the pair did not pick converts on its way into the core and again on its way out, though both its ends hold that asset.
- No notion of currency serves routing across the network. Moving a dollar token from one ledger to another can be nearly free or cost as much as converting currencies; some conversions cost a hundred times others; and some hops are outside Dassie, before a payment enters or after it leaves. Cross-currency payments are expected to convert twice, into a widely used settlement currency and out of it, since markets for every pair of hundreds of currencies cost too much.
- A STREAM sender slows down on T04 ([Stream](../areas/stream.md#congestion-control)). A worse rate it sees only as less arriving, and the receiver refuses a packet below the sender's minimum.

## Decision

- **A peering has settlement methods:** every scheme both nodes peer on, each with its own accounts, credit, position, thresholds and requests to settle, as a peer's ledger has them now. The methods are agreed when the peering is made, and change while it lasts: a scheme both come to run is added, and a method is dropped when its scheme is removed, no longer run, or unable to receive for as long as a silent peer is kept. The peering, its settlement channel and the mark of an end for room stay one for the pair, and ending the peering ends every method; a peering left with none ends.
- **A method is dropped as a peering ends, on that method alone** ([ending-a-peering](ending-a-peering.md)). The node that drops it collects first: it takes no new packets on it, waits for its own settlements on it under way, asks the peer to settle on it once and waits for that, each for a bounded time; then it tells the peer, which drops it at once and settles nothing more on it. Neither side pays the rest: what is left stays on the method's accounts, for a later method on that scheme to settle. A method on a scheme that cannot receive, or that the node no longer runs, is dropped at once, without collecting, since no settlement on it can arrive. The owner's removal of a scheme drops its methods and is no longer refused for them: the scheme runs until each is dropped, which takes a bounded time, then goes.
- **Within peer protocol v1.** Methods are new messages within v1, as `hubPacket` is, and a node that has them says so with a link-state extension of a new type, so neither needs a new protocol version ([Peer protocol](../areas/peer-protocol.md#versions)). Between two nodes that both have them, the methods are agreed and changed by those messages, and each Prepare names its method. With a node that does not, a peering has one method, the scheme it was made on, and Prepares travel as now. A stored peering becomes a peering with that one method, without peering again; more are added once both nodes have methods.
- **The next hop keeps the packet's ledger where it can.** Among a destination's first hops, the connector draws one with a method on the ledger the packet came in on ([weighted-next-hops](weighted-next-hops.md)), and sends it on that method. Only when none has one does the packet convert: the hop is drawn among all first hops, and the packet goes on its method with the best rate in the node's price table, which pays out what the node holds above its target. It never takes a longer path to avoid a conversion. Packets from the owner's own endpoints never convert ([owner-ledger](owner-ledger.md)): their first hop's method is on the ledger the owner pays from, as now. What counts as a change is local: any ledger other than the incoming one, whatever its currency.
- **A method is chosen by structure, never by room.** Which method a packet takes depends on its incoming ledger and the methods available (active, synced, able to receive, not wound down, not withdrawn), never on the room each has. A packet whose method is short of room gets T04, as now, so a STREAM sender slows down.
- **Withdrawn per method, ended per peering.** A method short of room past the grace without settling is withdrawn, with a hold-down, as an edge is now ([Network](../areas/network.md#advertised-peers)), and its packets take another first hop or another method from then on. The peer leaves the link state only once every method is withdrawn or unavailable. A method short for as long as ends a peering for room ends the peering.
- **Credit per method.** The junior extends the default line on each method, and a peer earns credit on the method its fees were booked on. An end for room keeps the lower credit on every method, a method added after it included.
- **A hub stays as it is:** a pair in the same hub settles over it alone ([the design](../designs/settlement-hubs.md#11-choosing-a-hub)). Until that changes, a hub is never added as a method to a peering with direct methods, nor a direct method to a peering over a hub; how a directly peered pair comes to its hub stays as [Settlement hubs](../roadmap.md#settlement-hubs) has it. Whether a hub becomes one method beside its underlying ledger's, and which of the two a packet takes, is open ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#settlement-methods)).

## Accepted

- **No netting across methods.** A pair that pays one way on one ledger and the other way on another settles both: more settlements and ledger fees on mixed links.
- **More exposure.** What a peer can cost is the sum over its methods, bounded by the schemes a node peers on (`MAX_SCHEMES`).
- **New messages and a link-state extension within v1,** for methods agreed, added and dropped, and named on each Prepare; a peering with a node that has no methods keeps one.
- **What is left when a method is dropped stays owed,** as at a peering's end: at most the credit extended on that method, until a later method on its scheme settles it.
- **Greedy.** Keeping the ledger at one hop can force a conversion later that another path would have avoided: a node does not know the ledgers past its peers.
- **A method withdrawn for room changes the rate** of the packets it carried, once, as a route change does now. A STREAM sender whose minimum the new rate misses sees F99.
- **Market makers keep two nodes.** The connector never sends a packet back to the peer it came from, and the owner's own endpoints never convert ([owner-ledger](owner-ledger.md)).

## Rejected

- *One scheme per pair, picked by a rank the registry ships (a shared hub, then the network's native asset, then tokens):* a core uniform in one asset makes every payment in another convert twice, though both its ends share that asset.
- *A global currency for routing, with paths through a settlement currency computed by the network:* no notion of currency fits every ledger's costs, and hops outside Dassie are not seen.
- *At most one conversion per payment:* it needs a market for every pair of currencies.
- *Several peerings per pair, one per scheme:* every rule kept per pair (advertising, termination, slot caps, the end for room) would have to say which peering it means.
- *Another method when one is short of room:* packets still get through, at a worse rate, so a STREAM sender sees no T04 and does not slow down; the detour converts on its way out of the ledger and again on its way back, leaving drift at two nodes for a shortage the next settlement ends; and the rate changes with every settlement.
- *Moving a pair to a new scheme by ending and peering again:* it strands what is owed on the old ledger, starts credit afresh, and leaves an end for room on the old ledger only.
- *One credit budget across methods:* it would need exchange rates in the credit rules, which each node otherwise keeps in each ledger's own units.
- *Settling one method's debt in another's asset, to rebalance:* a conversion at settlement, at par or at a rate both sides would have to agree on; the curve already pays whoever brings back what a node lacks.
