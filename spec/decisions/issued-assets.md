# An issued asset's issuer is the owner's counterparty, and a dollar token pauses off its peg

**Areas:** [XRPL](../areas/xrpl.md#rlusd), [Settlement](../areas/settlement.md#when-peers-cannot-pay-us), [Connector](../areas/connector.md#inventory-pricing). Built in the token scheme, with test RLUSD disabled, and the peg guard, which reads its pool proven at the account's anchor ([XRPL](../areas/xrpl.md#the-peg-guard)) and is not yet shown in the wallet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#mainnet-schemes)), and that halts its hub's ledger, which no scheme books yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#token-hubs)); transfer fees ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#not-built-yet)) are not. [open-rate-sources](open-rate-sources.md) turns the guard off once XRP's rate comes from mainnet's XRP/RLUSD pool, until a dollar token has a price of its own (not built).

## Facts

- A token on the XRP Ledger, such as RLUSD, is backed by its issuer. RLUSD's issuer can freeze a holder's line, which stops the holder paying anyone but the issuer, and deeper, receiving too; and it can claw back a holder's balance, with a transaction that is no Payment.
- What a peer can cost a node is bounded by the credit it extends plus what is in flight ([own-books](own-books.md)). Nothing bounds what an issuer can take: the whole holding.
- Rates are the median of three sources ([exchange-rates](exchange-rates.md)); the sources a node has quote fiat currencies and XRP. A dollar token priced as `USD` needs no new source, and converts one to one with every other dollar ledger.
- **At par, a de-peg costs at the boundaries.** On the token's own ledger, the debts on both sides of a transit are in the token, so they match whatever it is worth. A conversion is where par is wrong: someone holding a token worth less than a dollar pays it in and takes XRP or another dollar out at par, and the node is drained; off the peg upward, the drain runs the other way. Settling for debt that arose in other assets is a conversion too.
- **The XRP Ledger prices the token.** An AMM pool of XRP and the token holds both, and their ratio times the XRP/USD rate the node already has is the token's price in dollars. Both balances live in two objects, the pool account's root (its XRP) and its line to the issuer (the token), which one state proof proves together, as of one validated ledger ([verified-ledgers](verified-ledgers.md)). On mainnet the RLUSD/XRP pool is deep and trades every few minutes, and its price has stayed within about a percent of a dollar; dollar tokens that lost their peg moved several percent to tens of percent, for hours to days. On the testnet, DSUSD's pool is held at the market by its keeper, with short excursions it corrects within minutes ([dsusd](dsusd.md)); test RLUSD's pool prices it nowhere near a dollar, and nothing on the testnet gives it one.
- The issuer's account, where a global freeze and a transfer fee live, is an object a state proof proves like any other ([verified-ledgers](verified-ledgers.md)).

## Decision

- **The issuer is a counterparty the owner chooses to trust,** by running a scheme of its token. What it takes is the owner's loss: a clawback is a withdrawal, booked once a proven transaction on the line shows it, which a server can delay ([verified-ledgers](verified-ledgers.md)), like any other ([Ledger](../areas/ledger.md#what-the-ledger-refuses-and-what-it-records)). A frozen line makes the scheme settle nothing until it thaws; peerings stay. This holds on mainnet as on the testnet.
- **Peers' bounds do not change.** A peer still costs at most the credit extended plus what is in flight; an issuer's act against a peer is that peer's loss, and shows up for us only as a settlement that failed.
- **A dollar token is booked as `USD`, at par,** on a ledger of its own. It converts one to one with other dollar ledgers and needs no rate.
- **The scheme holds the line safe from others:** rippling is disabled on our side, and only the issuer's token on the scheme's own line counts.
- **The issuer's freezes and fee are proven,** as any state is: our line's freeze and deep freeze from the line, the issuer's global freeze and transfer fee from its account, in the state proof the account takes at each anchor.
- **A peg guard pauses a dollar token's scheme while its pool prices it off a dollar.** The price is the pool's ratio, from one state proof of both its balances at one validated ledger, times the node's own XRP/USD rate. The scheme pauses once every reading has been outside a band around a dollar for a while, and resumes once every reading has been back inside a narrower band for longer, so neither the pool's noise nor a pool pushed for a moment trips it. Off the peg upward or downward is the same pause.
- **What a pause stops:** the connector converts nothing to or from its ledger, as for a ledger whose rate is stale ([Connector](../areas/connector.md#inventory-pricing)), so the rate feed names it no more. What arrives on the ledger is still booked, at par: refusing to book what happened would only make the books wrong. The scheme says it cannot receive meanwhile.
- **The pause covers the token's hub too.** Peers settle only over hubs, and the token's scheme is only the owner's book ([hubs-only](hubs-only.md)), so a node converts the token on the ledger of the token's hub ([token-hubs](token-hubs.md)). While the guard pauses the book, the connector converts nothing to or from the hub's ledger either, as for the book's own. Settling over the hub goes on: the hub moves the token as it is, and transit within the hub's ledger runs as within the book's. A hub whose book has a guard converts nothing while that book is not running, since no reading can tell the token is on its peg.
- **It fails closed.** A reading counts only when both of the pool's balances are proven at one ledger, that ledger is an anchor that closed no longer ago than a validation stays fresh, by the node's clock, the pool holds enough XRP to be a price, and the rate is fresh. Balances proven at two ledgers could pair an old XRP balance with a newer token balance, a ratio the pool never had. Without a valid reading for a while, the scheme pauses as if off its peg.
- **Which pool.** Mainnet RLUSD reads mainnet's RLUSD/XRP pool. On the testnet, DSUSD reads its DSUSD/XRP pool, so the guard runs before real money does. The pool is found by its asset pair: the proven `AMM` object under the pair's key names the pool's account, which the pair alone does not give, so no server can substitute another, and a pool made again is found without a release. A first proof of the `AMM` object gives the account; each reading then proves the `AMM` object with that account's two balances at one ledger, so it shows the pair still names that account ([the design](../designs/xrpl-validation.md#4-what-is-proven-and-how)).
- **Test RLUSD is disabled,** and DSUSD is the testnet's stand-in for RLUSD ([mainnet-schemes](mainnet-schemes.md)). Test RLUSD has no dollar price on the testnet, so a guard would keep it paused for good, and unguarded it would be the one dollar token a node books at par with nothing to tell it otherwise. DSUSD's pool is held at the market, so on the testnet DSUSD exercises what mainnet RLUSD runs: the token scheme and its guard.
- **The guard is the token scheme's,** without IO, beside the issuer's freezes, whose effects it shares. Its state is kept with the scheme's, so a restart neither skips a recovery nor trips again. The owner sees it, with the last price and the ledger it is as of.

## Accepted

- **A de-peg inside the band goes unseen,** and one outside it costs what converts at par before the guard trips.
- **A scheme new to the guard runs from its start, as if read in the band then.** A de-peg already under way when the scheme starts costs what converts before the guard trips, as one that starts a moment later would; without a reading for as long, it pauses as any guard does. A restart keeps the guard's state, so only a scheme added anew, or one kept from before the guard, starts so.
- **A pause halts the token's routing** once the rooms of its peerings are used, and a node can take no conversion into or out of it until it recovers.
- **A lying server can show the guard a reading** as old as a validation stays fresh, by its ledger's close time against the node's clock, and older by as much as that clock runs behind. Past that, its readings count for nothing, so it can hold the guard on the wrong side for that long and the staleness bound after, as for a guard unread, or pause it by refusing to answer ([verified-ledgers](verified-ledgers.md)).
- **A problem reading the pool pauses the scheme:** a busy server, a pool too thin, a stale rate.
- **Owners who hold test RLUSD can no longer book it in a scheme.** It stays on their lines; it was never worth anything.
- **An issuer can take a node's holding at any time,** and the node can only record it. Spending is then refused until the owner's funds recover, as after any loss.

## Rejected

- *The token as a currency of its own, with its own rate,* or priced at its pool rate: three independent sources for it, a currency the owner sees beside the dollar, and no conversion one to one with other dollar ledgers, for an asset whose point is to be a dollar.
- *Credit to the issuer's account, booked as exposure:* the issuer is not a peer; it settles nothing and is asked nothing, so a credit line to it would bound nothing.
- *Exchange tickers for the token:* off the ledger, few venues list it, and each would be a source the node trusts alone. The pool is on the ledger the token lives on, and the node can verify it.
- *The issuer's attestations or reserve reports:* the counterparty grading itself.
- *A price oracle object on the ledger:* one publisher's word.
- *Pausing only outgoing settlements:* a token almost always de-pegs downward, and then the loser is the side that takes it at par; a paused node would keep collecting while paying nothing, and honest peers would end it for room. It also leaves conversions at par, the real loss, open.
- *Keeping test RLUSD, unguarded:* the testnet would run a dollar scheme that mainnet never runs, at par for a token its own pool prices far from a dollar, so a market maker could drain nodes of what they convert into it.
- *Guarding test RLUSD with DSUSD's pool price as a stand-in:* it would pause RLUSD for DSUSD's keeper lagging, not for anything RLUSD did, and guard it with a price that says nothing about it.
- *Failing open:* the guard would be blind exactly when de-pegs happen, with runs on the issuer, drained pools and busy servers.
- *Pausing only the book's own ledger:* with peers settling only over hubs, the conversions a de-peg drains a node through happen on the hub's ledger, so the guard would guard nothing.
- *Pausing settlement over the hub with the book:* the drain is in converting at par, not in moving the token; a hub that settles nothing would only cut members off from each other in the token while the de-peg lasts.
