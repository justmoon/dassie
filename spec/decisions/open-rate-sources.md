# A node takes its rates only from sources whose terms allow its use: XRP's from mainnet's XRP/RLUSD pool, the fiat currencies' from central banks, with no peg guard until a dollar token has a price of its own

**Areas:** [Connector](../areas/connector.md#exchange-rates), [XRPL](../areas/xrpl.md#the-peg-guard), [DSUSD keeper](../areas/dsusd-keeper.md#the-price), [Wallet](../areas/wallet.md). Not built yet ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#open-rate-sources)).

## Facts

- **One rate moves money: XRP's.** Every ledger a node runs is in XRP or in a dollar token booked as `USD` at par ([issued-assets](issued-assets.md), [mainnet-schemes](mainnet-schemes.md)). The other currencies a node keeps rates for only show the owner totals ([Connector](../areas/connector.md#exchange-rates)).
- **A node converts for a fee at its rates,** run by an owner who is not the project. Each owner is the user of whatever the node fetches, and the use is commercial.
- **Exchanges' market data is licensed for personal or internal use only.** Coinbase's terms forbid passing the data or anything derived from it to a third party, and deriving fair-value prices or valuations for settlement from it; Bitstamp's forbid collecting or distributing its prices; Kraken's let its content be used only for the user's own benefit and sell market data separately. The other large exchanges' terms read the same, and some say outright that they cover their public endpoints too.
- **Paid feeds sell redistribution only by contract.** A subscription to a feed such as Pyth Pro licenses the subscriber's own use and display, but no machine-readable feed to third parties, no use by anyone but its employees, and nothing that competes with the feed. Rights to pass prices on to other parties' nodes are negotiated, at thousands a month.
- **The XRP Ledger's state carries no terms,** and a validated ledger is signed by a quorum of the UNL. The node already proves a pool's balances at a validated ledger, against its network's proof server, for the peg guard ([verified-ledgers](verified-ledgers.md), [XRPL](../areas/xrpl.md#the-peg-guard)).
- **Mainnet's XRP/RLUSD pool is the deepest market of XRP against a dollar on the ledger.** It trades about its whole depth a day, and its price follows the exchanges' closely. The XRP/USDC pool is an order of magnitude shallower. A pool can be pushed off the market for little, and arbitrage brings it back within a few ledgers. The testnet's pools hold test money, at no market price except DSUSD's, which its keeper holds at the market ([dsusd](dsusd.md)).
- **The ledger's price oracles are thin.** `Oracle` entries hold prices their owner writes, provable like any entry. On mainnet the only live XRP/USD entry whose terms allow a node's use is Band's: one key writes it, every ten minutes. DIA's terms ask a fee of anyone who reads its prices, and the others found are long unwritten. Off the ledger, oracles' terms are silent or unclear, or restrict use, and their prices come from the same exchanges.
- **The peg guard needs XRP's price in dollars from outside the token.** It prices a dollar token as its pool's ratio times XRP's rate ([issued-assets](issued-assets.md)). With XRP's rate taken from that same pool, the product is a dollar whatever the token is worth.
- **Central banks publish daily reference rates.** The ECB's, Norges Bank's, the Bank of Canada's and the Czech National Bank's terms allow commercial use and reuse, if the bank is named as the source; each fixes its own rates from the market, at its own time of day, and together they cover every fiat currency a node keeps. The Bank of England, the Swiss National Bank and the Bank of Japan forbid commercial use of their exchange rates, or exclude them from their licence.

## Decision

- **A new node's sources are only ones whose terms allow a node to fetch them and convert by them, for a fee.** An owner may still list any source, and its terms are then the owner's to keep.
- **XRP's rate is mainnet's XRP/RLUSD pool's.** The node reads the pool proven at a validated ledger, as the peg guard reads its pool now, whatever the node's realm. Its price counts only while the pool holds at least a floor of XRP, and is the median of its readings over a short window, so a pool pushed for a ledger or two moves nothing. The pool is read more often than the minute the other sources are asked, and it is XRP's only source.
- **The pool is read, never traded or signed for.** The node follows mainnet's validated ledgers for its price with no key, so a test node signs nothing on mainnet and runs no mainnet scheme: a ledger still runs only in its node's realm ([mainnet-schemes](mainnet-schemes.md)).
- **No peg guard until a dollar token has a price of its own.** Once XRP's rate comes from the RLUSD pool, the guard could only ever read RLUSD as pegged, so it is turned off, on mainnet RLUSD and on testnet DSUSD alike, and a dollar token converts at par as any dollar ledger does. It runs again once a source of XRP's price in dollars is found that follows the market within seconds to minutes, lies outside every dollar token's pool, and has terms that allow a node's use. It is not a condition for real money.
- **The fiat currencies' rates come from four central banks:** the ECB, Norges Bank, the Bank of Canada and the Czech National Bank, each crossed to the dollar through its own base currency. Each is taken only while its newest day is no older than its calendar allows, as the ECB's is.
- **A currency whose only sources are daily is never a ledger's asset.** Daily rates are good enough for totals and nothing else. A ledger in another currency brings sources that follow its market within minutes.
- **The wallet names the sources,** as the central banks' terms ask, where it shows a rate or a total.
- **DSUSD's keeper takes its target from mainnet's XRP/RLUSD pool,** in place of an exchange's ticker, whose terms forbid what the keeper does: it publishes the price on the testnet's pool. It reads the pool from a mainnet server without a proof, as it reads the ticker now, since nothing of value rides on the testnet's price; its refusal of any server off the testnet stays for the server it signs on.

## Accepted

- **One source for XRP.** [exchange-rates](exchange-rates.md) rejects one source as a single point of failure. Any second or third source would be weaker than the pool: the USDC pool is cheaper to push, and Band's entry is up to ten minutes old, from one key. A median moves with whichever of them is wrong, and with only two they must agree, so either would add a way to stop every node's conversions without adding a better price.
- **A pushed pool moves XRP's rate.** The pool held off the market for more than half the window, against every arbitrageur, moves XRP's rate by as much, on every node, for as long. Holding it off costs what arbitrage takes from it every ledger; what it gains is bounded by what nodes convert meanwhile, which the inventory curve prices against whoever drains a node ([passive-rebalancing](passive-rebalancing.md)).
- **One proof server carries XRP's rate.** While mainnet's proof server is down, the pool is silent, and after the connector's staleness bound no node converts between XRP and the dollar, test nodes too. The exchanges' tickers needed no proof server, and a test node needed only the testnet's. A node already stalls its settlement while its own network's server is down; more servers, which the patch upstream lets anyone run ([roadmap](../roadmap.md#proof-servers)), shorten what both cost.
- **XRP's rate is in RLUSD, not in dollars, and a de-peg goes unseen.** A node books RLUSD at par, so its conversions between XRP and RLUSD stay right while RLUSD is off its peg. What it loses is what converts at par between RLUSD and other dollar ledgers, as [issued-assets](issued-assets.md) describes, and nothing pauses that, with real money too. RLUSD is the only dollar token a mainnet node runs, so there is no other dollar token to drain it into. On the testnet, DSUSD off the market costs test money only, and its keeper brings it back.
- **A test node follows mainnet's ledgers too:** mainnet's validator lists, its validations and its proof server, for one price alone. The project's mainnet proof server serves every test node's readings.
- **The order books are not read.** Proving a book's best offers needs proofs of every better quality's absence, which no one has built; the pool is proven as it is.
- **Fiat totals lag the market** by up to a day, and by several over a holiday. With four daily sources fixed at different hours, the middle two may disagree on a day the market moved, and a total is then shown no more until they agree again.
- **Attribution.** The wallet carries a line naming the central banks.

## Rejected

- *Exchanges' tickers, asked by each node,* as now: their terms license personal or internal use, and a node converts for its owner's fees.
- *A relay of the project's own, of exchanges' data or of a paid feed's:* both forbid passing their data on to third parties without a contract, and a relay is one point every node would trust for its rates. A validated ledger is signed already, and needs no relay.
- *A paid feed with redistribution rights* (Pyth Pro or a data vendor): thousands a month and a contract, and keys the project would have to hand out, against a node's zero configuration. It becomes worth asking for once a ledger moves money in a currency without an open market, or as the guard's source.
- *The XRP/USDC pool beside the RLUSD pool:* the cheapest of the sources to push, so it would give a pusher the median, or with two sources a way to make them disagree.
- *Band's `Oracle` entry as a source, or as the guard's:* written every ten minutes by one key, so it lags every move XRP makes within that, and a guard that compared it with the pool would trip on those moves or wait them out. BandChain's own prices are fresher, but come over another chain's client, and still lag by minutes.
- *Other oracles:* DIA's ask a fee of anyone who reads them, Chainlink's would bring another chain's client into the node for terms that may not allow it, and Pyth's need a paid key.
- *A guard on the same pool's rate:* it would read every token as pegged, and a guard that shows pegged while blind is worse than none, which at least does not pretend.
- *Keeping the guard on DSUSD alone:* the testnet would run a guard mainnet does not, against [mainnet-schemes](mainnet-schemes.md)' point of DSUSD standing in for RLUSD.
- *Stablecoin pools for the other fiat currencies:* only euro coins trade deeply enough, and each coin carries its own bias from its currency. Totals need no better than the central banks' rates.
- *The testnet's pools for XRP's rate:* they hold test money, and DSUSD's tracks the market only because its keeper reads it elsewhere.
